The Blank Square

Original fiction · Written for this activity

[¶1] At the neighborhood history table, Nora arranged photographs into straight rows. She had promised the librarian a map showing when each building on Alder Street had opened. By noon, almost every square on the map contained a date in her careful lettering. Only the little grocery on the corner remained blank.

[¶2] Mr. Salim studied a photograph of the store. “My mother brought me there the winter the pipes froze,” he said. Nora glanced at the empty square. “Was that 1978?” He considered. “Maybe. I remember carrying the bread home under my coat. The year is less clear.” Nora’s pencil hovered over the paper.

[¶3] In the next folder she found an advertisement dated 1981. Across its top, large letters announced, “Celebrating Our Fifth Year!” She counted backward and wrote 1976 on a sticky note. Then she noticed the small print: “Under the Rahman family’s ownership.” The advertisement said nothing about when the store itself had opened.

[¶4] Nora slid the sticky note under the folder. The map looked unfinished without that last date. She pictured visitors stopping at the blank square instead of noticing all the careful work around it. Mr. Salim leaned closer to the photograph. “The awning was striped then,” he said. “I had forgotten that.”

[¶5] “Could we record what you remember?” Nora asked. She set a small card beside the photograph and wrote, “Mr. Salim recalls visiting the store as a child during a winter freeze; opening year not yet confirmed.” On a second card she noted the change of ownership. Neither card filled the square, but both could be checked or added to later.

[¶6] When the librarian came by, Nora started to explain the gap. Before she could finish, a visitor bent over Mr. Salim’s card. “My aunt has a picture of that awning,” the woman said. Nora reached for a clean envelope. This time, she did not reach for her eraser.

F1. What most strongly motivates Nora’s desire to fill the last square at first?

F2. Why does the small print in paragraph 3 matter?

The Moving Line

Original fiction · Written for this activity

[¶1] Jonah had planned the community garden’s new bed all winter. On graph paper, six tomato plants stood in a perfect row, with a narrow path behind them. At Saturday’s planting session, he placed the paper on an overturned bucket and began marking the soil with string.

[¶2] “That corner stays in shade until late morning,” said Lila, who had joined the garden only a month earlier. Jonah looked at the bright patch beside his shoes. “It’s sunny now.” She nodded toward the apartment wall. “Now it is. I walk past here before school.” He pulled the string tighter. The row on his paper had no room for the wall’s shadow.

[¶3] Ms. Chen, the garden coordinator, handed them a tray of seedlings. “We can plant after lunch,” she said. “First, please check which beds need water.” Jonah started toward the tap. Lila stayed beside the new bed and pressed a short stick into the edge of the shadow. By the time he returned, she had placed another stick several inches away.

[¶4] Jonah set down the watering can. “How often are you marking it?” he asked. “Every twenty minutes.” He checked his watch, then pushed a third stick into the soil when the next interval passed. Neither of them mentioned the drawing. Together they watched the line of sticks move toward the wall.

[¶5] After lunch, the shaded corner was bright, but the marks showed how long it had taken the sunlight to reach it. Jonah carried his plan to the bucket and turned it sideways. A row along the other edge would leave the tomatoes in sun longer. It would also make the path less straight. He rubbed out the old line and asked Lila to hold the string at the new corner.

[¶6] As they lowered the first seedling into the soil, Lila asked whether he wanted to measure the spacing again. “Yes,” Jonah said, handing her the ruler. His drawing was smudged, and one corner had become damp. For the first time that morning, he left it on the bucket while they worked.

F7. Why does Jonah initially resist Lila’s observation?

F8. What does the sentence “The row on his paper had no room for the wall’s shadow” suggest?
