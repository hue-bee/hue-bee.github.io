Walkthrough · Breaks and help · 0:25–0:28

Ask Early; Don’t Troubleshoot Alone

Breaks and bathroom needs

Ask the proctor before leaving your seat. Do not assume you may pause the timer yourself or that an ordinary bathroom visit stops testing time.

All students stay for the standard 180-minute administration, except bathroom use. Approved extended-time students have 360 minutes of testing, with two 15-minute breaks after 180 and 270 minutes.

Illness or a frozen screen

Raise your hand immediately. Say what happened and follow the proctor’s instructions. Do not refresh, leave the testing app, or repeatedly click through a frozen screen on your own.

Report illness before beginning if possible. During breaks, no phone access and no discussion of test content.

Say the first sentence
“My screen is not responding.” “I need the bathroom.” “I am feeling ill.” What should you avoid assuming about the clock?

Fiction: Follow the Small Decisions

The Opening Line

Original fiction · Read for motivation, a turning point, and what a detail contributes.

[1]Mara arrived at the community theater carrying a roll of silver tape. Tonight, she was supposed to work the curtain while her older brother, Eli, read the opening lines. She had practiced the rope until she could raise the curtain without a sound. She liked being useful where no one could see her.

[2]At the door, Eli held out a folded program. “My voice is gone,” he whispered. Mara laughed until he tried to laugh too and produced only air. The director was helping younger children pin their costumes. Beyond the curtain, chairs scraped across the floor.

[3]“You know the lines,” Eli said. Mara pressed the tape into a flat oval. Knowing words alone in a kitchen was one thing. Sending them across a room full of strangers was another. “I can fix the loose curtain ring,” she said.

[4]Eli glanced at the ring and then at her. “It’s been loose all week.” He did not take the program back. Mara opened it. Across the first page, he had drawn tiny breaths between phrases—the same marks she had suggested while listening to him rehearse.

[5]From the other side of the curtain came a child’s sharp question: “Are they starting?” Mara tested the first sentence under her breath. It sounded smaller than she remembered, but it was there. She handed Eli the tape. “The ring is above your left shoulder.”

[6]When the lights dimmed, Mara stepped into the narrow pool of brightness. For a moment, she watched the curtain tremble behind her. Then she found the first small pencil mark on the program, drew a breath, and began.

Read the complete text. Notice the character’s choices and the turning point.

Reverse the Discount Machine

M1Level 3
After a 25% discount, a jacket costs 36 dollars. What was its original price?
Editable until committed
Which quantity is the whole?
Is 36 dollars the original 100%, or the part that remains? Should the original price be greater or less than 36 dollars?

Guided path 1 · Reverse the machine

Name the base. Let p be the original price. A 25% discount removes one quarter of that base.
Keep the uncut slice. The retained share is 75%, so the setup is 0.75 × p = 36.
Undo, then check. Divide the paid amount by the retained multiplier. Finish the arithmetic yourself; then reapply the discount.

Check, then Commit response; that response locks. No per-item timer. Level 4 is a supported stretch.

Light practice · Geometry stretch · 0:43–0:46

Scale the Box in Three Directions

M2Level 4
A rectangular prism measures 4 cm by 3 cm by 2 cm. Every dimension is doubled. What is the volume of the enlarged prism?
Editable until committed
Three directions, not one
If you double the number of cubes along the length, width, and height, how many separate doubling factors enter your product?

Guided path 2 · Count the layers

Label three dimensions. Volume counts layers of unit cubes: length × width × height.
Scale each direction. Write the new dimensions as (2 × 4), (2 × 3), and (2 × 2), all in centimeters.
Multiply the new dimensions. Keep all three factors. Finish independently and label the result in cubic centimeters.

Keep the drawing’s labels separate from the volume calculation.

Formula Recall: Shapes and Percents

Around and inside a circle

C = 2πr = πd
A = πr²

Say which is linear and which is square units. What does the radius measure?

Volume is base × height

V = Bh
Prism: V = lwh
Cylinder: V = πr²h

B is base area, not a side length. Why are the final units cubic?

Percents are multipliers

new = original × (1 ± r)
original = new ÷ (1 ± r)

r is the decimal rate. Use + for increase and − for decrease. Which amount is the base?
