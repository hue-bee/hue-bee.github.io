## Final Sprint Sequence (September 7 – Test Day, November 2026)

The existing plan's last dated week is September 2 (Algebra: Plugins). Assuming the November SHSAT falls in the first half of November, this leaves roughly 9 weeks. The sprint is organized in three sub-phases: **Diagnostic-Driven Repair (Weeks 11-14)**, **Full-Length Practice & Timing (Weeks 15-17)**, and **Taper & Confidence (Weeks 18-19)**.

### Phase A — Diagnostic-Driven Repair (Weeks 11–14, Sept 7–Oct 4)

**Week 11 — Data & Statistics Rebuild**
Math: Mean, median, mode, range; reading and constructing basic bar/line/scatter graphs; interpreting box plots and frequency tables — Julius has scored 0% on this domain on both tests, so this is taught from the ground up rather than reviewed. ELA: Fiction passage strategy — model annotating for character motivation, conflict, and tone shifts using two short fiction excerpts, since 27.78% accuracy signals unfamiliarity with narrative conventions rather than a skill deficit.
Homework: 10 data/statistics problems (mixed graph types) + one fiction excerpt with 6 questions, annotated in the "detail → what it proves" format.

**Week 12 — Measurement in Context & Percent Multi-Step**
Math: Revisit unit conversions but embed them in multi-step word problems (rate × time, scale drawings, density); percent of a percent, successive discounts/markups, percent change. ELA: Second fiction passage plus explicit re-teaching of "Connecting Specific Info to Main Purpose" — for every supporting-detail question, Julius writes the connecting sentence before selecting an answer choice.
Homework: 8 measurement word problems + 8 multi-step percent problems; one fiction passage with all detail-to-purpose questions labeled.

**Week 13 — Arithmetic Speed & Accuracy Audit**
Math: Diagnose why Arithmetic dropped from 100% to 25% — run a timed 15-problem arithmetic set (fractions, decimals, order of operations, negative numbers) and have Julius narrate his process aloud to catch whether errors are careless or conceptual; drill mental-math shortcuts for speed. ELA: Third fiction passage under light time pressure (10 minutes) to build stamina and pacing for narrative texts specifically.
Homework: Two timed arithmetic sets (10 problems each, 8-minute limit) + one fiction passage timed at 9 minutes.

**Week 14 — Inequalities & Ratios Precision Drill**
Math: Compound inequalities, graphing solution sets, inequality word problems; ratio/proportion problems with multiple steps or unit changes (the domain where Julius's accuracy dropped despite being a prior strength — likely careless errors under complexity). ELA: Mixed review passage set combining fiction, nonfiction, and one "Connecting Specific Info" heavy passage; error log review focused specifically on whether Julius's ELA misses cluster around this one skill.
Homework: 12 inequality/ratio mixed problems + one full ELA passage set (8 questions) with error-type labeling.

### Phase B — Full-Length Practice & Timing (Weeks 15–17, Oct 11–Oct 25)

**Week 15 — Full-Length Practice Test #1 (Diagnostic C)**
Administer a complete, timed released SHSAT form (Barron's or NYC DOE) under real test conditions (proctored, no breaks beyond the official ones). Score and generate a fresh accuracy-by-domain breakdown to compare against Diagnostics A and B.
Homework: None assigned this week — this is a rest/decompression period after the full test.

**Week 16 — Targeted Repair from Diagnostic C**
Zoom session restructured: spend the full 60 minutes reviewing Diagnostic C's specific misses, prioritizing whichever domains (Data/Statistics, Measurement, Percents, Fiction, Connecting Info) still show weakness. This week is intentionally not pre-planned by topic — it is built live from the new data, following the same "walk through every missed problem, group by error type" protocol used in Week 1.
Homework: Redo every Diagnostic C error under untimed, open-note conditions, then redo again timed and closed-note.

**Week 17 — Full-Length Practice Test #2 + Pacing Refinement**
Second full-length timed practice test, ideally a different released form than Week 15, focusing on pacing strategy: how many minutes per Math and ELA question Julius can afford, when to skip and flag versus grind, and bubble-sheet/answer-tracking hygiene for the real test format.
Homework: Written pacing plan in Julius's own words — how he will allocate time section by section on test day.

### Phase C — Taper & Confidence (Weeks 18–19, Nov 1–Test Day)

**Week 18 — Light Mixed Review & Confidence Building**
Short, high-success-rate mixed review sets across all domains — deliberately easier and lighter than prior weeks to build confidence and avoid burnout in the final stretch. Review test-day logistics: what to bring, admission ticket, calculator rules (none allowed), sleep/nutrition plan.
Homework: One short mixed Math set (10 problems) + one short ELA passage; both untimed, confidence-focused.

**Week 19 — Final Check-In (2–3 Days Before Test)**
Brief session (30-45 min) reviewing Julius's personal "cheat sheet" of formulas (circle/volume formulas, percent-change formula, inequality-flip rule) and his top 2-3 recurring error patterns from the error log across the entire program. No new content introduced.
Homework: None — rest before test day.

## Priority Ranking for the Sprint

If time runs short, the highest-leverage areas to prioritize, based on where Julius is furthest below a domain he has previously mastered or where the domain is entirely new, are:

- Data/Statistics and Measurement (Math) — never above 33%, still untaught in depth
- Connecting Specific Info to Main Purpose (ELA) — regressed and is Julius's single lowest ELA skill
- Fiction passages (ELA) — sharp, unexplained regression suggesting unfamiliarity with narrative text conventions
- Arithmetic speed/accuracy (Math) — a former strength that collapsed, likely fixable through timed drilling rather than re-teaching content
- Percents and Ratios/Proportions (Math) — both regressed despite being previously solid, suggesting careless-error patterns under multi-step complexity

This sequencing keeps the two full-length practice tests (Weeks 15 and 17) as the primary diagnostic checkpoints, so the plan can adapt in real time to whatever gaps remain closest to test day, consistent with how the original plan used Diagnostics A and B to drive its own content sequencing.
