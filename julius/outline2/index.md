Julius has made strong progress — his total score climbed from 462 (March diagnostic) to 530 (June mock, 88/114 raw) — but the diagnostics show that gains are uneven: Math swung from a 219 to 265 scaled score with real strength gains in Algebra (85.71%) and Word Problems (75%), while Arithmetic (25%), Data/Statistics (0%), and Measurements (0%) collapsed on the second test. ELA held steady at 77.19% overall but Fiction passages (27.78%) and Connecting Specific Information to Main Purpose (30.77%) remain clear gaps. ## Structural Principle for the Final Phase

Every remaining week should follow a "diagnose → isolate → re-test" loop rather than pure content delivery, since most target skills have already been introduced once. Each session keeps the same 60-minute Zoom format from SHSATPlan-rev.md (warm-up/error-log review, 35-minute Math block, 13-minute ELA block, homework assignment), but the Math and ELA blocks now alternate between **remediation weeks** (re-teaching a specific failure pattern) and **integrated timed-practice weeks** (mixed-topic sets simulating real test pressure).

## Weeks 1–3 (Sept 7 – Sept 27): Close the Two Untouched Content Gaps

**Math focus — Data/Statistics and Measurement (true 0% gaps):**
These are the only two Math domains where Julius has never scored above 33% on any diagnostic, making them the highest-leverage remaining content to teach from scratch.
- Week 1: Mean, median, mode, range; reading and interpreting frequency tables, bar graphs, and dot plots; box-and-whisker basics. Practice: 10 problems drawn directly from released DOE Data/Statistics questions, since these appeared on both diagnostics (questions 63, 77, 110 on Test 1; 75, 110 on Test 2).
- Week 2: Scatter plots and line of best fit (y = mx + b interpretation — question 76 on Test 1 tested this exact skill and was answered correctly, so build from that success); probability-and-statistics crossover problems.
- Week 3: Measurement — unit rate problems embedded in real-world contexts, converting between compound units (e.g., speed, density), and reading measurement from diagrams (the type Julius missed on questions 64 and 101 on the two tests).

**ELA focus — Fiction passage strategy rebuild:**
Fiction dropped from 83.33% to 27.78%, the single largest ELA regression. Devote these three weeks to character-motivation mapping, distinguishing narrator tone from character tone, and practicing "Connecting Specific Information to Main Purpose" questions specifically within fiction excerpts, since that skill sits at only 30.77% overall and is disproportionately tested in fiction and poetry passages.

## Weeks 4–6 (Sept 28 – Oct 18): Fluency and Consistency Drills

**Math focus — Arithmetic and Geometry re-anchoring:**
Arithmetic collapsing from 100% to 25% strongly suggests careless/timing errors rather than a true content gap (fraction, decimal, and negative-number operations were previously mastered). Run short, heavily timed arithmetic sets (6–8 minutes for 10 problems) each week to rebuild speed and accuracy simultaneously, paired with continued Geometry mixed practice (angles, circles, volume, area) drawn from all prior weeks' content to consolidate the 54.55% into a reliable 75%+.

**ELA focus — Inference and mixed passage timing:**
Run one full timed passage set per week mixing nonfiction, fiction, and poetry, since Julius's inference accuracy varies by passage type. After each set, require him to categorize every miss using the existing Error Log (Content Gap / Careless / Rushing) — the diagnostics suggest most ELA misses in June were Careless or Rushing given his strong underlying Main Idea (75-100%) and Inference (100% on Test 2's nonfiction section) skills.

## Weeks 7–8 (Oct 19 – Nov 1): Full-Length Simulated Exams

Administer two full, timed, released SHSAT practice forms under real test-day conditions (same time of day, no notes, strict 180-minute limit), one per week. After each, conduct a full session dedicated only to error-log review, prioritizing any recurrence of Data/Statistics, Measurement, or Fiction misses, since those are the areas with the least proven mastery under pressure.

## Week 9 (Nov 2 – exam date): Taper and Confidence Building

Light review only — a condensed formula sheet review (circle/volume/angle formulas, exponent rules, plug-in strategy steps), 15–20 minutes of easy-to-medium mixed problems to build confidence, and one short nonfiction passage for warm-up. Avoid introducing anything new or running another full timed test this close to the exam, since the goal is consolidation and stamina preservation rather than additional content exposure.
