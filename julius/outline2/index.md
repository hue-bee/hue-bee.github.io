
# Julius Dejori — Final Sprint SHSAT Study Plan (September–November 2026)
### Updated for the Computer-Adaptive Test (CAT) Format — Test Day: Wednesday, November 18, 2026

## What's Different on the 2026 CAT — And Why It Changes This Plan

Beginning with the Fall 2026 administration, the SHSAT is no longer a fixed-form digital test — it is now a full **Computer-Adaptive Test (CAT)**, and this changes both the number of questions Julius will see and the strategy he needs going into test day [cite:2][cite:3]. The most important structural changes:

- **50 scored questions per section (100 total)**, down from 57 per section (114 total) — every question now carries more relative weight, and the item pool is drawn dynamically rather than fixed [cite:9][cite:16].
- **Forward-only navigation on Math and on standalone ELA items (grammar/revising-editing).** Once Julius submits an answer and advances, he cannot go back, skip ahead and return, or change that answer — the "skip the hard one, come back later" strategy from the old plan no longer applies to these items [cite:3][cite:15][cite:16].
- **Limited flexibility only within ELA passage sets.** Julius can move between questions tied to the *same* Reading Comprehension passage and revise his answers freely, but once he submits that entire passage set, it locks and he cannot return [cite:2][cite:11][cite:13].
- **Difficulty adapts after every single response.** A correct answer raises his running ability estimate and triggers a harder next question; an incorrect answer lowers the estimate and triggers an easier one. This runs independently for ELA and for Math [cite:4][cite:9].
- **Early questions carry outsized weight.** Because the algorithm calibrates Julius's ability level from his first responses, an early careless error can route him toward an easier — and lower-scoring — difficulty path that is hard to recover from later in the same section [cite:10][cite:5].
- **Content and standards are unchanged.** The DOE has been explicit that this is a delivery/sequencing change, not a curriculum change — Julius still needs the same math domains (ratios/proportions, number system/expressions, equations/inequalities, statistics/probability, geometry) and the same ELA skills (Revising/Editing plus Reading Comprehension) [cite:2][cite:10][cite:11].
- **Calculator policy and total time are unchanged** — still no calculator, still 180 minutes, self-paced between the two sections [cite:3][cite:8].

Given these shifts, the priority for this sprint moves from *"maximize total correct answers across a fixed set"* to *"maximize first-attempt accuracy, especially early in each section, since there is no going back to fix a rushed guess."* Julius's existing content weaknesses (data/statistics, measurement, fiction passages, connecting-info questions) remain exactly as urgent as before — but the training now needs an added layer of **decision confidence under a no-backtrack format**, since re-checking or skip-and-return drills that used to be part of his practice routine no longer reflect real test conditions [cite:15][cite:16].

## Final Sprint Sequence (September 7 – November 18, 2026 Test Day)

The existing plan's last dated week is September 2 (Algebra: Plugins). The SHSAT has now been officially confirmed for **Wednesday, November 18, 2026** [cite:14], which gives roughly 10 weeks. The sprint is organized in three sub-phases: **Diagnostic-Driven Repair (Weeks 11-14)**, **Full-Length Adaptive-Style Practice & Timing (Weeks 15-17)**, and **Taper & Confidence (Weeks 18-20)**. Every full-length or timed drill from this point forward should be run under **no-backtrack conditions** on Math and standalone ELA items, to build the first-attempt-accuracy habit the CAT now demands.

### Phase A — Diagnostic-Driven Repair (Weeks 11–14, Sept 7–Oct 4)

**Week 11 — Data & Statistics Rebuild**
Math: Mean, median, mode, range; reading and constructing basic bar/line/scatter graphs; interpreting box plots and frequency tables — Julius has scored 0% on this domain on both tests, so this is taught from the ground up rather than reviewed. ELA: Fiction passage strategy — model annotating for character motivation, conflict, and tone shifts using two short fiction excerpts, since 27.78% accuracy signals unfamiliarity with narrative conventions rather than a skill deficit.
Homework: 10 data/statistics problems (mixed graph types), each answered once with no going back to check, to simulate CAT forward-only Math + one fiction excerpt with 6 questions, annotated in the "detail → what it proves" format.

**Week 12 — Measurement in Context & Percent Multi-Step**
Math: Revisit unit conversions but embed them in multi-step word problems (rate × time, scale drawings, density); percent of a percent, successive discounts/markups, percent change — practiced under a "commit and move on" rule, since Math is now forward-only on test day. ELA: Second fiction passage plus explicit re-teaching of "Connecting Specific Info to Main Purpose" — for every supporting-detail question, Julius writes the connecting sentence before selecting an answer choice.
Homework: 8 measurement word problems + 8 multi-step percent problems, answered in forward-only order; one fiction passage with all detail-to-purpose questions labeled, self-checked once before final submission (mirroring the ELA passage-set revision window).

**Week 13 — Arithmetic Speed & Accuracy Audit**
Math: Diagnose why Arithmetic dropped from 100% to 25% — run a timed 15-problem arithmetic set (fractions, decimals, order of operations, negative numbers) under strict forward-only rules and have Julius narrate his process aloud to catch whether errors are careless or conceptual; drill mental-math shortcuts for speed, since a careless slip can no longer be fixed later in the section. ELA: Third fiction passage under light time pressure (10 minutes) to build stamina and pacing for narrative texts specifically.
Homework: Two timed arithmetic sets (10 problems each, 8-minute limit, no backtracking allowed) + one fiction passage timed at 9 minutes.

**Week 14 — Inequalities & Ratios Precision Drill**
Math: Compound inequalities, graphing solution sets, inequality word problems; ratio/proportion problems with multiple steps or unit changes (the domain where Julius's accuracy dropped despite being a prior strength — likely careless errors under complexity, now higher-stakes under forward-only navigation). ELA: Mixed review passage set combining fiction, nonfiction, and one "Connecting Specific Info" heavy passage; error log review focused specifically on whether Julius's ELA misses cluster around this one skill.
Homework: 12 inequality/ratio mixed problems answered one-way, no revisiting + one full ELA passage set (8 questions) with error-type labeling.

### Phase B — Full-Length Adaptive-Style Practice & Timing (Weeks 15–17, Oct 11–Oct 25)

**Week 15 — Full-Length Practice Test #1 (Diagnostic C)**
Administer a complete, timed released SHSAT form under simulated CAT conditions as closely as possible: Julius must answer each Math and standalone ELA item before advancing and cannot return to it, while Reading Comprehension passage sets allow internal revision until submitted — matching official DOE navigation rules for the new format [cite:2][cite:15]. Score and generate a fresh accuracy-by-domain breakdown to compare against Diagnostics A and B, noting that the item count on test day is now 50 ELA + 50 Math rather than 57 + 57 [cite:3][cite:9].
Homework: None assigned this week — this is a rest/decompression period after the full test.

**Week 16 — Targeted Repair from Diagnostic C**
Zoom session restructured: spend the full 60 minutes reviewing Diagnostic C's specific misses, prioritizing whichever domains (Data/Statistics, Measurement, Percents, Fiction, Connecting Info) still show weakness, with special attention to any errors made early in a section — since on the real CAT, an early miss shifts Julius onto an easier, lower-ceiling difficulty path for the rest of that section [cite:5][cite:10]. This week is intentionally not pre-planned by topic — it is built live from the new data, following the same "walk through every missed problem, group by error type" protocol used in Week 1.
Homework: Redo every Diagnostic C error under untimed, open-note conditions, then redo again timed, closed-note, and forward-only.

**Week 17 — Full-Length Practice Test #2 + Pacing Refinement**
Second full-length timed practice test, ideally a different released form than Week 15, again run under no-backtrack Math/standalone-ELA rules, focusing on pacing strategy: how many minutes per Math and ELA question Julius can afford, how to commit confidently to an answer the first time since skip-and-return is no longer available, and bubble-sheet/answer-tracking hygiene appropriate to the CAT interface.
Homework: Written pacing plan in Julius's own words — how he will allocate time section by section on test day, including a explicit note on not second-guessing submitted Math answers.

### Phase C — Taper & Confidence (Weeks 18–20, Nov 1–Test Day)

**Week 18 — Light Mixed Review & Confidence Building**
Short, high-success-rate mixed review sets across all domains — deliberately easier and lighter than prior weeks to build confidence and avoid burnout in the final stretch, all practiced under forward-only conditions so the habit stays fresh. Review test-day logistics: what to bring, admission ticket, calculator rules (none allowed), sleep/nutrition plan, and a final walkthrough of the CAT navigation rules so there are no surprises on test day [cite:14][cite:15].
Homework: One short mixed Math set (10 problems) + one short ELA passage; both untimed, confidence-focused.

**Week 19 — Final Check-In (Nov 9–12, roughly one week before test day)**
Brief session (30-45 min) reviewing Julius's personal "cheat sheet" of formulas (circle/volume formulas, percent-change formula, inequality-flip rule) and his top 2-3 recurring error patterns from the error log across the entire program. Reinforce the single most important mental shift for test day: commit to each Math and standalone ELA answer with full attention the first time, since there is no going back. No new content introduced.
Homework: Light, optional review only — no new problem sets assigned.

**Week 20 — Rest & Final Confirmation (Nov 13–17, days before test day)**
Confirm test-day logistics (registration closed October 30; admission ticket, location, and arrival time should already be finalized) [cite:14]. No academic work — full rest, sleep, and light confidence-building conversation only.
Homework: None — rest before test day.
