Warm-Up · Quick Recall

Before We Move On: Prove It Stuck

Think first
If a number is multiplied by 3 and then increased by 5 to get 20, what was the FIRST thing done to the number? What should be the LAST thing you undo to solve it?
Why this matters
Every SHSAT test has several problems that are just paragraphs hiding a simple equation. Once you can translate reliably, you turn a "hard" word problem into an "easy" one-line solve.
Your Habit Target
Today isn't about grinding more equations. It's about building one repeatable habit: read the sentence once for meaning, then translate word-by-word into symbols, then solve. Say each translation step out loud before you write it.
Zone 1 · Multi-Step Equations

Solving Is Just Running the Machine Backward

Think about it
In 2x + 3 = 17, imagine x went into a machine: first it got multiplied by 2, then 3 got added. If you wanted to reverse the machine and get x back out, which step would you undo first — the +3 or the ×2?
Undo in reverse order (last operation done, first undone)
2x + 3 = 17  →  2x = 14  →  x = 7
Why reverse order works
The order of operations built the expression outward from x: multiply, then add. To peel it back to x alone, you subtract (undo the add) before you divide (undo the multiply) — the exact opposite order.
Zone 1 · Practice

Multi-Step Equations — Easy to Hard

1Level 1
Solve 4x + 5 = 25. What's the last operation done to x, and what should you undo first?
2Level 2
Solve 3x - 7 = -1. Watch your signs carefully when you add 7 to both sides.
3. Level 4: Solve -2(x - 4) + 6 = 2x - 10. Which value of x makes this true?
SHSAT Trap: Undoing Out of Order
A classic error is dividing before subtracting, which drags the constant into the division too. Always undo addition/subtraction across the WHOLE side before you touch multiplication or division.
Zone 2 · Distributing & Combining Like TermsHigh-Frequency

Clean the Equation Before You Solve It

Think about it
In 3(x + 4), does the 3 multiply just the x, or does it have to reach every term inside the parentheses? If you have 3 apples and 4 oranges and you triple the whole basket, how many of each do you get?
3(x + 4) = 3x + 12
Two cleanup moves, every time
Distribute — multiply the outside number by EVERY term inside the parentheses. Combine like terms — add up all the x-terms together and all the plain numbers together, on each side separately, before you touch the equals sign.
Zone 2 · Practice

Distribute & Combine — Easy to Hard

4Level 1
Simplify 2(x + 5) + 3x into one clean expression before doing anything else.
5Level 3
Solve 4(x - 2) = 2x + 6. Distribute on the left, then move the x-terms to one side.
6. Level 4: Solve -3(2x - 1) = 5(x + 2) - 1. What is x?
SHSAT Trap: Distributing a Negative Sign Halfway
In -3(2x - 1), the negative sign must multiply BOTH terms: -3 × 2x = -6x, and -3 × -1 = +3. Forgetting the sign flip on the second term is one of the most common careless errors on the test.
Zone 3 · Word-to-Equation TranslationCore Skill

Every Word Problem Is a Sentence in Disguise

Think about it
"3 more than twice a number is 17." Read it piece by piece: what does "twice a number" become? What does "3 more than" add? What does "is" always mean in translation? Build the equation left to right, exactly matching the sentence order.

Zone 3 · Practice

Word-to-Equation Translation — Easy to Hard

7Level 1
Translate and solve: "5 less than a number is 12." What number is it?
8Level 2
Translate and solve: "Three times a number, increased by 4, is 25." Write the equation before you solve it.
9. Level 4: "The sum of two consecutive integers is 47." Which equation correctly represents this?
SHSAT Trap: "Less Than" Flips the Order
"5 less than a number" is NOT 5 - x. It's x - 5. Whatever comes right before "less than" gets subtracted, but it goes SECOND in the expression — the opposite order of how you read it.
Zone 4 · Variables on Both SidesStretch Goal

Herd the x's to One Side First

Think about it
In 5x + 2 = 2x + 14, there are x-terms on both sides. If you subtract 2x from BOTH sides, what happens to the x-term on the right? Does the equation stay balanced?
5x + 2 = 2x + 14  →  3x + 2 = 14  →  x = 4
A simple rule of thumb
Move the SMALLER x-coefficient to the other side — it keeps your remaining x-term positive and avoids extra sign flips. Then it's just a regular multi-step equation you already know how to solve.
Zone 4 · Practice

Variables on Both Sides — Easy to Hard

10Level 2
Solve 6x - 3 = 3x + 12. Move the smaller x-term first.
11Level 3
Solve 2(x + 3) = 5x - 3. Distribute before you herd the x's.
12. Level 4: A number translated as "twice the sum of a number and 5 equals 3 less than the number" solves to which value?
SHSAT Trap: Combining Across the Equals Sign
You can only combine like terms that are on the SAME side of the equals sign. 6x and 3x can't be combined until 3x has been moved across — and moving across always flips its sign.
Zone 5 · Accuracy

Three Ways to Check an Equation Answer

Worked example: "3 more than twice a number is 17" → n = 7
CheckBefore You Start
Re-Read the Original Sentence
Does your equation match the sentence word for word? Read it back out loud as English — "twice a number, plus 3, is 17" — before you trust the algebra.
CheckAfter You Finish
Plug It Back Into the Sentence
Substitute n = 7: twice 7 is 14, plus 3 is 17. It matches the ORIGINAL sentence, not just your final equation — this catches translation errors, not just arithmetic ones.
CheckIf Time Allows
Re-Solve From a Different Line
Pick a middle step in your work and solve forward again from there. Landing on the same final answer twice is strong proof you didn't make a slip.
SHSAT Trap: Solving the Wrong Equation
The most common word-problem error isn't an algebra mistake — it's translating the sentence incorrectly in the first place. Always check your answer against the ENGLISH sentence, not just the equation you wrote.
ELA · Revising/Editing

Good Edits Make Meaning Clearer, Not Just Shorter

Think about it
Read: "Walking into the room, the lights were off." Who is doing the walking — the lights? What's wrong with this sentence, and how would you rewrite it so the subject actually matches the opening phrase?
Three common issues to hunt for
  • Redundancy — saying the same idea twice ("return back," "each and every").
  • Dangling modifiers — an opening phrase that doesn't logically attach to the subject.
  • Sentence clarity — vague pronouns, awkward word order, or run-ons that hide the main idea.
  • Julius scored 50% on Revising/Editing in Diag. A — today builds a repeatable "spot the issue" habit.
ELA · Practice

Spot the Sentence Issue

13Level 2
"The reason he was late is because he missed the bus." What redundancy is hiding in this sentence, and how would you fix it?
14Level 3
"Running quickly to catch the train, the doors closed just in time." Identify the dangling modifier and rewrite the sentence so it's logically clear who is running.
SHSAT Trap: "No Change" Isn't Always Safe
Don't default to "no change" just because a sentence sounds familiar. Read it literally — if the opening phrase doesn't logically describe the subject that follows, it needs a fix.
ELA · Practice, Continued

Two More Editing Checks

15Level 1
"She combined the two ingredients together in the bowl." Which word is redundant, and why can it be removed without losing meaning?
16Level 4
"After finishing his homework, the television was turned on by Marco to relax." This sentence has both a dangling modifier AND passive voice. Rewrite it as one clear, active sentence.
Habit Check
Before picking an edit, ask: "does this version say the exact same thing, only clearer and without repeats?" If an option changes the meaning, it's wrong — even if it sounds nicer.
