Pattern Location

When Did Accuracy Begin to Drift?

Early · Q1–5Fresh attention
Middle · Q6–10Routine settles
Late · Q11–15Fatigue pressure
Cluster early?
Ask whether the opening routine was rushed: Did you read the operation signs before touching the numbers? Did narration begin immediately?
Cluster late?
Look for shortening work, skipped labels, and sudden answer-size errors. These suggest stamina or clock pressure—not necessarily missing content.
Same operation repeatedly?
A repeated breakdown around fraction division, negative signs, or nested brackets points toward a skill or strategy gap.
Errors scattered?
If the content changes but the error repeats—misreading, skipping a line, committing too fast—the habit is the real target.
The decisive question
“Which explanation predicts the next error before it happens?” A useful diagnosis must make a testable prediction.

First Read · Part 1

Find the Story’s Pressure Point

Annotate
Original fiction · “The Brass Compass” · Part 1 of 2

With eleven minutes until the curtain rose, Lena found the empty velvet square where the brass compass should have been. The prop table held everything else in its white-tape outlines: the captain’s gloves, the rolled map, the chipped blue cup. Only the compass—a small object that appeared in the final scene—was gone.

“It was here after rehearsal,” Lena said, though no one had asked. Her voice sounded too loud in the narrow wing.

Beyond the curtain, the audience settled in a soft storm of programs and coughs. Lena could hear Mr. Ortiz, the drama teacher, giving the actors their final instructions. Last week she had missed a lighting cue, and although nobody had blamed her, she had spent every rehearsal since then arriving early, checking lists twice, and fastening a pencil to her clipboard with string.

She searched beneath the table, then inside the prop chest. Nothing. Her first thought was to tell Mr. Ortiz. Her second was that telling him would make the missing compass officially her mistake.

Dev, who played the captain, appeared in costume. “You have it?” he whispered.

Lena looked at the blank square of velvet. “I’m tracing it.”

Dev nodded as if this were a complete answer. “Good. You notice things.” Then he slipped back toward the stage.

Silent margin question
What does Lena fear losing besides the compass?

First Read · Part 2

Watch the Emotional Weather Change

Annotate
Original fiction · “The Brass Compass” · Part 2 of 2

His confidence stopped her. Lena had been searching as if speed could erase the problem. Now she forced herself to stand still.

She replayed the afternoon: the map had been unrolled, the cup had been carried to center stage, and the compass had been passed from Dev to Mara during the storm scene. Mara’s costume had snagged on something near the painted mast. Lena crouched beside it. A faint crescent of gold showed beneath a fold of black canvas.

The compass was wedged between the mast’s wooden base and its fabric cover. When Lena tugged it free, a smear of blue paint crossed the lid.

Mr. Ortiz entered the wing. “Problem?”

For half a second, Lena considered hiding the paint with her thumb. Then she held out the compass. “Found it. The mast caught it, and the lid is marked. It still opens.”

Mr. Ortiz examined the smear. “Can the audience see it?”

Lena stepped back to the distance of the first row. The blue line vanished against the metal’s dull shine.

“Then we have a compass,” he said. “After the show, we’ll have a better prop check.”

The stage manager raised two fingers: two minutes. Lena returned the compass to its velvet square, this time placing a strip of tape across the open side. The audience beyond the curtain no longer sounded like a storm. It sounded like rain moving away.

Silent margin question
Which action shows the clearest change in how Lena handles responsibility?

Turn the Audit Into One Observable Rule

My next-set accuracy rule
Write an action another person could see or hear. Avoid vague rules such as “be careful” or “focus more.”
Before I commit, I will…
Stress-test the rule
“Which error tag does this prevent? At what exact moment will you use it? How will your tutor know you did it?”

The goal is not “never miss.” The goal is “catch the pattern sooner.”
