Wind at the Roofline

1Noor had checked the rooftop weather station three times before the science fair judges arrived. The anemometer cups spun freely. The rain gauge stood level. On her tablet, a blue line recorded each gust that crossed the school roof. After six weeks of missed lunches and cold fingertips, the station was finally collecting clean data.

7“You built all this yourself?” asked Mr. Ibarra, the custodian, setting down his toolbox.

9“Mostly,” Noor said. Her father had helped drill the mounting holes, and her classmate Theo had written two lines of code, but those facts felt like footnotes. The judges were coming to see her project.

14A gust rattled the fence around the roof. The tablet’s blue line jumped, vanished, and became a row of gray dashes.

17Noor restarted the program. Nothing. She unplugged the sensor cable and pushed it back in. The dashes remained.

21“Wind tugged something loose,” Mr. Ibarra said. He opened his toolbox.

24“I’ve got it.” Noor crouched beside the station. If he repaired it now, the judges might think the whole project belonged to an adult with a toolbox.

29Through the stairwell window, Noor saw the first judge crossing the courtyard below. She traced the cable beneath the platform, but it disappeared behind a metal bracket. Her gloves were too thick to reach the connector. When she pulled them off, the wind stung her fingers.

36Mr. Ibarra did not move closer. “A weather station is a team,” he said. “Every instrument does one job, and the record works because the jobs connect.”

41Noor looked at the silent tablet. On its screen, six weeks of blue lines ended in a blank space. She remembered her father holding the ladder steady while she measured the roof, and Theo squinting at the code until the numbers appeared. The project had never been less hers because other hands had steadied it.

49“Can you hold the bracket back?” she asked.

51Mr. Ibarra wedged a screwdriver beneath the metal. Noor reached past it and found the cable pinched against a sharp edge. She freed the wire, pressed the connector into place, and wrapped it with a strip of rubber from the toolbox.

59The gray dashes blinked. Then a blue line climbed across the screen.

62When the judges stepped onto the roof, Noor was labeling the repair in her display notes. Under Unexpected Problems, she wrote: Loose sensor cable. Diagnosed and repaired with assistance.

69“Ready?” one judge asked.

71Noor glanced at Mr. Ibarra, who had already lifted his toolbox. “We are,” she said, and began with the wind.
