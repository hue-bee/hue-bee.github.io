Zone 1 · Sentence Clarity & RedundancyHigh-Frequency

If Deleting a Word Changes Nothing, Delete It

Think about it
“She returned back to her house again.” Cross out one word at a time. Which words can you remove without losing meaning?
The one-question test
Cut the word. Did the meaning shrink? No → cut it. Yes → keep it.
Zone 1 · Practice

Redundancy & Clarity — Easy to Hard

1Level 1
“The two twins both look alike.” Which single word makes “twins” redundant with something else in the sentence?
2Level 2
Revise for clarity: “Each and every student must individually complete their own personal assignment.” How many words repeat “individual”?
3Level 3
“The reason why the trip was cancelled is because of the storm.” Two words are doing the same job. Name them, then fix the sentence.
4Level 4
Which revision best fixes: “The committee, in their final decision, ultimately decided to postpone the annual yearly event”?
SHSAT Trap: “Shorter” Isn't Automatically Correct
Cut only words whose meaning is already stated elsewhere. Confirm no real information disappears.
Zone 2 · Dangling & Misplaced Modifiers

The Opening Phrase Must Describe the Very Next Noun

Think about it
“Running late for school, the bus was missed by Maria.” Who is doing the running? Is the noun immediately after the comma that person?
Opening phrase → comma → the doer of that action, immediately
The clever shortcut
Cover everything before the comma. Does the first noun after the comma make sense as the one doing that opening action? If not, the modifier is dangling.
Zone 2 · Practice

Spot the Dangling Modifier

5Level 1
“Excited for the trip, the suitcase was packed by Leo.” Does “the suitcase” feel excited?
6Level 2
“After finishing his homework, the television was turned on.” Rewrite so the doer appears right after the comma.
7Level 3
“Covered in mud, Jamal hosed off the dog.” Is this modifier dangling, or misplaced but logical? Who is actually covered in mud?
8Level 4
Which sentence has NO modifier error? Check the opening phrase against the very next noun.
SHSAT Trap: The Fix Isn't Always “Add a Name”
Sometimes the correct fix rewrites the whole sentence. Reread the full revision for sense.
Zone 3 · Full Editing PassageSHSAT-Style

Read for Meaning First, Then Hunt for Errors

Think about it
If you fix grammar sentence-by-sentence without reading the whole passage first, what could you miss — a tone shift, a pronoun with no clear antecedent, or an idea explained later?
The two-pass method
  • Pass 1 — Meaning: read once and get the gist.
  • Pass 2 — Line by line: name the error type.
  • Check that each revision still fits the surrounding sentences.
  • “No change” is a real answer; do not force an edit.
Zone 3 · Practice Passage, Part 1

Edit the Underlined Parts

(1) Many students think that studying for a test the night before is the most best way to prepare. (2) Rushing to memorize everything at once, the information often does not stick. (3) Instead, research shows that spacing out review sessions over several days improves memory retention and recall.
9Level 2
Sentence 1: “the most best way.” What error is this, and what is the one-word fix?
10Level 3
Apply the cover-and-check test to sentence 2. What is the actual error, and how would you rewrite it?
SHSAT Trap: Fixing a Sentence That Wasn't Broken
Sentence 3 has no error. Resist the urge to “improve” wording that is already correct.

Zone 4 · Solving Inequalities

Solve It Exactly Like an Equation — With One Catch

Think about it
Start with 2 < 4. Multiply both sides by -1. Is -2 < -4 still true? What must happen to the symbol to make the statement true again?
The one extra rule
× or ÷ by a negative → FLIP the inequality sign
Worked example
Solve -3x + 5 > 20: subtract 5 → -3x > 15; divide by -3 (flip!) → x < -5.
Zone 4 · Practice

Solving Inequalities — Easy to Hard

13Level 1
Solve x + 7 ≤ 12. Do you need to flip the sign?
14Level 2
Solve 4x - 3 > 9. Which operation do you undo first?
15Level 3
Solve -2x + 6 ≥ 16. At what exact step does the sign flip?
16Level 4
Solve -1 ≤ -2x + 3 < 9. Subtract 3 from all three parts, then divide by -2. How many times do you flip?
SHSAT Trap: Flipping at the Wrong Step
Flip only when multiplying or dividing by a negative — never when adding or subtracting a negative.
Zone 5 · Graphing on a Number LineSign-Flip Zone

Open Circle Means “Almost, But Not Quite”

Think about it
For x > 3, is 3 part of the solution? What about for x ≥ 3? Which circle represents “not included”?
< or > → open circle    ≤ or ≥ → closed circle
The clever shortcut
Once x is alone on the left, x < a shades left and x > a shades right.
Zone 5 · Practice

Solve, Then Graph It

17Level 2
Solve 2x - 5 ≥ 1, then describe the graph: open or closed circle, and which direction does it shade?
18Level 4
A number line shows a closed circle at -2 shading left. Which inequality below matches it?
Habit Check
Before graphing, re-isolate x and double-check whether you divided or multiplied by a negative along the way.

One Editing, One Inequality

SHSAT Trap: Word Problems Hide the Symbol
