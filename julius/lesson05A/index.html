Universal Method

The Three Questions That Collapse Complexity

1
What must the answer represent?
Circle the requested unit or quantity. A correct number with the wrong meaning is still wrong.
2
What stays linked?
Name the invariant: total sum, distance, original price, shared ratio part, or boundary condition.
3
What check can disprove me?
Estimate size, trace units, substitute a boundary, or rebuild the original condition.
Say it aloud
“I need ________________. The relationship that stays true is ________________. I will verify by ________________.”
Zone 1 · Data & Statistics

A Mean Is a Hidden Total

Build the idea
If five stacks balance at height 14, how many blocks exist altogether? If one stack is missing, what information recovers it?
Recover the invariant first
total = mean × number of values
Level 5 disguise
The problem may add or remove a value, give a new mean, and ask about an old value. Draw “before” and “after” totals separately.
Guided Solution · Type A

Missing Value From Two Means

ALevel 5
Six quiz scores have mean 82. After a seventh score is included, the mean becomes 84. What was the seventh score?
1
Rebuild “before”
Old total = 6 × 82. Why is multiplying valid even though the six scores differ?
2
Rebuild “after”
New total = 7 × 84. Which count belongs with the new mean?
3
Find the change
Seventh score = (7 × 84) − (6 × 82). Compute, then ask: must it exceed 84?
Self-check
Because the mean rose from 82 to 84, the added score must be above 84. If yours is not, stop before submitting.
Data Practice

Same Core Skill, More Layers

1Level 4
Five temperatures have a mean of 68°F. A sixth temperature raises the mean to 70°F. Find the sixth temperature, then explain why its position relative to 70°F is predictable.
2Level 5
A set of 8 values has mean 15. One value is removed; the remaining 7 values have mean 13. Find the removed value. Which “before/after” total must be larger?
Do not average the two means
Means have different weights when the group sizes differ. Convert each mean to a total before comparing.
Zone 2 · Measurement & Rates

Let the Units Write the Equation

Predict first
You need miles. You are given miles per hour and hours. Which operation makes “hours” disappear?
miles/hour × hours = miles
Two-leg trip rule
Compute each segment’s distance separately, then add. Different rates cannot be averaged unless the times are equal.
Guided Solution · Type B

Two Rates, One Total Distance

BLevel 5
A shuttle travels 1.5 hours at 36 mph and then 45 minutes at 48 mph. How far does it travel altogether?
1
Unify time units
45 minutes = 45/60 hour. Why would multiplying 48 by 45 be dimensionally wrong?
2
Build each segment
d₁ = 36(1.5) and d₂ = 48(45/60). Keep them separate.
3
Combine and bound
d = d₁ + d₂. Before computing: should the total be above or below 90 miles?
Verification
The trip lasts 2.25 hours at rates between 36 and 48 mph, so the total must lie between 81 and 108 miles.

Zone 4 · Ratios

Build a Shared Bridge

Connect the groups
If red:blue = 2:3 and blue:green = 4:5, why can’t you write red:blue:green = 2:3:5?
Bridge rule
The shared category must represent the same amount in both ratios. Scale both versions of “blue” to a common value before joining.
Ratio Practice

Label Every Number by Role

7Level 3
The ratio of red to blue beads is 3:5. If there are 64 beads total, how many are red? Mark part-to-part and part-to-whole before solving.
8Level 5
In Club A, boys:girls = 2:3. In Club B, girls:boys = 4:5. The clubs contain the same number of girls. Find the ratio of all boys to all girls after the clubs combine.
Reversed order trap
Club B gives girls:boys, not boys:girls. Rewrite both ratios in the same category order before building the shared bridge.
Zone 5 · Inequalities

A Constraint Is a Feasible Region

Translate before solving
“At most 40” includes 40. “More than 40” excludes it. Which words decide whether a boundary point is legal?
at most → ≤    at least → ≥
more than → >    fewer than → <
Level 5 move
When two limits must both hold, solve each constraint and test the overlap—not either one alone.
Constraint Practice

Model the Boundary, Then Test It

9Level 4
A museum charges $7 admission plus $3 per exhibit. A student can spend at most $25. Write and solve an inequality for the number of exhibits. Must the final value be a whole number?
10Level 5
A school buys notebooks for $2 each and folders for $3 each. It needs at least 18 items and may spend at most $45. If it buys 8 folders, what whole-number values are possible for the notebooks?
Boundary check
Substitute the smallest and largest whole-number values from your range into both original conditions. Which endpoint fails if your inequality symbol is wrong?
