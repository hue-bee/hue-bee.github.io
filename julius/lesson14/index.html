Zone 1 · MeanRebuild From Zero

Mean Is Leveling the Stacks

Think about it
Imagine four unequal stacks of blocks: 2, 4, 6, and 8 blocks tall. If you could take blocks from the tall stacks and move them to the short stacks until every stack were the same height, how tall would each stack end up? What did you just calculate, without knowing the formula?
Mean formula
mean = (sum of all values) ÷ (number of values)
Why this makes sense
The mean is the "level height" every stack would reach if the total number of blocks were spread perfectly evenly. That's why it's sensitive to one giant stack — a single tall stack pulls the level up for everyone.
Zone 1 · Median, Mode & Range

Median Is a Position, Not a Calculation

Think about it
Line up the numbers 3, 7, 9, 12, 20 from least to greatest. Which number sits exactly in the middle? Now try 3, 7, 9, 12 — an even count. There's no single middle number, so what should you do with the two numbers closest to the middle?
Odd count → middle value    Even count → average the two middle values
Mode & Range in one line each
Mode: the value that shows up most often (a data set can have zero, one, or several modes). Range: the biggest value minus the smallest value — a quick snapshot of spread, but blind to everything in between.
Zone 1 · Guided Practice · I-Do

Center & Spread — Building the Basics

1Level 1
Find the mean, median, mode, and range of: 4, 7, 7, 9, 13. Which measure did you compute first, and why?
2Level 1
Six students scored 82, 88, 91, 88, 95, 76 on a quiz. What is the median? Do you need to reorder the list first — and why does order matter for median but not for mean?
3Level 2
A data set has range 18 and minimum value 5. What is the maximum value? What single operation reverses the range formula?
4Level 2
Test scores: 70, 70, 85, 90, 95. Which measure of center best represents "the typical score" here — mean or mode? Justify using the actual numbers.
SHSAT Trap: Forgetting to Reorder Before Finding the Median
Median requires the list in order from least to greatest first. Students who find the "middle" of the list as given almost always land on the wrong value — always sort first, every time.
Zone 1 · SHSAT-Favorite Twist #1Mandatory Drill

Missing Value, Given the Target Mean

Think about it
Four numbers have a mean of 10. That means their sum must be exactly what? If three of those numbers are 6, 9, and 15, what single number is missing — and how did you get there without guessing?
sum needed = mean × count, then subtract the known values
Why this makes sense
The mean formula is really just an equation with the total sum hidden inside. Reverse-engineer the total first — multiply mean by count — then treat "missing value" like any algebra problem: total minus what you already have.
Zone 1 · SHSAT-Favorite Twist #2Mandatory Drill

Which Measure Moves When One Extreme Value Changes?

Think about it
Data set: 4, 5, 6, 7, 50. Find the mean and the median. Now replace 50 with 8. Recompute both. Which measure barely moved, and which measure swung wildly? Why does the "level the stacks" idea explain this?
Mean is pulled by extremes — median mostly ignores them
Why this makes sense
Mean must redistribute the total, so one giant value drags the "level" way up. Median only cares about position in the sorted order — unless the outlier crosses the middle position entirely, median barely notices it moved.
Zone 1 · You-Do · Easy to Hard

Missing Value & Outlier — On Your Own

5Level 2
Five numbers have a mean of 12. Four of them are 8, 10, 14, and 16. What is the fifth number?
6Level 3
Julius has test scores 78, 82, 90, and 85. What score does he need on a fifth test to raise his mean to exactly 85?
7Level 3
Data set: 2, 3, 3, 4, 200. If the value 200 is removed entirely (not replaced), does the mean or the median change more? Compute both cases to prove your answer.
8Level 4
A set of 7 numbers has mean 20. One number, currently 6, is changed to 62. By how much does the new mean increase? (Hint: you don't need to recompute the whole sum from scratch — what's the shortcut?)
SHSAT Trap: Recomputing the Whole Sum From Scratch
When only one value changes, the fastest method is: new mean = old mean + (change in that one value) ÷ count. Redoing the entire sum wastes time you don't have under the Commit Clock.
ELA · Fiction Entry · 83% → 28%

Four Things to Mark — Nothing Else

Think about it
Fiction dropped from 83% to 28% between diagnostics — a much bigger collapse than any math domain. What does a huge swing like that usually mean: a content gap, or a missing reading habit that needs to be taught explicitly?
The four-mark annotation model
  • Who wants what — identify the character's goal in one phrase.
  • What blocks them — the obstacle, person, or fear standing in the way.
  • Where the tone shifts — find the exact sentence where the mood changes.
  • What dialogue reveals — something narration alone does not tell you.
Why Only Four Marks
Over-annotating buries the signal in noise. These four categories cover almost every SHSAT fiction question type — motivation, conflict, tone, and inference from dialogue — without wasting time marking everything.
ELA · Instructor Think-Aloud · First Half

"The Long Way Home" — Annotated Model

1Maren stood at the edge of the driveway, backpack straps digging into her shoulders, watching her father's truck disappear around the corner. [who wants what: Maren wants him to stay] She had asked him twice already — once at breakfast, once by the door — and both times he had looked past her, toward the highway.

2"He'll be back before the leaves turn," her grandmother said, not looking up from the dish she was drying. [what blocks Maren: grandmother's deflection, father's absence] Maren counted the trees along the road. Green. All of them, still green.

3"You said that in June," Maren said. Her voice came out smaller than she wanted. [tone shift begins: quiet frustration surfacing]
What I noticed while reading
Maren never says "I'm upset" — the narration shows it through the truck detail and counting trees. Her one line of dialogue ("You said that in June") reveals distrust that the narration alone hadn't stated outright. That's the dialogue-reveals-more move.

ELA · Compare & Confirm

Check Your Marks Against the Model

Where Julius should have marked
Tone shift: line 5, "something in the older woman's voice had changed." Dialogue reveal: line 6, "he keeps coming back" reveals the father's absence is not abandonment — a fact the narration never states directly.
SHSAT Trap: Confusing Tone Shift With Plot Event
A tone shift is a change in feeling, not necessarily a new event. The leaf turning orange at the very end is imagery signaling change — not the shift itself. Don't mistake symbolic detail for the emotional turn.
