Part E — ELA: Poem Structure

4 Questions · ~10 min

Read the poem excerpt once for understanding. Then answer: why did the poet make each structural choice? Structure isn't decoration — it builds meaning.

The Tide, Returning
Tool:
Color:

[st.1] The tide comes in.
The tide comes in.
It does not ask the shore
if the shore is ready.

[st.2] Every evening the same gray water
climbs the same worn stones,
erasing the footprints of children
who built and left, built and left,
never once staying to watch
what the sea would do to their castles.

[st.3] The tide comes in.
The tide comes in.
And somewhere a grandmother
counts the years like driftwood,
gathering what the water gives back,
throwing nothing away.

[st.4] Gone.
Quiet.
Still.

Q1. The line "The tide comes in" repeats at the start of stanzas 1 and 3. What is the most likely effect of this repetition on the reader? L2
Q2. In the final stanza, the poet switches from long, flowing lines to short, one-word lines ("Gone. / Quiet. / Still."). What effect does this shift most likely create? L3
Q3. Why does the poet likely include the detail about children who "built and left, built and left" their sandcastles? L3
Q4. A student claims: "Stanza 4 is shorter than the others just to save space on the page." Is this claim supported, partially supported, or contradicted by the rest of the poem? L4
