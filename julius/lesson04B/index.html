Tool 4 · Worked Strategy

Distribute Toward a Round Number

WorkedStrategy Model
47 × 19
What nearby number makes 19 easier to multiply? What correction restores the original expression?
1
Rename 19
19 = 20 − 1. The new expression is 47(20 − 1).
2
Distribute
47 × 20 − 47 × 1. The first product is now a tens fact.
3
Correct once
Subtract one copy: 940 − 47. Before finishing, predict: should the result be slightly below 940?
Why this is faster
The method trades a difficult two-digit multiplication for one easy product and one subtraction.

Tool 5 · Worked Strategy

Estimate to Eliminate

WorkedChoice Elimination
A theater sold 397 tickets at $18.75 each. Which total is reasonable?
A$744.38
B$3,176.00
C$7,443.75
D$74,437.50
1
Round compatibly
397 ≈ 400 and 18.75 ≈ 20.
2
Find the scale
400 × 20 = 8,000. The exact value should be somewhat below 8,000.
3
Eliminate by magnitude
Only one option is in the correct thousands range and below the estimate.

Tool 6 · Worked Strategy

The 5-Second Sanity Check

WorkedError Catch
A student computes 38% of 240 and gets 912.
1
Sign and direction
A positive percent of a positive number must be positive.
2
Magnitude
Since 38% is less than 100%, the result must be less than 240.
3
Repair the setup
Set up 0.38 × 240, or use 40% − 2%. Finish the subtraction, then compare with 240.

Fiction Passage · Part 1

The Unsent Signal

1Maya reached the roof before sunset, carrying a wooden box that knocked against her knee at every stair. Her grandfather had called it a heliograph, though to her it looked like a mirror trapped inside a brass spider.
5“Tilt it until the sun hits the mark,” he had said. “Then tap the lever. Light can travel farther than a shout.”
9Across the river, the old water tower stood on a hill. Her cousin Leo waited there with a white sheet. If he saw three short flashes, he would wave it. That was the plan, and Maya had repeated it all week as if repetition could keep weather obedient.
15But a bank of clouds had climbed over the western buildings. The mirror held only a dull square of sky. Maya tapped the lever anyway: click, click, click. Nothing crossed the river.
20She checked the hinge, polished the glass with her sleeve, and aimed again. The rooftops below glowed copper, but the water tower remained gray. Her watch read 6:17. Leo had promised to wait until 6:20.
Fiction Passage · Part 2

The Unsent Signal

24Maya’s first thought was to run downstairs and call him. That would prove the machine useless, exactly as Leo had predicted. Then she noticed the office windows across the street catching the last light—each pane briefly bright when the clouds thinned.
30She loosened the brass collar and turned the mirror away from the tower. One office window flashed. Then another. She adjusted the angle by the width of a fingernail, watching the reflections step across the glass building like notes on a scale.
36When the brightest reflection reached the river-facing corner, Maya swung the mirror back toward the hill. The sun itself was hidden, but the borrowed light struck the water tower.
41Click, click, click.
43For a moment, nothing moved. Then a small white square lifted against the dark hill and dipped three times.
47Maya did not cheer. She placed both hands on the warm brass box, as if steadying something that had finally learned to speak.
