Graph Literacy Routine

Read the Frame Before the Picture

A
Axes
What does each direction represent?
U
Units
People, dollars, minutes, percent—or thousands?
Q
Question
Difference, total, trend, median, or guarantee?
D
Data
Only now extract the needed points.
Why this order?
If you stare at the bars first, your brain starts answering a question nobody asked. The frame tells you what counts; the question tells you which pixels matter.

The Blue Boat

1Nia pressed the scraper beneath a blister of blue paint. It lifted in one perfect curl, revealing gray wood underneath. For a second she admired the clean strip; then the wind tossed the curl into the harbor.

4“There goes another piece of your prizewinner,” Mateo said from the dock. He had arrived carrying two paper cups and the expression he used when he expected to be ignored.

7“It will be a prizewinner when I’m done.” Nia did not look up. The Harbor Day parade began Saturday morning. Three days remained, and Grandfather’s skiff still looked like it had lost an argument with every winter since 1982.

11Mateo set one cup on the overturned bait bucket. “You know the judges don’t care who sands the boat.”

13“I care.”

14That was true, though not complete. Since Grandfather had moved inland, adults kept offering to “help with the old boat,” which usually meant deciding it should be sold. Nia had entered the parade before anyone could make that decision for her.

18She drove the scraper under another bubble. This one resisted. She leaned harder.

20“Careful,” Mateo said.

21The wood answered with a dry crack.

Original Fiction · Part 2 of 2

The Blue Boat Continued

22A narrow plank near the bow sagged inward. Nia froze, scraper still raised. The harbor sounds—the halyards tapping masts, the gulls bargaining over a crust—suddenly seemed far away.

25Mateo stepped into the skiff without asking. He crouched beside the split and ran one finger along it. “This was already soft,” he said. “You just found it before the judges did.”

29“That is an extremely cheerful description of a hole.”

31“It’s not a hole yet.” He pulled a pencil from behind his ear and marked a rectangle around the damaged wood. “We cut here, fit a new piece, seal the edges.”

35Nia watched him draw. Mateo had helped Grandfather patch the skiff last spring, while she had been busy designing its new sail. She had called his repairs boring. Now the neat pencil lines looked like a door.

39“I didn’t ask you to take over,” she said.

41Mateo handed her the pencil. “Good. I didn’t offer.”

43For the first time that afternoon, Nia laughed. She extended the rectangle by half an inch. “Your corner was crooked.”

46“Naturally.” Mateo picked up the second scraper. Together they worked until the damaged plank was bare. Under the fading light, the strip of exposed wood no longer resembled a failure. It looked like the first line of a plan.

Cheat Sheet
Reading the Picture
Graphs
A-U-Q-D
Axes → units → question → data. Read values only after you know what relationship is requested.
Frequency
Seats vs. Weight
Median uses ordered positions. Mean uses Σ(value × frequency) ÷ total frequency.
Box Plot
Five Landmarks
Minimum, Q1, median, Q3, maximum. IQR = Q3 − Q1. Each quartile holds about 25%.
Fiction
Hidden Engine
Who wants what? What blocks it? Where does the attitude shift? What does dialogue reveal indirectly?
