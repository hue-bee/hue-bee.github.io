Warm-Up · Quick Recall

Before We Move On: Prove It Stuck

Think first
On a number line, how far apart are -3 and 5? Don't subtract yet — picture the two points and count the spaces between them. Now: is distance ever negative?
Why this matters
Absolute value and factors/multiples both earned 0% on your last diagnostic — but neither is actually hard. They're both about noticing a pattern you already half-know and giving it a name and a rule.
Your Habit Target
Today isn't about memorizing "make it positive." It's about understanding *why* absolute value is always non-negative, and *why* every number breaks down into a unique set of primes. Say the reasoning out loud before you compute.
Zone 1 · Absolute Value Basics

Absolute Value Is Distance, Not a Sign Trick

Think about it
|7| means "how far is 7 from 0?" and |-7| means "how far is -7 from 0?" Are those two distances the same or different? What does that tell you about why |-7| = 7, not -7?
The definition — distance from zero, always non-negative
|x| = x if x ≥ 0    |x| = -x if x < 0
Zone 1 · Practice

Absolute Value Basics — Easy to Hard

1Level 1
Evaluate |-9| + |4|. Ask yourself: does the minus sign inside the bars survive?
2Level 2
Evaluate |3 - 8|. Do the subtraction inside the bars first, or take absolute value first — does order matter here?
3Level 3
Evaluate -|-6|. Careful — the negative sign is outside the bars. What does it do to your final answer?
4Level 4
If |x - 4| = 9, what are the two possible values of x? Think of it as two distances from 4, one in each direction.
SHSAT Trap: Simplifying Inside the Bars Too Early
Always do the arithmetic inside the absolute value bars completely before taking the absolute value. |3 - 8| is |-5| = 5, not |3| - |8| = -5.
Zone 2 · Equations & InequalitiesHigh-Frequency

One Absolute Value Equation Is Secretly Two Equations

Think about it
If |x| = 5, what two numbers could x be? Both are exactly 5 away from zero — one in each direction. Now, if |x - 2| = 5, what point is "5 away" measured from instead of zero?
|x| = n  →  x = n  or  x = -n  (n ≥ 0)
Inequalities flip the picture
|x| < n means x is inside a window: -n < x < n. |x| > n means x is outside a window: x < -n or x > n.
Zone 2 · Practice

Absolute Value Equations — Easy to Hard

5Level 1
Solve |x| = 12. How many solutions should you expect, and what are they?
6Level 3
Solve |2x + 1| = 7. Set up your two branch equations before isolating x in either one.
7. Level 4: Solve and graph |x + 3| ≤ 4. Which range describes all solutions?
SHSAT Trap: Forgetting the Negative Branch
The most common error is solving only the positive case and stopping. Every |expression| = n equation has two branches unless n = 0 — always write both before you finish.
Zone 3 · Factors & Prime Factorization

Every Number Is Built From Prime Ingredients

Think about it
A factor of 12 is any number that divides evenly into 12. List all the factors of 12. Now break 12 down into only prime numbers multiplied together — how many 2's and 3's do you need?
Key vocabulary
Factor = a number that divides evenly into another. Prime factorization = writing a number as a product of only prime numbers. Every whole number greater than 1 has exactly one prime factorization — that's why it's such a powerful tool.
Zone 3 · Practice

Factors & Prime Factorization — Easy to Hard

8Level 1
List all the factors of 24. How many are there? Try pairing them up from the outside in (1×24, 2×12...) so you don't miss any.
9Level 2
Write the prime factorization of 90 using a factor tree. Which prime do you divide out first — does it matter which one you pick?
10Level 3
Is 91 prime? Test it by dividing by small primes in order (2, 3, 5, 7...) — where can you stop checking?
11Level 4
If a number's prime factorization is 2³ × 5², how many total factors does it have? (Hint: add 1 to each exponent, then multiply.)
SHSAT Trap: Forgetting 1 and the Number Itself
Every whole number has itself and 1 as factors — it's easy to forget these when listing factor pairs. Always start your pair list with 1 × n and work inward.
Zone 4 · GCF & LCM — Stretch Goal

Two Questions, Same Prime Factorization

Think about it
12 = 2² × 3 and 18 = 2 × 3². What's the biggest set of primes that both numbers share? What's the smallest set of primes that could rebuild both numbers at once?
The rule
GCF (Greatest Common Factor): take the lowest power of each shared prime. LCM (Least Common Multiple): take the highest power of every prime that appears in either number.
GCF × LCM = the product of the two original numbers
Zone 4 · Practice

GCF & LCM — Easy to Hard

12Level 1
Find the GCF of 8 and 20. List factors of each first — which is the biggest one they share?
13Level 2
Find the LCM of 6 and 8 using prime factorization. What's the highest power of 2 you need? Of 3?
14Level 4
Two buses leave the station at the same time. Bus A returns every 12 minutes, Bus B returns every 18 minutes. In how many minutes will they next leave together? Which tool — GCF or LCM — answers a "when do they line up again" question?
SHSAT Trap: Mixing Up GCF and LCM Word Problems
"Cutting into equal pieces" or "grouping evenly" problems usually want GCF (splitting apart). "Happening again at the same time" or "lining up" problems usually want LCM (repeating together). Ask which direction the problem is going before picking a tool.
Zone 5 · Mixed Practice

Mixed Number Sense — Spot the Skill

Think about it
The SHSAT often blends these ideas in one question. Before solving, ask: "is this really about distance from zero, or is it really about how numbers divide into each other?"
15Level 4
Let n be the smallest positive integer such that |n - 15| is a multiple of both 4 and 6. What is n? Break this into two steps: what values make |n - 15| a common multiple, then check which n is smallest and positive.
SHSAT Trap: Solving the Wrong Sub-Problem First
Multi-skill problems reward slowing down before writing anything. Circle the exact quantity the question asks for last — it's easy to solve for a middle step and stop there.
Zone 5 · Accuracy

Three Ways to Check a Number Sense Answer

Worked example: |2x + 1| = 7 → x = 3 or x = -4
CheckBefore You Start
Sanity-Check the Sign
Is your final absolute value answer non-negative? If you ever land on a negative number sitting alone outside bars that should be inside, you made a sign error.
CheckAfter You Finish
Plug Both Solutions Back In
Substitute both x = 3 and x = -4 into |2x + 1|: both should give exactly 7. If one doesn't, redo that branch.
CheckIf Time Allows
Re-Factor From Scratch
For factor/multiple problems, rebuild the prime factorization on a blank line without looking at your first attempt. Matching results twice is strong proof.
SHSAT Trap: Confusing Factors With Multiples
Factors of a number are smaller than or equal to it (they divide in). Multiples of a number are equal to or bigger than it (you multiply out). Before you answer, ask: "am I shrinking down or building up?"
ELA · Inference

Inference Is a Bridge, Not a Guess

Think about it
If a passage says "she packed her bags at midnight and left without saying goodbye," what can you conclude about how she felt — even though the text never states an emotion directly? What evidence supports your conclusion?
The inference sentence frame
  • "The text says ___, which means ___."
  • A stated detail question can be answered by pointing at the text.
  • An inference question needs one logical step beyond what's written.
  • Julius scored 63% on inference in Diag. A — down from 83% on Form 1. Today rebuilds consistency.
ELA · Practice

Stated Detail or Inference?

16Level 2
A passage states: "By 1920, the factory employed three times as many workers as it had in 1900." Is the statement "the factory grew" a stated detail or an inference?
17Level 3
A character "counted her coins twice before buying the bread." What can you reasonably infer about her financial situation, and what one phrase is your evidence?
SHSAT Trap: Choosing an Inference That's Too Big a Leap
A correct inference is one small logical step from the text — not a wild guess. If an answer choice requires outside knowledge the passage never hints at, it's a trap, not an inference.
ELA · Practice, Continued

Two More Inference Checks

18Level 1
A passage says: "He wore his winter coat, even though it was only September." What can you infer about the weather or the character's habits? Point to the exact phrase that supports your answer.
19Level 4
A scientist "double-checked her data three times and asked a colleague to review it before publishing." Which is the stronger inference: she is meticulous, or she is uncertain about her results? Defend your choice using the specific details given — not just a gut feeling.
Habit Check
Before locking in an inference answer, ask: "could I point to the exact sentence that supports this?" If not, it's probably too big a leap — go back and find your evidence first.
