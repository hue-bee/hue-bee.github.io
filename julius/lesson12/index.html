Warm-Up · Quick Recall

Before We Move On: Prove It Stuck

Think first
If you're given x + 5 = 12 and four answer choices for x, is it faster to solve the equation algebraically, or to try each answer choice in the equation? What does "plug in" actually mean?
Why this matters
Plug-Ins earned 0% on your last diagnostic — but it's really the laziest, safest way to answer a multiple-choice algebra question: test real numbers instead of manipulating variables. Today, editing skills and algebra both get the same fix: stop guessing, start testing/checking systematically.
Your Habit Target
Today isn't about memorizing a checklist. It's about asking one clarifying question before you act: for ELA, "what relationship or job does this sentence need to do?" For math, "which answer choice, plugged in, makes both sides true?" Say the reasoning out loud before you commit.
Zone 1 · TransitionsHigh-Frequency

A Transition Is a Signpost for the Reader's Brain

Think about it
Read: "The lab was cancelled. ___, the students still submitted their reports." Does the second sentence agree, disagree, add a surprise, or give an example? What single word signals "surprise despite the first sentence"?
Match the relationship, THEN the word
Add-on: also, furthermore  ·  Contrast: however, although  ·  Cause: therefore, as a result  ·  Example: for instance
Zone 1 · Practice

Transitions — Easy to Hard

1Level 1
"Maria loves painting. ___, she spends every weekend in the art studio." Does the second sentence add supporting proof, or contradict the first? Which word fits: However or In fact?
2Level 2
"The bridge was under repair. ___, drivers had to take a ten-mile detour." Is the second sentence a RESULT of the first, or an EXAMPLE of it? What word do you need?
3Level 3
"Many scientists believed the theory was flawed. ___, new evidence eventually proved them wrong." Careful — is this agreement, or a reversal across time? Which transition captures a turn against expectation?
4Level 4
A paragraph moves: cause sentence → effect sentence → a sentence that seems to both restate the cause AND add a new contrasting idea. Which transition word would need to do "double duty," and is a single transition word ever enough — or does the sentence need restructuring instead?
SHSAT Trap: Sound-Right Isn't Logic-Right
"Also" and "however" can both sound fine in a sentence, but only one matches the actual logical relationship. Always name the relationship first (agree/add, contrast, cause/effect, example) — THEN pick the word that matches it.
Zone 2 · Topic Sentence Revision

A Topic Sentence Is a Promise the Paragraph Must Keep

Think about it
A paragraph's topic sentence says "Dogs make excellent pets," but every sentence after it is actually about cat behavior. Is the fix to rewrite all the cat sentences, or to rewrite the promise (topic sentence) so it matches what the paragraph actually delivers?
The two-question test
1. What is every supporting sentence actually about? 2. Does the topic sentence's promise match that shared subject exactly — not too broad, not too narrow? Revise the topic sentence to fit the evidence, not the other way around.
Zone 2 · Practice

Topic Sentence Revision — Easy to Hard

5Level 1
Topic sentence: "Basketball is the most popular sport." Every supporting sentence discusses only soccer's global fan base. What single word in the topic sentence needs to change?
6Level 2
Topic sentence: "Recycling helps the planet." The paragraph only discusses how recycling saves cities money on landfill costs. Is "helps the planet" too broad or too narrow for this evidence — and what's a tighter promise?
7Level 3
Topic sentence: "The trip was fun." Supporting sentences describe rain delays, a lost suitcase, AND one great beach afternoon. Does "fun" match ALL the evidence, or only part of it — what promise would actually fit every sentence?
SHSAT Trap: Fixing the Wrong Half
When a topic sentence doesn't match its paragraph, students often try to rewrite every supporting sentence. It's almost always faster — and it's what the SHSAT is testing — to revise the ONE topic sentence to match the evidence that's already there.
Zone 3 · Eliminating Off-Topic Sentences

Every Sentence Has One Job: Prove the Topic Sentence

Think about it
A paragraph about "why bees are essential to farming" includes one sentence about a beekeeper's favorite hat color. Does that sentence prove bees are essential to farming? What test can you run on ANY sentence to check if it belongs?
The one-question filter
For each sentence, ask: "does this directly support or explain the topic sentence's promise?" If the honest answer is no — even if the sentence is interesting or true — it's off-topic and gets cut.
Zone 3 · Practice

Off-Topic Sentences — Easy to Hard

8Level 1
Topic: "Volcanoes reshape landscapes." Sentence: "Some volcanoes are found underwater." Does this prove HOW volcanoes reshape landscapes, or is it a side fact? Keep or cut?
9Level 2
Topic: "The chef's new menu focuses on local vegetables." Which sentence should be cut: (A) "Carrots come from a farm ten miles away," or (B) "The chef once worked on a cruise ship"? Justify using the one-question filter.
10Level 3
A paragraph about "why the library extended its hours" includes: "The library was built in 1962." Is this sentence off-topic, or could it be background context that still needs cutting? Defend your call.
SHSAT Trap: "True and Interesting" Isn't the Same as "On-Topic"
Test writers often insert a factually true, mildly interesting sentence that simply doesn't prove the topic sentence's specific promise. True and interesting is not the bar — directly supporting the topic is the bar.
ELA · Wrap-Up Check

Name the Skill Before You Answer

Think about it
The SHSAT often mixes transitions, topic-sentence fit, and off-topic sentences within a single revising/editing passage. Before touching any answer choice, ask: "is this question about the LOGIC between two sentences, the PROMISE of a paragraph, or whether a sentence BELONGS at all?"

Revising/editing questions look similar at a glance but test three different logical moves. Slow down on the question stem — it always tells you which of the three jobs you're doing.
Zone 4 · Plug-In Strategy0% Zone — Rebuild

Don't Solve It — Test the Answer Choices

Think about it
You're given 2x + 3 = 11 with choices A) 3 B) 4 C) 5 D) 6. Instead of solving for x algebraically, what happens if you substitute each choice for x and check which one makes the equation TRUE? Which one works?
The Plug-In Method, in 3 steps
1. Start with choice C (middle)   2. Substitute & check both sides   3. Too big/small? Adjust direction, retest
Why this is the clever way
On multiple choice, the correct number is ALWAYS sitting right in front of you. Plugging in trades "can I do the algebra correctly" for "can I do arithmetic correctly" — and arithmetic is much harder to mess up under time pressure.
Zone 4 · Practice, Set 1

Plug-Ins — Easy to Hard, Part 1

11Level 1
3x - 4 = 11. Choices: A) 3   B) 5   C) 7   D) 9. Start at C — plug in 7. Is 3(7)-4 too big, too small, or exactly 11?
12Level 1
If n + 9 = 2n, what is n? Choices: A) 6   B) 7   C) 9   D) 12. Plug each choice into BOTH sides — which value makes them equal?
13Level 2
A number increased by 6 equals twice the number decreased by 3. Choices for the number: A) 7   B) 8   C) 9   D) 10. Translate into an equation first, then plug in from C. Which side ends up bigger with 9 — and which direction should you move?
14Level 2
Julius is 4 years older than his sister. In 3 years, he will be twice as old as she is now. Choices for his sister's CURRENT age: A) 1   B) 3   C) 5   D) 7. Plug in an age for the sister, then build Julius's age from the clue — does the "in 3 years" condition hold?
SHSAT Trap: Plugging In Only Half the Equation
Always substitute into BOTH sides of the equation (or every condition in a word problem) before declaring a match. A choice that satisfies only one clue and not the other is a trap answer.
Zone 4 · Practice, Set 2

Plug-Ins — Easy to Hard, Part 2

15Level 3
x² - 2x = 15. Choices: A) 3   B) 5   C) 7   D) 9. Plug in from C: is 7²-2(7) too big or too small compared to 15? Which direction do you move next?
16Level 3
The product of two consecutive integers is 56. Choices for the smaller integer: A) 5   B) 6   C) 7   D) 8. Plug in, multiply the number by the very next integer — does it land exactly on 56?
17Level 4
A rectangle's length is 3 more than twice its width, and its area is 65. Choices for the width: A) 4   B) 5   C) 6   D) 7. Plug in a width, build the length from the clue, then multiply for area — which width makes area = 65 exactly?
18Level 4
x² + x = 30, and the answer must satisfy the equation AND be positive. Choices: A) −6   B) 5   C) 6   D) 30. Two choices might satisfy the equation algebraically — which extra condition in the problem eliminates one of them?
SHSAT Trap: Quadratics Can Have Two "Correct" Numbers
Squared terms often produce two values that technically satisfy the equation (one positive, one negative). Always re-check the problem's wording — words like "positive," "a length," or "an age" eliminate the impossible option.
