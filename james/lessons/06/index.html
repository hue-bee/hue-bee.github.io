Warm-Up · Sprint

Before We Move On: Which Way Do the Units Point?

Think first
If 1 mile = 5,280 feet, and you're told a distance in feet but need it in miles, are you working with a NUMBER BIGGER than 5,280, or smaller than 1? Which operation gets you there — multiply or divide?
Why this matters
Every conversion problem is really the same question in disguise: am I moving from a small unit to a big one, or a big one to a small one? Get the direction right and the arithmetic is trivial — get it backwards and every answer choice looks tempting.
Your Habit Target
Before touching a calculator: write the conversion as a fraction with units labeled on top and bottom. Ask "does the unit I don't want cancel out?" If yes, you're set up correctly. If no, flip the fraction.
Zone 1 · Conversion DirectionHigh-Frequency

The Units-Cancel Check Is Your Compass

Think about it
On planet Zorb, 1 sind = 0.75 plunks. You have 8 plunks and need sinds. Do you multiply 8 × 0.75, or divide 8 ÷ 0.75? Write "8 plunks × (1 sind / 0.75 plunks)" — which unit cancels?
The move
8 plunks ÷ 0.75 = 10.67 sinds
Why this is the clever way
8 × 0.75 = 6.00 is sitting right there as an answer choice — it's the "Wrong Direction" trap. The units-cancel check removes the guesswork permanently: write the unit fraction, confirm the unwanted unit cancels, and the correct operation falls out automatically. No memorizing "multiply when going small to big."
Tutor Move
Tutor-plays-student: solve it as 8 × 0.75 = 6.00 out loud and let James catch the error by checking which unit actually cancels. Name the trap together: "Wrong Direction."

2Level 2

3Level 3

1 nib = 1.6 corks, and 1 cork = 0.5 dashes. You have 12 nibs and need the answer in dashes. Chain two unit fractions together — which units need to cancel in the middle?
SHSAT Trap: Wrong Direction
Multiplying when you should divide (or vice versa) is always an offered answer choice — test writers know it's the instinctive mistake. The units-cancel check is mechanical and fast; it removes the guesswork permanently.
Zone 2 · Unit Rates & Fractions

Rebuild the Whole Before You Divide

Think about it
Michael earned $213 after $32 was taken out for tax, over a 20-hour week. If the question asks his pay rate BEFORE tax, do you divide 213 by 20 first, or do you need to do something to the 213 first?
The two-question test
1. Is the number I have the FULL amount the question wants, or a piece of it? 2. If it's a piece, add back what was removed (tax, tip, fee) BEFORE dividing by hours or units. (213 + 32) ÷ 20 = $12.25 per hour.
A second clever move: dividing by a small fraction
40 ÷ ⅓ looks like it should shrink, but 40 ÷ (1/5) = 200. Dividing by a fraction less than 1 always makes the result BIGGER, because you're asking "how many fifths fit into 40" — and fifths are small, so a lot of them fit.

If tax, tips, or fees were already removed, dividing the number you're given by hours or units gives you the AFTER-deduction rate — a trap answer choice. Rebuild the full amount first.
Zone 3 · Similar Figures

Perimeter Scales Once, Area Scales Twice

Think about it
Two similar triangles have a side ratio of 1:3. If the small triangle's perimeter is 12, is the big one's perimeter 36, or something else? What about area — does it also just multiply by 3?
Two different rules, same ratio k
Perimeter ratio = k  ·  Area ratio = k²
Why area gets squared
Perimeter is a length — one dimension — so it scales by k exactly once. Area is length × length — two dimensions — so BOTH dimensions get multiplied by k, giving k × k = k². Picture tiling the big triangle with copies of the small one: a 1:3 side ratio means 9 small triangles fit inside, not 3.
Zone 3 · Practice

Similar Figures — Easy to Hard

9Level 1
Two similar rectangles have a side ratio of 1:4. If the small one has area 6, what is the big one's area? Which power of 4 do you need?
10Level 2
Similar triangles A and B have perimeters 15 and 45. What is the side ratio, and if triangle A's area is 10, what is triangle B's area?
11Level 3
Two similar pentagons have areas 8 and 72. What is the ratio of their perimeters? (Work backwards: find k from the area ratio first.)
12Level 4
A small similar model of a park has a fountain with area 3 square feet; the real fountain's area is 300 times bigger. What linear scale factor was used to build the model, and how many times longer is a real path than the same path on the model?
SHSAT Trap: Using One Ratio for Both
The most common wrong answer multiplies area by the side ratio (k) instead of k² — or multiplies perimeter by k² by mistake. State which quantity you're scaling BEFORE you pick the exponent.
Zone 4 · Mixed Word ProblemsFinal-Line Check

Circle the Exact Thing the Question Wants

Think about it
Claire has run 650 meters of a 2,500-meter race and each lap is 120 meters. The question asks how many MORE complete laps she needs to run AT LEAST the remaining distance. After you compute 1,850 ÷ 120 = 15.42, do you round up or down — and why does "at least" decide that?
The rounding rule
"At least" / "needed" → round UP  ·  "How many complete" → round DOWN
Tutor Move

Solving the equation correctly isn't the same as answering what was asked. A ratio, a rate, and a lap count are three different "final lines" — circle which one the stem actually wants before you select a choice.
