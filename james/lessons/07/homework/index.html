A Cooler Route to School

[1] On hot September afternoons, students walking home from Harbor Middle School often avoided Linden Avenue. [2] The wide sidewalk offered little shade, and its dark pavement released heat long after dismissal. [3] A student environmental club decided to investigate whether a different walking route could keep students cooler.

[4] First, club members selected three routes of similar length. [5] On five clear afternoons, they recorded the air temperature and the pavement temperature at four points along each route. [6] They also counted trees and noted where buildings cast shadows. [7] The club's advisor owns a bicycle with bright yellow handlebars.

[8] The measurements revealed a pattern. [9] Although the Oak Street route was only one block longer than Linden Avenue, its average pavement temperature was six degrees lower. [10] In addition, trees or building shadows covered nearly two-thirds of its sidewalk by 3:30 p.m. [11] Therefore, the club recommended Oak Street as the school's “cool route.”

[12] The club then created a map showing water fountains and shaded rest points along Oak Street. [13] School leaders posted the map near the main exit and shared it with families. [14] The project showed that a useful recommendation can begin with careful local measurements.
