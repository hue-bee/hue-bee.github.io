Tool 1 · Proportions

A Proportion Is a Two-Sided Copy Machine

What stays synchronized?
If 3 notebooks cost $12, and the notebook count is multiplied by the same factor, what must happen to the cost? Why would changing only one side break the relationship?
quantity A / quantity B = matching A / matching B
Clever check: the diagonal handshake
Cross-products should agree because each diagonal contains one value from each matching pair. If they disagree, the columns were mismatched.
Tool 2 · Conversions

Units Are Train Tickets

Choose direction with labels
You have miles and want feet. Which form of “5,280 feet = 1 mile” places miles opposite miles so they cancel? What label survives?
starting amount × conversion factor = destination unit
Named trap: Wrong Direction
Do not ask “multiply or divide?” first. Ask “Which unit must disappear?” The arithmetic direction then chooses itself.
Tool 3 · Unit Rates

Find the One-Unit Bridge

Why pass through one?
If a worker earns $147 for 12 hours, why is “dollars for 1 hour” a useful bridge to any other number of hours?
The one-unit bridge
Divide both quantities by the same number to reach one unit. Then multiply both by the scale needed for the destination.
Label before number
Write “dollars per hour,” not just a decimal. A naked number cannot tell you whether the division went in the correct direction.
Tool 4 · Interpretation

The Noun Chooses How You Round

Can the object be split?
A calculation produces 15.42. If the answer represents laps needed, seats needed, or buses needed, what does the leftover 0.42 force you to do? What changes if it represents complete packages made?
Needed / at least
Climb to the next whole object.
Complete / full
Keep only finished groups.
Measured amount
Use the requested precision.

ELA · Organization

A Paragraph Is a Small Machine

Every sentence needs a job
If the paragraph’s claim is the machine’s purpose, which sentence starts it, which sentences supply parts or evidence, and which sentence closes the casing?
1
Topic sentence: announces the paragraph’s controlling idea.
2
Support: develops that exact idea with reasons, facts, or examples.
3
Link: shows how one idea relates to the next.
4
Closing: completes the point or connects it to the larger claim.
ELA · Transitions

Transitions Are Road Signs, Not Decorations

Name the relationship first
Sentence A makes one claim. Sentence B adds, contrasts, causes, or illustrates. Which relationship exists before you inspect any transition choices?
Addition
another supporting idea
Contrast
turns against expectation
Cause/Result
one idea produces another
Example
specific case proves a general point
Trap: fancy but illogical
A transition may sound polished and still point in the wrong logical direction. Test the relationship, not the vocabulary level.
ELA · Conclusions

Use an Umbrella, Not a Microscope

How wide should the ending be?
If a passage discusses cost, access, and environmental effects, should the conclusion mention only the final detail about cost? What idea can cover all three?
Three tests for a conclusion
1
Matches the passage’s overall claim.
2
Adds no brand-new evidence or topic.
3
Feels broad enough to close, not narrow enough to continue.
