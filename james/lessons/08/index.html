Math · Pooling Groups

Fractions Describe Groups; Objects Merge

A question to settle first
If a tiny jar is almost all yellow and a huge jar is barely yellow, should the two jars have equal influence after they are poured together? What should determine the influence instead?
Reliable route
probability → count → pool → probability
Trap: Don’t Average
Averaging two probabilities silently pretends the groups are the same size. Convert to actual object counts first.
Worked Model 1 · Three Steps

Merge Two Jars Without Averaging

Situation

Jar Q holds 12 counters, and one-third are yellow. Jar R holds 8 counters, and three-fourths are yellow. Everything is poured into Jar S.

Predict first
Which original jar contributes more yellow counters? Which jar contributes more total counters? Why do those two facts both matter?
1
Translate each fraction into objects.
12 × 1/3 = 4 yellow; 8 × 3/4 = 6 yellow.
2
Pool matching counts.
Yellow: 4 + 6 = 10. Total: 12 + 8 = 20.
3
Re-form one probability.
10/20 = 1/2. The denominator is the entire new jar.
Why the shortcut fails
The mean of one-third and three-fourths treats 12 counters and 8 counters as equally large groups. They are not.

Math · Without Replacement

After Each Draw, Take a New Photograph

Freeze the scene
A red tile is drawn and not returned. In the next photograph, how many red tiles remain? How many total tiles remain? Which numbers must change together?
Sequence rule
P(A then B) = P(A) × P(B after A)
The frozen-denominator trap
Without replacement, the total shrinks every time. If a favorable object was removed, the favorable count shrinks too.
Worked Model 2 · Three Steps

Four Specific Draws Without Replacement

Situation

A bag holds 4 black and 5 white beads. Four beads are drawn without replacement. Find the probability that all four drawn beads are black.

1
Write one fraction per draw.
4/9, 3/8, 2/7, 1/6. Recount after each black bead leaves.
2
Multiply because the word is “and.”
(4/9)(3/8)(2/7)(1/6).
3
Simplify across factors.
The product reduces to 1/126. A rare sequence should produce a small probability.
Reasonableness lens
Each successful draw makes the next black draw less likely, so the running product must keep shrinking.

First Read · Story

Poem: “After the Storm”

The rain unthreads the avenue,
then leaves each railing bright.
I walk the blocks we used to know
and count the windows’ light.

A tailor moon above the roofs
draws silver through the seams;
the city, torn by wind and dark,
is stitched again with gleams.
Literal retelling only
Where is the speaker? What has just ended? What does the speaker observe changing from the first stanza to the second?
Do not decode yet
On the first read, resist explaining the moon, seams, or stitching. Build the event sequence first; symbols need a literal floor to stand on.
Original instructional poem created for this lesson
Second Read · Voice

Speaker, Addressee, and Tone Shift

Speaker is not automatically the poet
What can the words prove about the voice inside this poem? Which personal detail is stated, and which biography would be invented?
Addressee test
Look for “you,” a name, a command, or a question. If none appears, the speaker may be thinking privately rather than addressing a named person.
Third Read · Shape

Line Breaks Are Invisible Punctuation

Enjambment
A sentence runs past the end of a line without a full stop.
What changes when the line breaks?
Read “The rain unthreads the avenue, / then leaves each railing bright.” Pause once at the comma, then read straight through. Which reading better matches the grammar? What word gains attention by starting line 2?
Trap: treating every line as a sentence
Poetry lines are visual units, not guaranteed grammatical units. Follow punctuation first; then ask what the visual break emphasizes.
Worked Model 3 · Figurative Comparison

Cross the Shared-Quality Bridge

1
List concrete qualities of the image.
Sewing joins, repairs, binds, and proceeds stitch by stitch.
2
Test those qualities against the subject.
Scattered lights visually reconnect a city divided by darkness and storm.
3
State the effect in plain language.
The extended metaphor frames returning light as gradual repair and renewal.
Elimination test
Any choice focused on literal tailoring misses the bridge. The correct interpretation must fit both sides of the comparison.
