Sprint · Cold Start

Convert Fast: Fraction, Decimal, Percent

Think first
A percent is just "out of 100." So 0.5% — is that half of one percent, or fifty percent? Say the fraction out loud before you write anything.
Timed — 5 minutes, log your time
1/2  ·  1/4  ·  3/8  ·  1/200  ·  0.5%  ·  0.3%  ·  99.7%
Zone 1 · Reverse Percent

One Template Solves Almost Every "Find the Original" Problem

Think about it
If a price goes UP 5%, you multiply the old price by 1.05 to get the new price. So if you're given the NEW price and asked for the OLD one, what operation undoes multiplication?
The only template you need this week
new = old × (1 ± r)   ➞   old = new ÷ (1 ± r)
Zone 1 · Practice

Reverse Percent — Easy to Hard

1Level 1
After a 20% increase, a number becomes 60. What was the original number? Which factor does "increase" give you — 1.20 or 0.80?
2Level 2
A shirt's price dropped 15% to $42.50. What was the original price? Watch the sign on r before you divide.
3Level 3
Tuesday's temperature was 84°, which is 5% higher than Monday's. What was Monday's temperature? Set up 84 = 1.05x first — do not skip the equation.
4Level 4
A price of $63 already includes a 10% markup over the wholesale cost. Find the wholesale cost, then find the price after a separate 10% discount off that wholesale cost. Two reversals, one problem.
Zone 2 · The TrapHighest-Yield

Solving Correctly Is Only Half the Job

Work it with me
Wednesday's temperature, 84°, is 5% higher than Tuesday's. We solve: 84 = 1.05x, so x = 80. Now stop — before you circle 80, reread the question. Does it ask for Tuesday's temperature, or something else?
Trap name: "Right Math, Wrong Line"
If the actual question is "how much lower was Tuesday than Wednesday," the answer is 84 − 80 = 8° lower — a difference, not the value 80. The arithmetic was perfect. The wrong choice is offered on purpose.
Zone 2 · Practice

Value or Difference? Read the Last Line Twice

5Level 1
A club had 25 members. Membership grew 20%. How many members does the club have now? First: is this asking for the new value, or a change?
6Level 3
Monday's attendance was 150 people. Tuesday's attendance was 10% lower. How much lower, in people, was Tuesday than Monday — not what Tuesday's attendance was. Solve, then circle the actual target before answering.
Habit to build today
After every percent problem, before selecting an answer, say out loud: "the question wants ___." If you can't finish that sentence in one word — value or difference — go back and reread the last line.
Zone 3 · Complement Percent

What's Missing From 100%?

Think about it
"99.7% of customers were satisfied." Everyone jumps to 99.7 as the important number. But if a question asks about the customers who were NOT satisfied, what percent is that — and what fraction does it reduce to?
100% − 99.7% = 0.3% = 3/1000
Zone 3 · Practice

Complement Percent — Easy to Hard

7Level 1
80% of a class passed a quiz. What fraction did NOT pass? Start from 100% first.
8Level 3
99.7% of parts passed inspection. What fraction of parts did NOT pass? Watch for these answer choices: 3/100 and 3/10 — both are traps built from misplacing the decimal.
SHSAT Trap: Decimal Drift
0.3% is not 3/100 (that's 3%) and not 3/10 (that's 30%). Convert to a decimal first — 0.003 — then count the zeroes into the denominator: 3/1000. Both wrong choices are always sitting right there waiting for a rushed decimal move.
Zone 3 · Timed Finish

Mixed Reverse & Complement — Last 3 at 75 Seconds

A number increased by 8% is 108. What was the original number?
10Level 3 · 75s
99.5% of a shipment arrived undamaged. What fraction arrived damaged? Two-minute cap applies — if you pass 75 seconds, flag it aloud and move on.
11Level 4 · 75s
Friday's sales were 12% below Thursday's, and Thursday's were $250. How much MORE did Thursday sell than Friday, in dollars? Circle: value, or difference?

ELA · Function of a Detail

A Purpose Answer Names a Job, Not a Summary

Think about it
A sentence says "The bridge, completed in 1932, spans 600 feet." A summary would restate that fact. But what is that sentence's JOB in the passage — is it there to establish scale, to set a time period, or to introduce a person? A job is a verb, not a fact.
Trap name: "True But Useless"
One answer choice will accurately restate the sentence's content. It will be completely true — and completely wrong, because the question asked what the sentence DOES, not what it SAYS.
ELA · Worked Example

Cover the Choices. Say the Job First.

The passage quotes a Scottish traveler describing Lowell
Cover the answer choices with your hand. Read only the quotation and the sentences around it. Out loud: what is this quotation doing in the passage? Not what it says about Lowell — what job it performs for the author's argument.
The job, once you say it
Quoting an outside visitor — especially a foreign one — is a classic move to show that a place's reputation reached beyond its own community. The job here is establishing that Lowell had a national, even international, reputation. Only after saying that job in your own words do you reveal the choices and look for the one that matches it.
Procedure — non-negotiable this week
Cover choices → reread the sentence in context → say the job out loud → reveal choices → match. Never reverse this order, even under time pressure.
ELA · Practice

Purpose Questions — Choices Stay Covered

13Level 1
A passage opens with a short anecdote about a single factory worker before discussing the broader industry. What job does that opening anecdote perform? Say it in one phrase before looking at any choices.
14Level 2
A paragraph lists three statistics about population growth right after a claim that a city was "booming." What job do those statistics do for the claim just made?
15Level 3
A sentence describes a mill town as having "leather-headedness — ignorance and idiocy." Is the job here to give a neutral fact, or to reveal the author's attitude? Which choice will be true but useless?
16Level 4
A closing sentence states that "his life, like his city's history, seemed to travel toward one ending." What job does this comparison do at the end of a biographical passage? Name the job before considering what the sentence literally claims.
Recap · Two New Traps

Named Today — Say Them From Memory

Right Math, Wrong Line — Math
You solved the equation correctly, then answered a different question than the one asked. The fix: circle the exact noun and unit the final line wants, every time, before selecting.
True But Useless — ELA
You picked the choice that accurately restates the sentence's content instead of the one that names its function. The fix: cover the choices, say the job out loud, then look.
Why name traps at all
A named trap gets spotted mid-problem. An abstract warning ("be careful") does not. From today forward, when you catch yourself about to fall into one, say its name out loud — that's the whole defense.

Check Your Work

Before You Start
Circle the Target Noun
Is the question asking for a value (a price, a temperature) or a difference (how much more/less)? Circle it before you touch the numbers.
After You Finish
Sanity-Check the Direction
If the quantity increased, your original should be SMALLER than the new value. If it decreased, the original should be LARGER. If not, you divided by the wrong factor.
If Time Allows
Say the Job Out Loud (ELA)
Before picking a purpose answer, finish the sentence: "this sentence's job is to ___." If you can't finish it in one phrase, reread the surrounding lines.
