Zone 1 · The Flip Rule

Why Does the Sign Flip? — See It, Don't Memorize It

Think about it
You know 2 < 5 is true. Multiply both sides by −1: is −2 < −5, or is −2 > −5? Look at a number line — which of −2 and −5 is actually farther right? What does that tell you about what negation does to order?
The rule, once you've seen it
2 < 5  ×(−1)→  −2 > −5

One-Step Inequalities — Easy to Hard

1Level 1
Solve: x − 6 > 2. Does this one flip? Draw the number line — open or closed circle at your boundary?
2Level 2

3Level 3

Zone 2 · Compound InequalitiesNew Skill

Three Parts, One Move at a Time — The Mirror

Think about it
−5 ≤ 1 − 3x ≤ 4 is really two inequalities glued together sharing the middle term. Whatever you do to the middle, you must do to BOTH outer numbers. Subtract 1 from all three parts — what do you get? Then divide all three by −3. What has to happen to the direction of BOTH inequality signs?
The Mirror
After dividing by −3, the mirror-image number line — the one you'd get by flipping only ONE sign, or none — is always sitting there as an offered wrong choice. Flip both signs, or flip neither. Never just one.
Zone 2 · Practice — Number Line on Every One

Compound Inequalities — Same Operation, All Three Parts

5Level 1
Solve and plot: −2 < x + 3 < 5. No negative multiplication here — does either sign flip?
6Level 2
Solve and plot: 4 ≤ 2x ≤ 10. Divide all three parts by 2 — positive, so the signs stay put. Closed or open circles?
7Level 3
Solve and plot: 3 < −2x + 1 < 9. Subtract 1 from all three parts, then divide by −2. Say "flip both" before you write the new signs.
8Level 4
Solve and plot: −7 ≤ 5 − 4x < 1. Notice the two ends use different symbols (≤ vs <) — after the flip, which end gets the closed circle now?
The Mirror, restated
If your final interval "looks backwards" compared to your intuition — like 5 ≤ x ≤ 2 — you flipped only one sign, or you flipped when you shouldn't have. Both boundary numbers must still make sense with the smaller one on the left.
Zone 3 · Number-Line TEI MechanicsMotor Skill

Closed vs. Open — Plot It Right, or It Scores Zero

Think about it
"x ≤ 2" includes 2 itself. "x < 2" does not. If a number-line plotting question is scored all-or-nothing, what physically has to look different about the dot at 2 in each case — and which direction does the shading or arrow go?
The mechanical rule
≤ or ≥ (equal allowed) → filled/closed circle. < or > (strict) → open circle. Shade or draw the ray/segment toward every value that satisfies the inequality — never toward the excluded side.

Zone 4 · Catch the TutorHighest-Yield Drill

Watch the Tutor Solve This — Then Fix It

The tutor's work (on purpose, wrong)
"Solve −4 ≤ 2 − 3x ≤ 11. Subtract 2 from all three: −6 ≤ −3x ≤ 9. Divide by −3: 2 ≤ x ≤ −3." Look at that final answer — 2 ≤ x ≤ −3 says x is bigger than 2 AND smaller than −3 at the same time. Is that even possible? What actually went wrong?
The fix, once you say it
Dividing by −3 flips BOTH signs — the tutor flipped neither. Correct: −6 ≤ −3x ≤ 9 → divide by −3 and flip → 2 ≥ x ≥ −3, rewritten in order as −3 ≤ x ≤ 2. An impossible-looking answer like 2 ≤ x ≤ −3 is always a sign that a flip got skipped.

Recap · Today's Guardrails

One Rule, One Motor Skill

The Mirror — Math
Multiplying or dividing an inequality by a negative flips the sign — in a compound inequality, it flips BOTH signs, every single time. The mirror-image (unflipped) answer is always an offered choice.
Circle Type — TEI Mechanics
≤ / ≥ → closed circle (equal is allowed). < / > → open circle (strict). All-or-nothing scoring means the right interval with the wrong circle still scores zero.
The self-check that catches almost every flip error
If your final compound answer reads like "bigger number ≤ x ≤ smaller number," it's impossible — you skipped a flip somewhere. Rewrite with the smaller boundary on the left before you plot anything.

Before You Start
Is a Negative Multiplying or Dividing x?
Addition/subtraction never flips anything. Multiplying/dividing by a negative always flips — in compound inequalities, flip BOTH signs at once.
Before You Plot
Closed or Open?
≤ / ≥ → closed (filled) circle. < / > → open circle. Shade toward every value that works, never toward the excluded side.
Self-Check
Does the Answer Even Make Sense?
"Bigger ≤ x ≤ smaller" is impossible. If you see that, a flip was skipped — go back and rewrite with the smaller boundary on the left.
The Mirror
The unflipped, mirror-image number line is always an offered wrong choice. Flip both signs, or flip neither — never just one.
