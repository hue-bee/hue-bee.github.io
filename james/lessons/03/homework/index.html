Part A — Percents of Derived Quantities

6 Problems · ~18 min

A "derived quantity" is a value you must calculate first (like an area, a difference, or a total) before you can take a percent of it. Show ALL work — find the base quantity BEFORE applying the percent.

Reminders percent × base = part % change = (new − old) ÷ old Find the derived quantity FIRST
Problem 1 L2

A rectangular garden is 20 ft by 15 ft. What is 40% of the garden's area?

Answer: ft²
Problem 2 L2

A school had 640 students last year. This year enrollment grew by 25%. What is the increase in students (not the new total)?

Answer: students
Problem 3 L3

A store's revenue was $8,000 in Week 1 and $9,200 in Week 2. What percent of the Week 2 revenue does the increase from Week 1 to Week 2 represent?

Answer: %
Problem 4 L3

A rectangle originally measuring 60 m by 30 m has its length increased to 72 m and its width increased to 36 m. By what percent did the AREA increase?

Answer: %
Problem 5 L3

In a survey of 500 people, 55% support a new park, 20% oppose it, and the rest are undecided. If half of the undecided voters later join the "support" side, what is the NEW number of supporters?

Answer: people
Problem 6 L4

A city's population is 72,000. Of these, 62% are adults. Of the adults, 30% are employed in manufacturing. How many people in the ENTIRE city work in manufacturing?

Answer: people

Part C — ELA: "Through" = Method

6 Questions · ~15 min

When a question asks HOW an author conveys, illustrates, or develops an idea "through" something, the correct answer must name a METHOD (a technique), never a fact. Name the method silently before reading the choices.

The Locksmith's Hands

[¶1] Mr. Alvarado had repaired locks in the same four-block radius for thirty-one years, and his shop smelled permanently of machine oil and brass filings. When a customer described a jammed deadbolt over the phone, he could often guess the exact make and model before he ever saw it — the sound of a sticking pin, described secondhand, was enough. His workbench, cluttered with hundreds of small metal pieces sorted by size rather than by name, looked chaotic to any visitor but was, to him, a perfectly ordered map.

[¶2] One winter afternoon, a young woman rushed in, locked out of her apartment with a sleeping infant inside. Mr. Alvarado did not ask questions. He grabbed a single tension wrench and a pick, walked the six blocks himself in the cold rather than wait for her to bring the door to him, and had the lock open within ninety seconds of kneeling before it. He refused payment, saying only that some jobs were not jobs at all.

[¶3] His fingers, thickened and scarred from three decades of work, moved with a precision that seemed to bypass conscious thought — the particular give of a pin tumbler lock, the faint click of a wafer lock giving way, the resistance of a rusted deadbolt versus a merely stiff one. He could distinguish these sensations the way a musician distinguishes notes, and he had long since stopped trying to explain the feeling to apprentices who asked him to describe it in words.

[¶4] When Mr. Alvarado finally retired at seventy-one, he did not sell his workbench or his thousands of sorted parts. He gave them, piece by piece, to the two young locksmiths he had trained, insisting that they memorize the feel of the tools before they ever touched a written manual. "The manual," he told them, "explains what a lock is. Only your hands will ever tell you how to open one."

Q1. The author conveys Mr. Alvarado's expertise in ¶3 primarily through ___. L2
Q2. The passage illustrates Mr. Alvarado's generosity through ___. L2
Q3. In ¶4, Mr. Alvarado's final statement to his apprentices ("The manual explains what a lock is...") functions primarily to: L3
Q4. A student claims: "The author uses only chronology, listing events strictly in the order they happened, to develop the passage." Is this claim supported, partially supported, or contradicted? L3
Q5. Which choice best names the method used in ¶2 to develop the idea that Mr. Alvarado prioritizes people over profit? L3
Q6. The author most likely includes the detail that Mr. Alvarado's workbench "looked chaotic to any visitor but was, to him, a perfectly ordered map" (¶1) in order to: L4

Part C2 — ELA: Naming the Method

6 Questions · ~15 min

Read each mini-passage, then name the method BEFORE looking at any choice. Remember: WHAT is a fact; HOW is a method.

Six Short Passages

[A] Coach Reyes never gave motivational speeches before games. Instead, he would stand at half-court and simply say, "Remember December," referring to the single practice when the team, down by thirty points in a scrimmage, refused to quit and clawed back to win by two. That was all he ever needed to say.

[B] On one side of the border town, families gathered for a harvest festival with music and shared meals. On the other side, less than a mile away, a similar festival had been cancelled for the third year running due to drought. The two communities, once nearly identical, now told very different stories about the same season.

[C] The bakery opened its doors at 4:00 a.m. sharp. By 4:15, the first loaves were in the oven. By 6:00, the display case was full. By 7:30, half of it was already sold. By closing at 2:00 p.m., not a single loaf remained.

[D] The violin's opening notes were thin and hesitant, almost apologetic, like a whisper testing whether anyone in the room was listening. But as the piece progressed, the sound thickened and warmed, filling every corner of the hall until it seemed to press gently against the walls themselves.

[E] A single dented water bottle sat on the shelf of the climbing gym's lost-and-found for eleven years. No one ever claimed it. The owner kept it anyway, unwilling to throw away the one object that had outlasted three different gym locations and every staff member who had ever worked there.

[F] Compared to sedans, which prioritize a smooth low-speed ride, off-road trucks are built with reinforced suspensions that trade comfort for durability on uneven terrain — a difference that becomes obvious the moment either vehicle leaves a paved road.

Q7. Passage [A] develops Coach Reyes's coaching philosophy primarily through ___. L2
Q8. Passage [B] illustrates the divide between the two communities primarily through ___. L2
Q9. Passage [C] conveys the bakery's popularity primarily through ___. L2
Q10. Passage [D] conveys the change in the violin's sound primarily through ___. L3
Q11. Which passage relies MOST heavily on a single anecdote (a short, specific past-tense story) to make its point? L3
Q12. A student claims: "Passage [F] uses an anecdote to explain the difference between sedans and off-road trucks." Is this claim supported, partially supported, or contradicted? L4
