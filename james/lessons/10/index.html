Zone 1 · Cube Roots + Volume

A Cube Has One Repeated Edge

Think first
If a cube's volume is 512 cubic units, what single edge value multiplied by itself three times creates 512? Why is dividing 512 by 3 not the inverse operation?
V = s³   ⇒   s = ∛V
Memory shortcut
A cube-root question is a recognition question disguised as computation. Search the perfect-cube chain before reaching for arithmetic.
Zone 1 · Surface Area

Volume Fills; Surface Area Wraps

SA = 2lw + 2lh + 2wh
Count the face pairs
Which two faces have area lw? Which two have area lh? Which two have area wh? Why does every product appear twice?
SHSAT Trap: The Three-Face Shortcut
Seeing only the front, top, and side in a drawing does not erase their hidden partners. The object still has six faces.
Worked Example · Three Deliberate Moves

From Cube Volume to Surface Area

Problem
A cube has volume 343 cubic centimeters. Find its surface area.
1
Recover the edge
s = ∛343 = 7
The perfect-cube chain supplies the edge.
2
Find one face
s² = 7² = 49
Each square face has area 49 cm².
3
Count six faces
6(49) = 294
Surface area is 294 cm².
Clever check
The unit changes from cubic centimeters to square centimeters because the question changes from filling space to covering faces.

Zone 2 · Tiling

Count Along the Edges, Then Multiply

tiles across = room length ÷ tile side
total tiles = across × down
Why this works
A tile is two-dimensional. Counting one edge tells you only one row. What second count is needed before the entire rectangle is covered?
SHSAT Trap: Dividing Area by a Side
If you use total area, divide by tile area, not tile side. The units expose the mistake: square units cannot be divided by linear units to produce a tile count.
Worked Example · Keep Units Honest

A Room Tiled With Equal Squares

Problem
A 12-foot by 7.5-foot floor is covered with square tiles whose side length is 0.75 foot. How many tiles are needed?
1
Count across
12 ÷ 0.75 = 16
Sixteen tile widths fit along the length.
2
Count down
7.5 ÷ 0.75 = 10
Ten tile heights fit along the width.
3
Build the grid
16 × 10 = 160
The floor needs 160 tiles.
Second route, same structure
Room area is 90 ft²; tile area is 0.5625 ft². Dividing area by area also produces a pure count.

Zone 3 · Coordinate Geometry

Coordinates Encode Two Independent Moves

midpoint = ((x₁+x₂)/2, (y₁+y₂)/2)
slope = vertical change ÷ horizontal change
Read the movement
From A to B, how far do you travel horizontally? How far vertically? Which change belongs in the numerator when slope represents rise per unit of run?
Coordinate Swap
The first coordinate is horizontal; the second is vertical. Keep the x-column and y-column separate from the moment you copy the points.

Zone 4 · Angle Relationships

Name the Relationship Before Writing an Equation

Ask in this order
Are the angles opposite at an intersection? Do they form a straight line? Are they inside a triangle? Is one an exterior angle? Each relationship supplies a different equation.
Vertical angles: equal
Linear pair: sum to 180°
Triangle interior: sum to 180°
Exterior angle: equals two remote interior angles

Zone 4 · Triangle Properties

Use Structure to Reduce the Unknowns

Isosceles
Two equal sides create two equal base angles. Mark the equal pair before assigning variables.
Equilateral
Three equal sides create three 60° angles. Do not waste time solving what the definition already tells you.
Right triangle
One angle is 90°, so the two acute angles sum to 90°. Use the smaller total immediately.
Compression question
What information lets you replace two angle variables with one? The fastest geometry solution often begins by noticing equality, not by calculating.
Zone 5 · Composite Area

Choose the Cleaner Story: Add or Subtract

Two valid decompositions
Add: divide the figure into non-overlapping rectangles.
Subtract: start with one outer rectangle and remove a missing rectangle.
composite area = whole − cutout
SHSAT Trap: Double-Counting the Overlap
If two added rectangles overlap, the shared region is counted twice. Draw the dividing line so pieces touch but never overlap.
Worked Example · Subtraction Route

One Outer Rectangle, One Missing Corner

Problem
An L-shaped figure fits inside a 14-unit by 10-unit rectangle. A 5-unit by 4-unit corner is missing. Find the L-shape's area.
1
Build the whole
14 × 10 = 140
Imagine the missing corner restored.
2
Measure the cutout
5 × 4 = 20
The removed corner is one rectangle.
3
Subtract once
140 − 20 = 120
The remaining area is 120 square units.
Clever check
The L-shape must be smaller than 140 and larger than half of 140. The result passes both bounds.
