Zone 1 · 8-minute sprint

Ratios Become Counts

Rule of objects
A ratio gives parts, not actual objects. First find the size of one part. Then rebuild every count.
1Level 1
A box has 24 clips in a blue:red ratio of 1:3. How many clips are blue?
2Level 2
Thirty beads are split green:yellow:purple in a 2:5:3 ratio. Find each count.
3Level 3
The ratio of red to non-red tiles is 3:7. There are 42 non-red tiles. How many tiles are there altogether?
4Level 4
A jar’s black:white:gray ratio is 4:3:5. After 6 gray counters are removed, 54 remain. Reconstruct the original counts.
Zone 2 · Conditional probability

Removal Changes Both Parts of the Fraction

Think first
If one blue, one red, and one yellow clip leave the box, why can neither the favorable count nor the total stay the same?
P(target next) = target remaining / total remaining
The movie-frame method
Freeze the story after every action. Cross out what left. The next probability must be computed from the newest frame, never the opening frame.
Trap: Shrinking Only the Denominator
If a favorable object leaves, the numerator shrinks too. Ask: “Did the target color lose an object?” before writing the fraction.

Guided walkthrough 1 · finish the last line

A Hard Removal Problem in Three Frames

Problem
A bag holds 36 tokens in a red:blue:green ratio of 2:3:4. Two red, one blue, and three green tokens are removed. Determine the probability that the next token is blue.
1Build the opening frame
Total ratio parts: 2+3+4. One part: 36 ÷ (2+3+4). Multiply by 2, 3, and 4.
2Apply every change
New red: R−2
New blue: B−1
New green: G−3
3Write the new-world fraction
P(blue)= blue remaining / total remaining
Evaluate and simplify independently.
Explain before computing
Why is subtracting 6 only from the denominator incomplete reasoning?
Zone 3 · Two-stage transfers

A Transfer Creates Different Possible Worlds

Think first
If the transferred ball’s color is unknown, can Box A have one fixed composition afterward? What must we branch on?
P(final event) = sum of (branch probability × within-branch probability)
Name the branch
“Red moved” and “blue moved” are not decorations. Each label tells you exactly how to redraw the destination box before the second draw.

Branch, Update, Weight, Combine

1. Branch

Guided walkthrough 2 · finish the arithmetic

A Transfer Problem in Three Weighted Steps

Problem
Box B has 2 red and 4 blue balls. Box A has 3 red and 5 blue balls. A random ball moves from B to A; then a random ball is drawn from A. Determine the probability that the drawn ball is red.
1Weight the transfer
Red branch: 2/(2+4)
Blue branch: 4/(2+4)
2Redraw Box A
If red moved: 4R, 5B
If blue moved: 3R, 6B
3Multiply, then combine
[2/6 × 4/9] + [4/6 × 3/9]
Reasonableness test
Before calculating, should the final probability be closer to Box A’s original red fraction or to 1? What feature of the setup supports your prediction?
Zone 4 · Counting principle

One Choice per Slot? Multiply.

Think first
A toy has 3 paintable parts and each part may use any of 3 colors. Why does adding 3+3+3 miss combinations?
total outcomes = choices for slot 1 × slot 2 × slot 3
The sentence-to-slots test
Whenever you hear “and then,” draw another slot. Each slot records one independent stage of the outcome.

Trap: Adding Independent Stages
Add alternatives within one slot; multiply stages across slots. “Red or blue” suggests addition. “Shirt and pants” suggests multiplication.
Zone 4 · Restrictions

Restrictions Change a Slot

Unrestricted
Each slot keeps its full number of choices. Multiply the slot counts.
No repetition
After a choice is used, later slots have fewer choices. The slots descend.
Must include / cannot include
Place the restriction first, or count all outcomes and subtract the forbidden group.
Decision question
Does the rule remove choices from later slots, force a particular slot, or create a forbidden group that is easier to subtract?

Guided walkthrough 3 · leave the expression unevaluated

A Restricted Count in Three Moves

Problem
A 4-digit code uses digits 0 through 6. Repetition is allowed. Determine how many codes contain at least one 5.
1Count the whole universe
There are 7 choices in each of 4 slots:
7 × 7 × 7 × 7
2Count the forbidden group
If 5 is forbidden, each slot has 6 choices:
6 × 6 × 6 × 6
3Subtract the complement
all codes − codes with no 5
Why subtraction wins
If you counted codes with exactly one 5, what valid codes would still be missing?
