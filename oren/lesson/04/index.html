Diagnostic · Ratios, Percentages, Proportions

Translate Before You Calculate

The clever shortcut: known info → what's asked → one equation. Then calculate.
1Level 2
A recipe calls for flour and sugar in a ratio of 5:2. If a batch uses 20 cups of flour, how many cups of sugar are needed?
2Level 4
A jacket's price is discounted 25%, then the sale price is discounted another 10%. If the original price was $80, what is the final price?
⚠️ SHSAT Trap: Stacking Percentages Wrong
Two discounts never simply add (25% + 10% ≠ 35% off). Each percentage applies to the current price, not the original — multiply successive factors: 0.75 × 0.90.
Diagnostic · Equations & Inequalities

One Rule Everyone Forgets Under Pressure

🤔 Think first
If you multiply or divide both sides of an inequality by a negative number, what has to happen to the inequality sign? Say the rule before you touch the problem.
3Level 3
Solve for x:  -3x + 7 < 22
⚠️ SHSAT Trap: Forgetting to Flip
Dividing both sides by a negative number flips the inequality direction. This single missed step is one of the most common point-losers on equation/inequality items.
Diagnostic · Geometry, Statistics & Probability

Draw It Before You Trust It

4Level 3
Two fair six-sided dice are rolled. What is the probability that the sum of the two dice is exactly 7?
⚠️ SHSAT Trap: Miscounting Outcomes
There are 36 total outcomes for two dice, not 6 — each die is independent. Listing pairs (1,6),(2,5),(3,4),(4,3),(5,2),(6,1) beats trying to guess the count.
Diagnostic · Revising and Editing

Read It Out Loud in Your Head

🤔 Think about it
"Every student who studies for the SHSAT and practice their pacing improves." What's wrong with the verb "practice" here — does it match "student" (singular) or "students" (plural)?
5Level 2
Choose the correction: "Every student who studies for the SHSAT and practice their pacing improves."
⚠️ SHSAT Trap: Subject Buried Mid-Sentence
"Every student" is singular — the true subject. Everything between the subject and the verb ("who studies for the SHSAT") is a distraction clause; strip it out mentally to test agreement.

"Urban beekeeping has grown rapidly in cities worldwide. Advocates argue that rooftop hives support pollinator populations and produce local honey, but critics note that dense hive placement can strain limited urban forage and increase competition with native pollinators."
6Level 3

⚠️ SHSAT Trap: The Half-Right Answer
Choice A grabs only the "critics" half of the passage. The correct central-idea answer must account for every part of the passage — both the "advocates" and "critics" viewpoints.
The Reteach · 20 Minutes

Fixing the One Thing That Matters

Whichever weakness the diagnostic revealed, we drill down here. If nothing stood out clearly, default to the two skills below — they transfer across the whole exam.
If math is weaker
Translate every word problem into three things before calculating: what you know, what's being asked, and one equation connecting them.
If ELA is weaker
Name what the question demands, predict an answer from the passage first, then eliminate choices that are unsupported, exaggerated, or only partly true.
If accuracy is the issue
Reread the question, check units and signs, and estimate whether your final answer is reasonable — every single time, no exceptions.
If pacing is the issue
After roughly two minutes on any question, make your best educated choice and move — a guess beats a blank, and a blank beats a spiral.
The Reteach · Apply It Now

Word-Problem Translation — Easy to Hard

7Level 2
A tutoring session costs $60 per hour. Write and solve an equation for the total cost C of a 2.5-hour session.
8Level 4
A train travels 210 miles in 3.5 hours. At that same rate, how many minutes will it take to travel 90 miles?
⚠️ SHSAT Trap: Mismatched Units
The question asks for minutes, but rate is given in hours. Solve in the given units first, then convert at the very last step — converting early invites arithmetic slips.

Digital Strategy · Your Playbook

Five Habits for the Adaptive Test

✓Habit 1
Scrap Paper, Every Time
Record the question number, key facts, and calculations — even on-screen questions need off-screen work.
✓Habit 2
Verify Before You Click
Confirm you selected the answer you actually intended. There's no undo once you advance.
✓Habit 3
Eliminate, Never Blank
If uncertain, cross out what's wrong and pick your best remaining option — an unresolved question can't advance you.
✓Habit 4
Pace at ~1.8 Min/Question
That's the rough average — reading sets will eat more time than standalone items, so budget accordingly.
✅ Practice on the real platform
NYC Public Schools recommends learning the digital tools and directions, taking at least one full-length official practice test under timed conditions, and reviewing errors for patterns — not paper tests alone.
Timed Practice · 7 Minutes

Prove It Works Under the Clock

Rule: no going back once you commit. Use your new strategy exactly as if this were the real test.
9Level 3
A store marks up a $40 item by 30%, then later discounts the new price by 20%. What is the final price?
10Level 4
Choose the best revision: "Each of the students, despite their busy schedules, have found time to review their notes daily."
⚠️ Time Check
Roughly 1.8 minutes per question means both problems together should take about 3.5–4 minutes. If you're stuck past 2 minutes on one, eliminate and commit.
Zone 5 · The Verification Habit

Not "Is It Right?" — "How Do I Know?"

✓Before
Reread the Ask
Confirm what's actually being requested — total, difference, or one part of a multi-step problem — before you calculate anything.
✓During
Check Units and Signs
Minutes vs. hours, negative vs. positive — these silent errors cost more points than tough content ever does.
✓After
Estimate & Sanity-Check
Does the final number make sense in context? A discount should be smaller, a rate should be reasonable — trust the estimate.
⚠️ Confidence Without Proof
Feeling sure isn't the same as being right — and on the CAT you don't get a second look. Name your check every time before you commit an answer.
