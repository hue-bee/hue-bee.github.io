Warm-Up · Sign Rules

Before the New Stuff — Prove the Old Stuff Sticks

Think first
What happens to the sign when you multiply two negatives? What about a negative times a positive? Say both rules out loud before you touch a pencil.
Why we keep circling back
Your diagnostic flagged sign errors as a real gap — not a knowledge gap, a habit gap. A single missed negative sign can flip an entire GCF or exponent problem today, so we re-check it every session until it's automatic.
Your Habit Target

Zone 1 · Prime Factorization

Every Number Has One True Recipe

Think about it
Is 2 a factor of 60? Is 5? Is 60 itself prime, or can you keep splitting it? Keep dividing by the smallest prime that fits until every branch ends in a prime number — that's the whole method.
60 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 5 = 2² × 3 × 5
Zone 1 · Practice

Prime Factorization — Easy to Hard

1Level 2
Write the prime factorization of 84. Start with the smallest prime that divides it — is it even? Then keep going.
2Level 4
What is the smallest number that has exactly three different prime factors, all less than 10? List the primes, then multiply.
SHSAT Trap: Stopping Too Early
A common mistake is stopping the tree once you hit two factors that "look small enough," like 4 × 21. Neither 4 nor 21 is prime — keep splitting until every leaf on the tree is a prime number.
Zone 2 · GCF & LCM

One Factor Tree, Two Different Questions

Think about it
GCF asks: what's the biggest thing both numbers share? LCM asks: what's the smallest thing both numbers can build? Once you have both prime factorizations side by side, which primes do you take the smaller power of, and which do you take the larger power of?
Memory shortcut
  • GCF — multiply the shared primes, each raised to the smaller power.
  • LCM — multiply every prime that appears, each raised to the larger power.
  • GCF is never bigger than the smaller number. LCM is never smaller than the larger number.
12 = 2²·3    18 = 2·3²   →   GCF = 2·3 = 6    LCM = 2²·3² = 36
Zone 2 · Practice

GCF & LCM — Easy to Hard

3Level 2
Find the GCF of 24 and 36. Factor tree each one first.
4Level 4
Two runners circle a track — one every 8 minutes, the other every 12 minutes. If they start together, how many minutes until they're both back at the start at the same time?
SHSAT Trap: GCF vs. LCM Word Problems
"Sharing into equal groups" or "cutting into the largest equal pieces" signals GCF. "Happening again at the same time" or "lining up together" signals LCM. Underline the clue phrase before you pick which one to compute.
Zone 3 · Exponent Rules

Exponents Count Repeats, Not Values

Think about it
If 2³ means 2×2×2, and 2² means 2×2, what happens when you multiply 2³ × 2²? Count the total 2's being multiplied — don't multiply the exponents.
The rules — same base only
  • Multiplying: add the exponents — aᵌ·aⁿ = aᵌ⁺ⁿ.
  • Dividing: subtract the exponents — aᵌ÷aⁿ = aᵌ⁻ⁿ.
  • A power raised to a power: multiply the exponents.
2³ × 2² = 2⁵ = 32
Zone 3 · Practice

Exponent Rules — Easy to Hard

5Level 2
Simplify 3⁴ × 3². Same base — what do you do to the exponents?
6Level 4
Simplify (2³)² ÷ 2⁴. Do the power-of-a-power step before you divide.
SHSAT Trap: Different Bases
The add/subtract exponent shortcut only works when the bases match. 2³ × 3² is not 6⁵ — you have to calculate each power separately and then multiply the results.
Zone 4 · Radicals (Stretch Goal)

A Square Root Undoes a Square

Think about it
What number, multiplied by itself, gives you 72? It's not a whole number — but is there a perfect square hiding inside 72 that you can pull out? Try splitting 72 into a perfect square times something left over.
Find the Perfect Square Factor
Break the number under the radical into a perfect square times a leftover factor, then pull the perfect square's root outside the radical sign.
√72 = √(36×2) = 6√2
Zone 4 · Practice

Radicals — Easy to Hard

7Level 3
Simplify √50. What perfect square divides evenly into 50?
8Level 4
Simplify √18 + √8. Simplify each radical fully before you try to combine them.
SHSAT Trap: Adding Radicals Too Early
You can only combine radical terms once they share the exact same number under the radical sign. √18 + √8 isn't √26 — simplify each one first, and you'll usually find they match up.

Before You Start
Estimate First
GCF can never exceed the smaller number (24). If your answer comes out bigger than 24, something's wrong.
After You Finish
Plug It Back In
You got GCF = 12. Does 24 ÷ 12 come out whole? Does 36 ÷ 12 come out whole? Both must check out.
If Time Allows
Redo a Different Way
List all factors of 24 and 36 separately and find the largest one they share — same answer, different path proves it.
SHSAT Trap: Confidence Without Proof
Feeling sure isn't the same as being right. Name the check you actually used before you commit to an answer.
