Zone 1 · Circles

Every Circle Part Is a Slice of the Whole

Think about it
A slice of pizza is a fraction of the pie. If the central angle is 90°, what fraction of the circle do you have? What fraction of the crust? Of the cheese? Is it the same fraction every time?
Ask yourself first
Which piece of the whole am I measuring: crust (circumference) or cheese (area)? Write the fraction before any formula.
Arc length
(θ/360) · 2πr
Sector area
(θ/360) · πr²
Radians
arc = rθ  ·  180° = π
Zone 1 · Angles in Circles

The Edge Sees Half of What the Center Sees

Central vs. inscribed
Both angles "look at" the same arc AB. The center angle equals the arc. The angle on the edge, farther away, sees only half. A diameter subtends 180°, so any inscribed angle on it is 90°.
Radius meets tangent at 90°
A tangent touches the circle at one point, and the radius to that point is perpendicular to it. That hidden right angle turns many circle problems into Pythagorean problems.
Think first
If the problem says "tangent" and the figure shows no right angle, where should you draw one? Which segment becomes the hypotenuse?
Memory hook
Center angle = arc. Edge angle = half the arc. Tangent plus radius = right angle. Mark all three on the figure before computing.
Zone 1 · Circles in the xy-Plane

Complete the Square, Read Center and Radius

Think about it
Standard form (x − h)² + (y − k)² = r² hands you the center and radius. Expanded form hides them. Which number do you add to x² + 6x to make a perfect square? Why must you add it to both sides?
1
Group and move the constant
x² + 6x + y² − 4y = 12
2
Half the coefficient, squared
Add (6/2)² = 9 and (−4/2)² = 4 to both sides.
3
Read it off
(x + 3)² + (y − 2)² = 25: center (−3, 2), radius 5.
SAT Trap: Reading Signs and r²
(x + 3)² means h = −3, not 3. The right side is r², so 25 gives a radius of 5. Watch for a coefficient on x² and y²: divide it out first.

Worked Example · Level 3

Arc Length Back to an Inscribed Angle

ModelLevel 3
A circle with center O has radius 6. Minor arc AB has length 4π. Point C lies on the major arc. What is the measure of inscribed angle ACB?
Why it works
You never need a formula for the angle. Turn length into a fraction, the fraction into the central angle, then halve.
1
Find the whole
Circumference = 2π(6) = 12π.
2
Write the fraction first
4π / 12π = 1/3 of the circle.
3
Fraction to central angle
(1/3)(360°) = 120° = ∠AOB.
4
Edge sees half
∠ACB = 120° / 2 = 60°.
Worked Example · Level 4

A Tangent Line From a Right Angle You Cannot See

ModelLevel 4
A circle in the xy-plane has center (2, −1) and passes through (5, 3). Line ℓ is tangent to the circle at (5, 3). What is an equation of ℓ?
1
Radius slope
From (2, −1) to (5, 3): (3 − (−1)) / (5 − 2) = 4/3.
2
Tangent is perpendicular
Negative reciprocal: slope of ℓ = −3/4.
3
Point-slope at the tangency point
y − 3 = −(3/4)(x − 5).
4
Clean up and check
y = −(3/4)x + 27/4. Check: (5, 3) satisfies it, and (4/3)(−3/4) = −1.
Zone 2 · Scaling

Count the Dimensions, Get the Exponent

Think about it
Double every edge of a cube. How many of the small cubes fit inside the big one? How many small faces cover one big face? Which answer is about area, and which is about volume?
The one-line rule
The exponent equals the number of dimensions being measured. Length is 1-D, so k. Area is 2-D, so k². Volume is 3-D, so k³.
area ratio = k²    volume ratio = k³
1 m = 100 cm  ⇒  1 m³ = 1,000,000 cm³
SAT Trap: Units Scale Too
Unit conversions follow the same exponent rule. Square meters convert by 100², cubic meters by 100³. Convert before you enter the answer.

Worked Example · Level 5

A Scale Model and a Unit Change in One Problem

ModelLevel 5
A model of a grain silo is built at a scale of 1 : 40. The silo is a cylinder topped by a hemisphere. The model holds 300 cubic centimeters. What is the volume of the actual silo, in cubic meters?
1
Ignore the shape
The silo's shape is the same in both. Similar solids need only the scale factor, so you need no volume formula.
2
Linear factor to volume factor
k = 40, so volume scales by 40³ = 64,000.
3
Scale the volume
300 × 64,000 = 19,200,000 cm³.
4
Convert with the cube
1 m³ = 100³ cm³ = 10⁶ cm³, so 19,200,000 / 10⁶ = 19.2 m³.

Zone 4 · Desmos Triage

When to Graph, When to Reason

Graph it

  • A line meets a circle, or two curves meet: type both equations, then click the intersections.
  • Roots are messy decimals or radicals.
  • "How many solutions?" or "exactly one point" in a coordinate setting.
  • A constant to find: add a slider and watch the condition happen.

Reason it

  • The figure has no coordinates, as in an angle chase, inscribed angles, or similar triangles.
  • A scale factor or ratio does all the work.
  • The answer needs an exact form such as aπ or b√3 that a decimal hides.
  • Typing would take longer than the two-line algebra.
The 15-second decision
Before you open Desmos, ask: are there equations to type? If the problem is only a picture, the calculator has nothing to graph.
Exact-form check
Desmos may report 0.8333 when the choices read 5/6. Convert the decimal, or use Desmos to check algebra you have already done.
