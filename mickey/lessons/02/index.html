0–8 minutes · Error review

Find the Missed Trigger

Ask about one logged error
What did the diagram give you that you failed to mark? Which theorem should have become visible after that mark? At what exact moment did your approach become longer than necessary?
Classify the cause
  • Recognition: the relationship never became visible.
  • Correspondence: correct theorem, mismatched sides.
  • Reference angle: opposite and adjacent were named from the wrong angle.
  • Execution: correct setup, arithmetic or entry error.
Do not re-solve everything
Rebuild only the decision point that failed. The goal is a reusable trigger, not a longer explanation of yesterday’s numbers.
8–20 minutes · Angle engine

One Angle Fact Can Propagate

Think first
At an intersection, if one angle is known, how many of the other three are forced? Which are equal, and which complete a straight line?
Clever method · Equal-or-180
Do not memorize eight angle names as eight rules. At each crossing, ask only: “Is this angle equal to the known angle, or does it pair with it to make 180°?” Parallel lines copy that pattern to the second crossing.
Angles · Differentiated practice

Angle Chasing: Levels 3–5

1Level 3
Two parallel lines are cut by a transversal. One obtuse angle is (5x−8)°; its corresponding angle is (3x+28)°. Write the governing equation. Which word in the problem justifies it?
2Level 4
At the upper intersection, an angle is (7x+2)°. A same-side interior angle at the lower intersection is (11x−20)°. What must their sum be, and why?
3Level 5
A zigzag crosses three parallel lines. The two acute turns are a° and b°; the reflex turn between them is r°. Draw an auxiliary parallel through the middle vertex and express r using a and b.
Advanced shortcut
When a diagram has too many crossings, carry only one acute measure and its supplement. Every angle in a parallel-line network belongs to one of those two families.
Triangle engine

Search for 180°, Equality, or an Exterior Angle

interior sum = 180°
exterior angle = sum of two remote interior angles
Socratic sequence
Which sides are marked equal? What angles does that force equal? Is the requested angle inside the triangle, on a straight extension, or at the vertex of an isosceles pair?
Clever method · Work backward from the target
Circle the requested angle. Trace one relationship outward at a time until you reach a given value. This prevents solving every angle in the figure.
Similarity

Prove the Match Before the Proportion

1
Find two angle matchesUsually shared, vertical, or parallel-line angles.
2
Write correspondenceMatch vertex to vertex in the same order.
3
Build one scale factorUse known corresponding sides first.
4
Transfer the factorApply it only to corresponding lengths.
Clever method · Vertex map
Write a three-letter map such as A↔D, B↔E, C↔F. Then every valid proportion reads down matching columns. This is safer than choosing sides by appearance.
SAT trap · Reversing only one ratio
Either small/large or large/small works, but the direction must remain consistent in both ratios. One flipped fraction creates a plausible wrong choice.
Similarity · Clean diagram

The Parallel Segment Creates a Smaller Copy

Ask, do not assume
Which angle is shared? Which two angle pairs are equal because the blue segment is parallel to the base? What is the correct vertex map for the small and large triangles?
Clever method · Whole side first
If one side is split into pieces, compute or label the whole side before writing a small-to-large ratio. Piece/whole is usually cleaner than mixing piece/piece across triangles.
Diagram warning
SAT diagrams may be drawn to scale, but measurements must come from labels and theorems—not visual estimation.
Triangles · Differentiated practice

Similarity and Scale: Levels 3–5

4Level 3
△ABC∼△DEF. The side pairs are AB↔DE and BC↔EF. If AB=12, DE=18, BC=20, write one consistent proportion for EF.
5Level 4
A line parallel to a triangle’s base cuts the other two sides into lengths 9 and 6 on the left, and 12 and x on the right. Which ratios compare matching “top piece to whole side” quantities?
6Level 5
Two similar triangles have areas 98 and 200. A side of the smaller triangle is 14. Without taking unnecessary decimals, express the corresponding larger side.
Scale-factor control

Linear, Area, and Volume Are Different Dials

k
Lengths & perimeters

Every one-dimensional measurement scales directly.

k²
Areas

Two independent directions each acquire a factor of k.

k³
Volumes

Three independent dimensions each acquire a factor of k.

Dimensional question
If the area ratio is 25:49, why is the side-length ratio not 25:49? What operation moves you from a square relationship back to a linear one?
Clever method · Read the noun
Before using a scale factor, underline the noun being compared: side, perimeter, area, or volume. The noun determines the exponent.
20–34 minutes · Right triangles

Choose the Tool from What Is Known

A
Two sidesPythagorean theorem or a recognizable triple.
B
Special anglesUse 30–60–90 or 45–45–90 ratios.
C
Angle + sideName opposite, adjacent, and hypotenuse.
D
Similar right trianglesUse a ratio property; avoid solving extra sides.
Decision rule: Pythagorean theorem connects three sides. Trigonometry connects an acute angle with two sides. Special-triangle ratios replace both when the angle structure gives an exact shortcut.
Right-triangle structure

Triples Are Compressed Pythagorean Theorems

a² + b² = c²
High-value primitive triples

3–4–5 · 5–12–13 · 8–15–17 · 7–24–25

Clever method · Check the scale first
Before squaring, divide all visible sides by a common factor. A disguised 9–12–15 triangle is simply 3–4–5 scaled by 3.
Special triangles

Exact Ratios Beat Decimal Trig

Pattern question
Why must the side opposite 30° be the shortest? In a 45–45–90 triangle, what symmetry forces the two legs to match?
Trig selection

SOH–CAH–TOA Is a Side-Selection Filter

sin θ = opp/hyp   cos θ = adj/hyp   tan θ = opp/adj
Ask in this order
Where is the reference angle? Which side is always the hypotenuse? Which of the other two sides does not touch the angle? Which ratio contains exactly the known and unknown sides?
Clever method · Cross out the unused side
After naming the three sides, cross out the side absent from the problem. The remaining pair uniquely identifies sine, cosine, or tangent.
Why trig transfers

A Trig Ratio Belongs to the Angle

What survives scaling?
If every side is multiplied by k, what happens to opposite/hypotenuse? Why does the factor disappear?
Clever explanation
Trig is a similarity shortcut. Every right triangle with the same acute angle has the same shape, so corresponding side ratios remain constant even when all lengths change.
3k/5k = 3/5

Resist the reflex
Do you need the missing leg to answer? Which two named sides does cosine use? Can you identify both directly from the labels?
Clever method
A provided side can be a distractor. Choose the ratio first; calculate a missing side only if the selected ratio actually needs it.
Trig · Level 4

Complementary Angles Swap Sine and Cosine

8Level 4
In a right triangle, the acute angles are u° and v°. If sin u = 7/25, which expression must equal 7/25?
Asin v
Bcos v
Ctan v
D1/cos v
Rotate your viewpoint
A leg opposite u becomes what relative to v? Does the hypotenuse change when the reference angle changes?
Clever method · Rename, do not recalculate
The same two side lengths form sine for one acute angle and cosine for the other. The ratio is unchanged; only the side names rotate.
sin u = cos(90°−u)
Trig · Level 5 passage

Translate the Story into One Right Triangle

Observation deck design. An architect plans a straight access ramp whose horizontal run is 36 feet. Building rules require the ramp to make an angle of θ with level ground, where tan θ = 1/12. A landing will be installed at the vertical height reached by the ramp.
9Level 5
Write an equation connecting the landing height h to the 36-foot run. Then determine what additional calculation, if any, would be required to find the ramp’s actual length.
Clever method · Attach units to the triangle
“Horizontal run” is adjacent, “vertical height” is opposite, and “ramp length” is the hypotenuse. The words name the sides before any sketch does.
