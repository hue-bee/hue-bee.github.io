Four-step filterGoal→Relevant notes→Blueprint→Two locks
Lock 1: AccuracyEvery claim must match the notes. Preserve comparisons, quantities, and limits.

Select only what matters

Questions 1–2 • ~4 min

Name the requested relationship. Do not choose an accurate difference when the goal asks for a similarity—or the reverse.

Question 1

SimilarityCore

While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes:

  • Composer Imani Cole builds pieces from recordings of ordinary household sounds.
  • Composer Theo Lin builds pieces from recordings of mechanical sounds in workshops.
  • Both composers digitally alter the recordings before arranging them into musical patterns.
  • Cole often performs with a violinist; Lin usually performs alone.

The student wants to emphasize a similarity in how Cole and Lin create their music. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?

Question 3

The student wants to introduce Sethi’s experiment and its main finding. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?

Sequence and trade-offs

Questions 5–6 • ~5 min

Time order must remain intact. A trade-off must show both the gain and the corresponding cost.

Question 5

Chronological developmentCore

While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes:

  • In 2008, the Alder Seed Library began distributing seeds from a cabinet at a community center.
  • In 2013, it added an online catalog, though seeds still had to be collected in person.
  • In 2019, it began mailing seeds to registered users.
  • In 2022, it introduced workshops on seed saving.
  • The library distributes both vegetable and flower seeds.

The student wants to trace how access to the library’s seeds expanded from 2008 through 2019. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?

The student wants to emphasize the trade-off involved in choosing the overhead camera instead of the scanner. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?

E

Protect the claim

Questions 7–8 • ~5 min

Keep limits attached to the correct population. Distinguish a researcher’s interpretation from an established fact.

Question 7

Scope limitationStretch

While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes:

  • A study tested whether synchronized captions helped viewers recall details from short instructional videos.
  • The 84 participants were adults who were fluent readers of the caption language.
  • Participants were randomly assigned to watch the same videos with or without captions.
  • The caption group recalled more details on average.
  • The researchers did not test children or people still learning to read the caption language.

The student wants to present the main finding while identifying a specific limit on which viewers it can be generalized to. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?

Confidence:

Question 8

Scholarly disagreementStretch

While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes:

  • An excavation at Hillmere uncovered stone slabs bearing parallel grooves.
  • Archaeologist Leena Das argues that wear inside the grooves indicates that the slabs were used to sharpen tools.
  • Historian Omar Bell argues that the regular spacing of the grooves indicates that they were primarily decorative.
  • Both researchers agree that people deliberately cut the grooves.
  • No metal tools were found beside the slabs.

The student wants to identify the researchers’ disagreement about the original purpose of the grooves. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?

F

Qualify with precision

Questions 9–10 • ~5 min

Check what each number measures and which conditions apply. Do not let a true statement omit half the goal.

Question 9

Numerical qualificationStretch

While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes:

  • In March, 90 of 300 visitors to the Mercer Museum attended a free demonstration.
  • In April, 120 of 600 visitors attended a free demonstration.
  • Thus, 30% of March visitors and 20% of April visitors attended.
  • The museum added two evening demonstrations in April.
  • The demonstrations were held in the same room during both months.

The student wants to show that demonstration attendance rose in number but fell as a share of all visitors. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?

Confidence:

Question 10

Condition and exceptionStretch

While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes:

  • Engineers tested a reflective coating on outdoor storage boxes.
  • On dry, sunny days, coated boxes stayed cooler inside than uncoated boxes.
  • After rain, when a continuous film of water covered the boxes, the temperature difference disappeared.
  • Once the boxes dried, the difference returned.
  • The coating was applied with a roller.

The student wants to emphasize the condition under which the coating’s observed cooling advantage disappeared. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?

G

Connect the evidence

Questions 11–12 • ~5 min

For a mechanism, preserve the causal chain. To reconcile results, explain how both can hold under different conditions.

Question 11

Explain a mechanismCore

While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes:

  • A prototype irrigation valve contains a strip made from two bonded materials.
  • As the strip warms, one material expands more than the other.
  • This unequal expansion bends the strip.
  • The bending motion opens the valve, allowing water to flow.
  • The prototype was displayed at a school engineering fair.

The student wants to explain how warming causes the prototype valve to release water. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?

Confidence:

Question 12

Reconcile findingsStretch

While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes:

  • Team A tested a noise-reducing curtain with speakers producing mostly high-frequency sound.
  • Team A recorded a large reduction in sound passing through the curtain.
  • Team B tested the same curtain with speakers producing mostly low-frequency sound.
  • Team B recorded only a small reduction.
  • A separate test found that the curtain absorbs high-frequency sound more effectively than low-frequency sound.

The student wants to explain why the teams’ results need not conflict. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?
