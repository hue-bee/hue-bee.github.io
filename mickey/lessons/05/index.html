Zone 1 · Retrieval

Name the Relationship — Then the Transition

T1Level 1
The alloy is unusually light. ______, it retains its strength at high temperatures.
What is the second sentence doing: adding, contrasting, or causing?
T2Level 2
The survey sample was small. ______, the researchers treated the pattern as preliminary.
Does sentence two oppose the first, result from it, or illustrate it?
T3Level 3
The archive lacks letters from the expedition. ______, shipping records independently confirm its route.
Is this simple contrast or a concession: “despite that limitation”?
Retrieval rule: cover the blank mentally. State the logical relationship in ordinary words before naming a transition.
Zone 2 · Anatomy

A Synthesis Item Has Three Moving Parts

1

Notes

A small research bank. Some facts matter; some are deliberate noise.

2

Writer's goal

The scoring criterion. Its verb determines what the sentence must do.

3

Choices

Different selections and arrangements of note content. Only one best fits.

The common misread
Treating the notes as a passage to summarize. The task is selective construction, not comprehensive reporting.

The job-description test

The goal is a job description. A choice can be impressive and truthful yet still be the wrong hire.

The noise-budget test

Every extra fact spends attention. Keep a detail only if it performs work required by the goal.

Worked Problem · Findings

Level 3 — Introduce a Study and Its Finding

1Level 3
A student has taken the following notes:
  • Researchers measured afternoon temperatures on twelve city blocks.
  • Six blocks had extensive rooftop gardens; six did not.
  • The paired blocks were similar in building height and street width.
  • On clear summer days, blocks with rooftop gardens averaged 1.8°C cooler.
  • The project received funding from a regional planning council.
The student wants to introduce the study and its main finding. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes?
ATwelve city blocks were included in a planning-council-funded project about rooftop gardens.
BAfter matching blocks for building height and street width, researchers found that blocks with extensive rooftop gardens averaged 1.8°C cooler on clear summer afternoons than blocks without them.
CRooftop gardens, which regional planners funded, can reduce city temperatures in every weather condition.
DSix of the twelve blocks in the study had rooftop gardens, and the other six had similar buildings.
Step-by-Step Solve · Problem 1

Three Moves, Then Stop Before the Letter

1

Translate the goal

“Introduce the study and finding” becomes: researchers + comparison design + temperature result.

2

Mark required notes

Keep the matched groups and 1.8°C difference. The funder does not help perform this goal.

3

Apply both locks

Reject any choice that merely lists setup facts or expands “clear summer days” into every condition.

Study context + valid comparison + central result
Your final move: select the choice that matches this frame without strengthening the evidence.

  • Artist Rafael Ortiz creates landscapes from outdated nautical charts.
  • Both artists preserve labels and route lines from their source materials.
  • Park began exhibiting in 2018; Ortiz began exhibiting in 2015.
The student wants to emphasize a similarity between Park’s and Ortiz’s artistic methods. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information?
APark began exhibiting three years after Ortiz did.
BPark makes portraits, whereas Ortiz makes landscapes.
CAlthough their subjects differ, both Park and Ortiz retain printed features from the maps they reuse.
DOutdated maps can be reused by artists rather than discarded.
Zone 4 · Differentiated Practice

Level 3 — Explain and Illustrate

3Level 3
A student has taken the following notes:
  • A keystone species has an unusually large effect on its ecosystem relative to its abundance.
  • Sea otters eat sea urchins.
  • Sea urchins graze on kelp.
  • Where sea otter numbers decline, sea urchin populations can expand and kelp forests can shrink.
  • Sea otters have dense fur rather than blubber.
The student wants to explain the concept of a keystone species and illustrate it with sea otters. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information?
ASea otters have dense fur and eat sea urchins, which graze on kelp.
BA keystone species can strongly shape an ecosystem despite not being abundant; for example, by limiting kelp-eating sea urchins, sea otters can help sustain kelp forests.
CWhen sea otter numbers decline, their unusually dense fur cannot protect kelp forests.
DKelp forests shrink because sea otters are less abundant than sea urchins.
Zone 4 · Hard Case

Level 4 — Emphasize a Methodological Trade-off

4Level 4
A student has taken the following notes:
  • Fixed acoustic sensors record continuously at selected forest sites.
  • Mobile acoustic surveys record along routes traveled by researchers.
  • Fixed sensors can capture sounds at night without a person present.
  • Mobile surveys cover more locations in a single day.
  • Both methods can detect species that are difficult to see.
The student wants to emphasize a trade-off between the two methods. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information?
ABoth fixed sensors and mobile surveys can detect species that observers may not see.
BFixed sensors operate at selected sites, while mobile surveys are conducted along researcher-traveled routes.
CWhereas fixed sensors provide continuous, unattended recording at particular sites, mobile surveys trade continuous coverage for access to more locations in a day.
DMobile surveys are more effective than fixed sensors because they cover more locations.
Step-by-Step Solve · Problem 4

Make “Trade-off” Visible in the Grammar

1. Define the relation.A trade-off requires an advantage and a corresponding limitation.
2. Pair matching dimensions.Continuity belongs with site coverage; visibility detection is shared background.
3. Inspect the grammar.Look for a structure such as “whereas” or “trades X for Y,” not two disconnected facts.
Precision check: “more locations” does not prove “more effective.” Preserve the notes' scope.
Zone 4 · Hard Case

Level 4 — Establish a Chronological Development

5Level 4
A student has taken the following notes:
  • In 1992, astronomers confirmed planets orbiting a pulsar.
  • In 1995, astronomers confirmed 51 Pegasi b, the first planet found orbiting a Sun-like star.
  • 51 Pegasi b was detected through changes in its star's radial velocity.
  • The transit method later enabled large surveys to identify planets by small, periodic dips in starlight.
  • Different methods are sensitive to different planetary characteristics.
The student wants to describe the chronological development of exoplanet detection. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information?
AExoplanets can be detected by radial velocity or by periodic dips in starlight.
BAfter the 1992 pulsar-planet confirmation, the 1995 detection of 51 Pegasi b marked the first confirmed planet around a Sun-like star; later, transit surveys greatly expanded planet searches.
CBecause different methods detect different planetary characteristics, 51 Pegasi b was the first exoplanet ever confirmed.
DTransit surveys preceded the radial-velocity detection of 51 Pegasi b but followed the pulsar discoveries.
Zone 5 · Advanced

Level 5 — Present a Finding While Qualifying It

6Level 5
A student has taken the following notes:
  • A meta-analysis combined 27 experiments on sleep and memory consolidation.
  • Across the studies, sleep was generally associated with better later recall than an equivalent period awake.
  • The size of the benefit varied substantially across experiments.
  • Studies used different memory tasks and sleep intervals.
  • Most participants were healthy adults under age 35.
  • The authors cautioned against assuming the same effect size for older adults or clinical populations.
The student wants to present the overall finding while carefully qualifying its generalizability. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information?
AA meta-analysis of 27 experiments proves that sleep improves every kind of memory for people of all ages.
BAlthough sleep was generally associated with better later recall, variation in tasks, intervals, and participant populations limits how broadly the meta-analysis’s effect size should be generalized.
CThe studies used different memory tasks because most participants were healthy adults under age 35.
DSleep and wakefulness produced different recall results in 27 experiments, all of which used comparable methods.
Step-by-Step Solve · Problem 6

Protect the Claim from Overreach

Supported center
generally better recall
across 27 experiments
Required boundary
effect size varied
tasks and samples differed
1. Preserve the hedge.“Generally associated” cannot become “proves” or “always.”
2. Name the boundary.The goal requires a limitation, not merely another study detail.
3. Match limitation to claim.Method and sample variation restrict broad generalization of effect size.
Advanced cue: when the goal says “qualify,” the best sentence often contains both a concession and a scope boundary.
Zone 5 · Advanced

Level 5 — Frame a Scholarly Disagreement Precisely

7Level 5
A student has taken the following notes:
  • Excavations at Port Selene uncovered ninth-century glazed ceramic fragments.
  • Historian N. Rahman argues that their chemical match with kilns across the sea indicates regular long-distance trade.
  • Archaeologist I. Petrov notes that only eleven fragments have been recovered.
  • Petrov argues that the fragments could have arrived through occasional elite exchange rather than routine commerce.
  • Both researchers agree that the fragments were not made locally.
  • No shipping records from Port Selene survive.
The student wants to introduce the researchers’ disagreement about what the fragments imply. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information?
ARahman and Petrov agree that Port Selene regularly imported glazed ceramics from overseas kilns.
BBecause no shipping records survive, neither researcher can determine where the fragments were made.
CWhile Rahman interprets the overseas chemical match as evidence of regular trade, Petrov argues that the small number of fragments is also consistent with occasional elite exchange.
DEleven ninth-century fragments were found at Port Selene, and both researchers study long-distance exchange.
