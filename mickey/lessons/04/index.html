Cold Redo · M2 Q21

Three-Step Unit Chain Without the Final Selection

1
Convert area
Because both dimensions change, square the factor: one square meter corresponds to (3.28)² square feet.
2
Convert time
The rate is “per hour.” Multiply by an hour-to-minute factor arranged so hours cancel.
3
Estimate before choosing
The number should become much smaller than 250 after both conversions. Use magnitude to reject options.
250 ft²/hr × (1 m² / 3.28² ft²) × (1 hr / 60 min) = ______ m²/min

Cold Redo · RW M1 Q24

Synthesis Sequence: Events, Not Research Steps

R1Level 4
Notes, condensed: Researchers proposed that Saturn once had a large moon called Chrysalis. About 160 million years ago, it likely approached Saturn closely; tidal forces broke it apart; the resulting debris may have formed Saturn’s rings.
Goal: Recount the sequence of events proposed by the researchers.
A.Chrysalis approached Saturn; ring debris then caused the moon to break apart.
B.Close-orbiting moons can be broken apart by planetary tidal forces.
C.Chrysalis approached Saturn, tidal forces fragmented it, and the debris later formed rings.
D.Researchers proposed a moon, ran simulations, and then formed a hypothesis.

Cold Redo · RW M2 Q8

Inference: Resolve Obscurity With Context

R3Level 4
Classical Nahuatl poetry uses difrasismo: two parallel nouns conventionally act as one metaphor. The expression meaning literally “the eagle, the jaguar” signifies “warrior.” Because the nouns’ relation to the intended concept may seem tenuous, the metaphor often becomes intelligible only through associations from Aztec ceremonial culture.
Which claim about this example is most strongly supported?
A.Its nouns are semantically equivalent.
B.Modern readers miss its formal verse function whenever they miss its meaning.
C.Its seeming obscurity can be resolved through the relevant cultural context.
D.Its frequency proves that every Aztec listener understood it.
Evidence boundary
Which option restates the final relationship with the fewest new assumptions? Which options introduce equivalence, present-day readers, or frequency without textual support?

Transitions System

The Blank Is a Bridge Between Ideas

Do not ask which word sounds sophisticated. Ask what relationship the second idea has to the first.

Transitions · Core Routine

Cover Choices Until You Can Name the Relationship

1
Read around the blank
Read the sentence before and after. Ignore the transition options for now.
2
Compress each idea
Reduce both sides to 3–7 words. Keep direction, degree, and any evaluation.
3
Name the bridge
Say continuation, contrast, result, example, sequence, or clarification; then open choices.
Idea 1 → relationship → Idea 2
Why choices stay covered
Transitions can sound grammatically smooth while expressing the wrong logic. Prediction turns a vocabulary contest into a relationship match.
Transitions · Logic Map

Six Families, One Decision

Continuation

Same direction; new support.

also, moreover, indeed

Contrast

Idea 2 opposes or limits idea 1.

however, by contrast

Cause / Result

Idea 1 produces idea 2.

therefore, consequently

Example

Specific case supports a broader claim.

for example, specifically

Sequence

Ideas are ordered in time or process.

next, subsequently

Clarification

Idea 2 restates or sharpens idea 1.

in other words, that is

Family before word
Several words may belong to one family. The SAT usually separates options by logic first; nuance matters only after the family is correct.

Transitions · Item T3

Contrast vs. Concession: Did the First Idea Create an Expectation?

T3Level 4
The catalyst is costly to manufacture. ______, because it can be reused hundreds of times, the process may still be economical.
A.Similarly,
B.Nevertheless,
C.For instance,
D.Accordingly,
Concession is contrast plus a defeated expectation: “That obstacle is real; even so, the outcome survives.”
Transitions · Item T4

The Contrast Can Be Buried Mid-Sentence

T4Level 4
Early maps portrayed the coastline as nearly straight. Modern sonar measurements reveal numerous submerged valleys. The newer maps, ______, do more than add detail: they revise the basic shape long assumed by researchers.
A.in addition,
B.for example,
C.however,
D.therefore,
Do not compare adjacent nouns
Compare the claims: “straight coast” versus “basic shape revised.” The bridge may sit after the subject of the second clause.
Punctuation is not the logic
Several conjunctive adverbs fit the commas. The sentence relationship—not comma placement—selects the family.
