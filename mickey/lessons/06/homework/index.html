R1Choose one answer
Two editions of a novel can share a title while differing in their wording. A library’s revised catalog assigns a separate identifier to each edition. _____, a reader can now request a particular edition without confusing it with another version.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

R2Choose one answer
While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes:
  • In a classroom demonstration, a ceramic artist prepared a clay bowl.
  • The artist first pressed a woven mat against the soft clay.
  • The artist removed the mat, leaving its pattern in the clay.
  • The artist then fired the bowl in a kiln, hardening the patterned surface.
  • The mat was not placed in the kiln.

The student wants to describe the sequence by which the bowl’s patterned surface was produced. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?

Match the exact rhetorical goal. For inference, distinguish what the text supports from what merely could be true. Stop after six total minutes, then add your brief explanations.

R3Choose one answer
While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes:
  • A design class tested two pigments, Luma and Vela.
  • Luma was extracted from a naturally occurring mineral.
  • Vela was manufactured by combining chemicals in a laboratory.
  • Both pigments produced the same shade of blue.
  • Both pigments were used in the class’s final exhibition.

The student wants to emphasize a difference in the sources of the two pigments. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?

R4Choose one answer
A historian examined surviving account books from several workshops in one town. Most of the books came from the largest workshops, whose owners had the resources to preserve their records. The historian found that these workshops bought expensive imported dyes. She cautioned, however, that records from small workshops were scarce and that those workshops may have purchased different materials.

Which statement is most strongly supported by the text?

Verify before you score

+

Keep the answer key closed. Run both checking sweeps on the same work. If you change an answer, record the original and corrected responses below instead of erasing the evidence.

Sweep 1 · Coverage & meaning

Minute 1: Find blanks. Return only if you can name the next useful step.

Minute 2: Check units, requested quantity, and part-versus-whole relationships.

Minute 3: Compare each final entry with the last line of your work.

Sweep 2 · Challenge the result

Minute 1: Test restrictions in M10 and M12 against the original equations.

Minute 2: Independently verify one flagged item: substitution, graph, or a second area expression.

Minute 3: Recheck R2–R4 for goal mismatch or unsupported claims.

T1 · Transfer the dimension

Two similar solids have a surface-area ratio of 25 : 64, smaller to larger. Without choosing example dimensions, determine their volume ratio and explain why multiplying an area by a linear scale factor would not answer this question.

T2 · Audit a squared equation

A student solves √(x + 11) = x − 1 by squaring and obtains candidates x = 5 and x = −2. The student accepts both because both satisfy the squared equation. Evaluate that reasoning and verify each candidate in the original equation.

T1 and T2 are explanation activities, not SAT-format scored questions. Record them separately from the /20 question score.
