SESSION 6Reading & Writing14–16 MIN

Reading repair · Let the passage set the limit

Poetry in Classical Nahuatl, the language of the Aztec Empire, relies on difrasismo, or a parallel noun construction that conventionally operates as a single metaphor. For example, the common difrasismo in cuauhtli in ocelotl (literally, “the eagle, the jaguar”) signifies “warrior.” The device’s function is both formal—providing structure to lines of verse—and ritual: semantic relations among the two nouns and the concept they signify can be tenuous, as in the previous example, such that difrasismos are often only intelligible according to the conceptual associations observed in Aztec ceremonial culture. Which statement about the difrasismo in cuauhtli in ocelotl is most strongly supported by the text?

A.Its metaphorical significance derives from the semantic equivalence of the two nouns constituting the difrasismo.
B.Its unintelligibility may cause its formal function within a line of verse to go unnoticed by present-day readers.
C.Its apparent obscurity can be resolved when considered in the proper cultural context.
D.Its frequency in Classical Nahuatl poetry confirms its intelligibility to the Aztec audience.
Locate the sentence that defines the necessary context. Which option adds a claim the passage never made?

M04 · Undo the dimension first

Level 3
1
Name the linear scale factor s. Similar solids satisfy V₂ / V₁ = s3; therefore s = ∛(125 / 27).
2
Lateral area has two dimensions, so A₂ / A₁ = s2. Substitute A₁ = 54 only after naming that exponent.
3
Set up A₂ = 54 · (∛(125 / 27))2. Simplify independently and attach square centimeters.
A volume ratio is a three-layer disguise. Why would multiplying 54 by 125/27 over-scale the area?

M09 · Tangency compresses two equations

Level 4
1
Equate the expressions for y: x2 − 6x + 13 = mx + 4. Collect terms to obtain x2 − (6 + m)x + 9 = 0.
2
Exactly one intersection means one repeated real x-value. Set the discriminant to zero: (6 + m)2 − 36 = 0.
3
Keep both square-root branches when solving for m. Find each value, add them, then verify a single contact point in Desmos.
Why can two different slopes each create one point of contact? What would a zoomed-out graph conceal?

SESSION 6Guided path46–49 MIN

M12 · Radius becomes a distance

Level 5
1
The x-axis tangency is already encoded by center (0, r). Write the other line in standard form: 3x − y + 12 = 0.
2
A tangent line is one radius from the center: |12 − r| / √10 = r. Because r > 0, both sides are nonnegative.
3
Square and collect: (12 − r)2 = 10r2. Solve the resulting quadratic, keep only a positive radius, and test it in the unsquared equation.
A tangent is a radius-distance constraint. How does this eliminate the unknown contact point entirely?
