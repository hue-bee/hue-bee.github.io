# Digital SAT: What to Expect on Math and Reading & Writing

*Tutor handout — structure, content weights, and prep priorities*


## 1. Test Architecture at a Glance

The Digital SAT is a single adaptive test of **2 hours 14 minutes** of testing time (plan ~3 hours at the test center with check-in and break).

| Feature | Reading and Writing (RW) | Math |
|---|---|---|
| Total time | 64 min (two 32-min modules) | 70 min (two 35-min modules) |
| Questions | 54 (27 per module; 2 unscored) | 44 (22 per module; 2 unscored) |
| Average pace | ~71 seconds per question | ~95 seconds per question |
| Question format | 100% four-option multiple choice | ~75% multiple choice, ~25% student-produced response (SPR) |
| Tools | Annotation, Mark for Review, timer toggle | Same, plus built-in graphing calculator and formula sheet |
| Subject contexts | Literature, history/social studies, humanities, science | Science, social science, and real-world/everyday contexts |

**Order of events:** RW Module 1 → RW Module 2 → 10-minute break → Math Module 1 → Math Module 2.

### How the adaptivity works

- Adaptivity is **module-level**, not question-level.
- Module 1 in each section is a broad mix of easy, medium, and hard questions.
- Performance on Module 1 routes the student into a Module 2 that is, on average, **harder** (higher score ceiling) or **easier** (lower score ceiling).
- Within any module, students can move freely: skip ahead, go back, and flag items with **Mark for Review**.
- Modules cannot be revisited once submitted.

> **Coaching implication:** Module 1 accuracy disproportionately determines the attainable score. Front-half timing discipline is the highest-leverage habit to build.


## 2. Reading and Writing: What Shows Up

Expect **many short passages**, each with a single question attached — not long passages with question sets. Passages are typically one paragraph.

### Fixed question order (every module)

1. Craft and Structure
2. Information and Ideas
3. Standard English Conventions
4. Expression of Ideas

Within each domain, similar skills are grouped together and ordered roughly **easiest to hardest**.

### Domain distribution

| Domain | Share | Question count | Skills tested |
|---|---|---|---|
| Craft and Structure | ~28% | 13–15 | Words in Context; Text Structure and Purpose; Cross-Text Connections |
| Information and Ideas | ~26% | 12–14 | Central Ideas and Details; Command of Evidence (textual and quantitative); Inferences |
| Standard English Conventions | ~26% | 11–15 | Boundaries (punctuation, sentence structure); Form, Structure, and Sense |
| Expression of Ideas | ~20% | 8–12 | Rhetorical Synthesis; Transitions |

### What this means in practice

- **Roughly a quarter of RW is rule-based grammar.** Boundaries (commas, semicolons, colons, dashes, fragments, run-ons) plus Form/Structure/Sense (verb tense and agreement, pronouns, modifiers) is the fastest-improving content for most students.
- **Graph reading lives in the verbal section too.** Quantitative Command of Evidence items attach tables, bar graphs, and line graphs to short science and social science texts.
- **Rhetorical Synthesis** gives bulleted notes plus a stated goal; the task is choice-of-purpose, not grammar.
- **Transitions** questions reward reading the sentence *before and after* the blank rather than pattern-matching the word.



## 3. Math: What Shows Up

All four domains appear in **each** module, ordered roughly easiest to hardest. A graphing calculator and reference sheet are available for the entire section.

| Domain | Share | Question count | Core content |
|---|---|---|---|
| Algebra | ~35% | 13–15 | Linear equations in one and two variables; linear functions; systems of two linear equations; linear inequalities |
| Advanced Math | ~35% | 13–15 | Equivalent expressions; nonlinear equations and systems; quadratic, exponential, polynomial, rational, and radical functions |
| Problem-Solving and Data Analysis | ~15% | 5–7 | Ratios, rates, proportions, units; percentages; one- and two-variable data; probability and conditional probability; inference from samples and margin of error; evaluating statistical claims |
| Geometry and Trigonometry | ~15% | 5–7 | Area and volume; lines, angles, and triangles; right triangles and trigonometry; circles |

### What this means in practice

- **Algebra + Advanced Math ≈ 70% of the section.** A student weak in function notation, quadratics, and exponential growth cannot offset it with geometry mastery.
- **Word problems are shorter** than on the legacy paper test — the reading load was deliberately reduced.
- **SPR (fill-in) questions** have no answer choices to reverse-engineer. Drill both the algebra fluency and the entry conventions: fractions and decimals accepted, negatives allowed, no percent or dollar signs, no mixed numbers.
- **Calculator discipline matters more, not less.** With the graphing tool always available, teach when graphing beats algebra (systems, zeros, intersections) and when it wastes 40 seconds.


## 4. Scoring and Benchmarks

- **Total score:** 400–1600, reported in 10-point intervals.
- **Section scores:** 200–800 each for Reading and Writing and for Math.
- **No subscores or cross-test scores.** Reports instead provide domain-level performance plus Skills Insight descriptors.
- **College and Career Readiness benchmarks:** 480 RW, 530 Math.
- **Turnaround:** results in days, not weeks.

> **Coaching implication:** Domain-level reporting maps cleanly onto an error log. Track misses by the domain and skill names above so practice assignments are targeted rather than generic.



## 5. Prep Priorities (Ranked)

1. **Secure Module 1 accuracy under timing** in both sections — routing sets the ceiling.
2. **Teach Boundaries punctuation as an explicit rule set** — the densest scoring cluster in RW.
3. **Build Algebra and Advanced Math fluency before Geometry** — match the ~70% combined weight.
4. **Practice inside the official Bluebook app** so Mark for Review, annotation, the timer toggle, and the graphing calculator are automatic on test day.
5. **Train chart and table reading on both sides** — quantitative evidence in RW, data analysis in Math.
6. **Rehearse SPR entry format** so no correct answer is lost to a formatting error.
