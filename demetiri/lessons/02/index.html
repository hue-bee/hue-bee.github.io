Arithmetic Core · Place Value

Every Digit Has a Job and Address

Think first
In 50,310.04, why does the 3 contribute 300 while the 4 contributes only four hundredths?
50,310.04 = 50,000 + 300 + 10 + 0.04
Place-Value Trap
A zero can hold a place without adding value. Never delete interior zeros until you know what place they protect.

Arithmetic Core · Exponents

The Exponent Counts Factors, Not Size

Make the notation speak
What does the small 3 in 4³ tell you to repeat? Why is 4 × 3 a different instruction?
4³ = 4 × 4 × 4
base = repeated factor · exponent = number of factors
Parentheses Change the Base
(3 + 4)² squares the entire sum. 3² + 4² squares two separate bases. Do not distribute an exponent over addition.

Three-step model

1
Expand each power: 4² + 3³ = (4 × 4) + (3 × 3 × 3).
2
Evaluate the powers before adding: 16 + 27.
3
Combine only after the powers are complete. Keep the structure visible on scratch paper.
Proof question: Expand both (3 + 4)² and 3² + 4². Where do the extra cross-products come from?

Arithmetic Core · Order of Operations

One Legal Move Per Line

Why write intermediate lines?
If two operations have equal rank, what determines the order? How does a written line prevent a correct mental step from colliding with the next one?
Group → Powers → × ÷ → + −
Multiplication and division share a rank; work left to right. Addition and subtraction do the same.

Step-by-step model

1
18 − 2(3² − 5) ÷ 4
Evaluate the exponent inside the grouping.
2
18 − 2(9 − 5) ÷ 4
Finish the parentheses before leaving them.
3
18 − 2(4) ÷ 4
Now multiplication and division run left to right; subtraction waits.
TACHS Trap: “M Before D”
PEMDAS does not mean multiplication always comes before division. Equal-rank operations move from left to right.

Arithmetic Core · Negative Numbers

Subtraction Means Add the Opposite

Ask what direction changes
Starting at 1, subtracting positive 4 moves left. If the amount being subtracted is negative 4, why should the direction reverse?
1 − (−4) = 1 + 4
Subtracting a debt removes a loss.
Sign Trap
The rule is not “two negatives always make a positive.” It applies here because one sign is the subtraction operation and one belongs to the number.

Arithmetic Core · Factors and Primes

Use Structure Before Listing

Start with a fast test
The digits of 51 add to 6. What does that reveal immediately? Once one factor is found, what partner completes the pair?
Divisibility: even → divisible by 2; digit sum divisible by 3 or 9; last digit 0 or 5 → divisible by 5.
Common factors: first find the greatest common factor; every factor of it is shared.
72 = 8 × 9 = 2³ × 3²

Arithmetic Core · Decimals and Smart Products

Align Places; Reshape Friendly Numbers

Two structures, one habit
Why do decimal points—not the right edges—control subtraction? Why is 9 × 25 easier when 9 is treated as 10 − 1?

Two-step smart product

1
Replace 9 with a nearby friendly number: 9 × 25 = (10 − 1) × 25.
2
Distribute: 10 × 25 − 1 × 25. One easy product, then one correction.
Reasonableness: nine groups of 25 must be slightly less than ten groups of 25.
Decimal Trap
Zeros added at the right end of a decimal do not change its value; they make matching places visible.

0:40–0:58 · Ability

Paper Folding Is Reversible Geometry

Predict before opening
When one fold creates two layers and one punch passes through both, what must appear after unfolding? Which fold line acts like a mirror?
Fold → Count → Punch → Unfold backward → Mirror
Each unfolding reflects every existing hole across the fold line just opened.
Do Not Count Marks on the Folded Picture
Count layers underneath each punch. One visible punch can create several holes.
Paper Folding · One Fold

Equal Distance on Opposite Sides

25Level 1
Predict the number of holes after unfolding. Where must the partner hole lie relative to the vertical fold?
Distance test
If the punch is 2 cm from the fold on the folded half, how far from the fold is its mirror? What stays unchanged vertically?
Fast Elimination
Cross out any choice with the wrong hole count or unequal distances from the fold line.
Paper Folding · Two Folds

Unfold in Reverse Order

Three-step model

1
Count layers before looking at choices: two half-folds create four layers.
2
Undo the horizontal fold first. Mirror the punch across that horizontal line.
3
Undo the vertical fold next. Mirror both existing holes across the vertical line.
Why reverse order?
The most recent fold is physically on top and opens first. How would opening the earlier fold first scramble the location?
Paper Folding · Diagonal Fold

A Diagonal Fold Is Still a Mirror

Diagonal Trap
Do not reflect “left to right” automatically. The mirror direction is perpendicular to the diagonal fold line.
Perpendicular-distance test
If a punch is close to a diagonal crease, where must its reflected partner be? Which measurement must match on both sides?
26Level 3
Sketch the reflected point across the diagonal. Draw a short segment from the original point to its mirror. What angle should that segment form with the fold line?
27Level 4
After a vertical fold and then a diagonal fold, which crease must be reversed first? Predict the symmetry before drawing every hole.
