Part D — ELA: Theme & evidence

2 Questions ·

Read the original fiction passage. Write a theme statement before answering E1 and E2. Review both responses within this passage set; once you move to the error log, treat the set as submitted.

The Blue Clipboard

[¶1] Leena spent Saturday drawing a route for the visitors who would tour her building’s community garden. She used a ruler, numbered every planting bed, and placed each arrow exactly in the center of the path. At the top of the page she wrote FINAL. When her father offered to walk the route with her, she tapped the blue clipboard. “It’s all here,” she said.

[¶2] The next morning, Mr. Ortiz arrived with a cart of seedlings. He stopped at the three steps below the gate where Leena’s route began. “Does the tour have to start here?” he asked. Leena pointed to her first arrow and began explaining the order of the beds. Then she noticed his hands tightening around the cart’s handles. She lowered the clipboard. “Which entrance do you usually use?” Mr. Ortiz led her to a longer, level path beside the laundry room. Along the way, he pointed out where two carts could pass and where a branch caught on his sleeve.

[¶3] Before the visitors arrived, Leena drew a thick pencil line across her careful arrows. The planting beds no longer appeared in numerical order. She rubbed at the word FINAL until a gray cloud remained. When her father came downstairs and asked whether the map was finished, she handed him a pencil. “Will you walk it with me?” she asked.

E1. What does Leena learn about deciding when a plan is ready? Explain a theme that applies beyond gardening, and show how the overwritten route line develops it. Include one detail from paragraph 1 as well.

Choose, audit, then commit.

E2. A reader says, “Leena changes her map because she has lost confidence in making any decisions herself.” Evaluate this interpretation using one detail from paragraph 2 and one from the ending.

Choose, audit, then commit.
