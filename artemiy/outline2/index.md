**Original plan prepared:** November 8, 2026
**Expanded:** November 10, 2026
**Target test:** Fall 2027 SHSAT (8th-grade administration, digital computer-adaptive format; November 2027 assumed, official dates not yet published)
**Primary goal:** Raise Math accuracy from ~67–70% to 88%+ while holding ELA at 93%+

> **How to read this file.** Sections 1–2 update the original plan for the computer-adaptive test (CAT). Sections 3–4 are the math teaching content. Sections 5–8 are the calendar. ## 0. What Changed From the Original Plan

| Original plan said | Why it needs to change | Updated version |
|---|---|---|
| Targets out of 57 (e.g., 50+/57 Math) | The CAT has 50 questions per section, not 57 | Targets restated as percentages and as /50 on fixed-form practice tests (Section 10) |

The **content** of the original plan (geometry first, then percent/rates, then equation accuracy, then number sense) is sound and stays in the same priority order.

## 2. The Digital CAT: Rules and Strategy

### 2.1 Format facts (official NYC Public Schools description of the fall 2026 CAT)

- Two sections, ELA and Math, **50 questions each**, 180 minutes standard time, shared between sections.
- The student chooses which section to start with.
- Question types: multiple choice, fill-in-the-blank, and technology-enhanced items (TEI).
- Math areas named officially: ratios and proportional relationships; number system and expressions; equations; inequalities; statistics and probability; geometry.
- The test opens with an item or passage set of average difficulty, then re-estimates the score after each response. A correct answer may bring a harder question; an incorrect answer may bring an easier one.
- Every student answers the same number of questions on the same grade-level standards.
- Each subject includes **10 unscored field-test items**. Treat every question as if it counts.
- No calculator. Formulas and definitions are not provided.
- Taken on DOE-provided computers at school or at an assigned test site.

**Assumption to re-check:** these rules describe the fall 2026 administration. The guide for the fall 2027 test has not been published. Re-read the official SHSAT page and the new guide when they appear (typically before fall registration).

### 2.2 Navigation rules (the biggest behavior change)

| Question type | Can you skip? | Can you go back? |
|---|---|---|
| Math (all 50) | No, must answer to advance | No |
| ELA stand-alone revising/editing | No | No |
| ELA passage sets | Must answer all in the set | Yes, within the set until it is submitted; never after |
| Whole subject after submission | — | No |

### 2.3 The Commit Protocol for Math (tutor recommendation)

Because there is no return, the final check happens **before** clicking Next. Train this sequence until it is automatic:

1. **Read the last line first.** What exactly is being asked for (value of x, or 2x + 1? radius or diameter? percent or amount?). Write the target on scratch paper.
2. **Solve on paper.** Not in the head. One step per line.
3. **Sanity check (10–15 seconds).** Pick the fastest of: substitute back, estimate the size, check units, check sign.
4. **Match format.** For multi-select items, count how many answers are required. For fill-ins, compare the typed answer with the paper answer character by character.
5. **Commit and release.** Click Next and do not think about that question again.

**Time caps**

- Planning average: about 1.8 minutes per question across the whole test (180 minutes / 100 questions).
- Soft cap on a Math question: 2:30. At that point decide whether you are one step from done.
- Hard cap: 3:30. Eliminate what you can, choose the best remaining answer, commit.
- Never leave a block of questions for rapid guessing at the end. Official guidance says students should not assume fast guessing to finish beats a real attempt on each item, so pacing must be steady from question 1.

### 2.4 CAT mindset rules

- **A harder question is normal and usually a good sign.** It means the previous answers were right.
- **An easier question is not a verdict.** The engine is narrowing its estimate; some items are also unscored field-test questions.
- **Do not try to read the difficulty.** Any attention spent guessing how the test is going is taken from the current problem.
- **Do not slow-play.** Deliberately missing or coasting does not help; answer each question as accurately as possible.
- **One miss does not sink the test.** The score reflects the whole pattern of responses.

### 2.5 Fill-in and TEI precision

The Kweller #58 error (171 entered, 1.71 correct) is exactly the kind of mistake that is permanent on a no-return test.

**Typed-answer routine**

1. Circle the final answer on paper, with units.
2. Ask: "Is this size reasonable?" (A price after discount should be less than the original; a probability is between 0 and 1.)
3. Type it. Read the screen aloud digit by digit, including the decimal point and negative sign.
4. Re-read the question's requested form (fraction vs. decimal, rounded or not).

**TEI types to rehearse** (seen in official sample material and described by prep providers): multi-select with two or more correct answers, drag-and-drop, dropdown, and fill-in with the equation editor. For every multi-select item, the first note on scratch paper is the number of answers required.

**On-screen tools to learn early:** highlighter, answer eliminator, notepad, digital pencil, line reader mask, bookmark (passage sets only), pop-up definitions for glossed words, and the equation editor (parentheses, square root, multiplication dot, fractions).

### 2.6 Time budget (starting proposal, to be tested in Phase 3)

| Block | Minutes | Per question |
|---|---|---|
| Math, 50 questions | 95 | ~1.9 min |
| ELA, 50 questions | 80 | ~1.6 min |
| Buffer | 5 | — |

- ELA is the stronger section, so it funds the extra Math time. Adjust after the first two full digital tests.
- **Section order:** try Math-first on one full test and ELA-first on the next, then keep whichever produces the better Math accuracy. Decide by the end of June and never change it afterward.

## 3. Math: What Must Be Fixed (Expanded)

For each area: the evidence from the original plan, the rules to own, the traps that create wrong answers, and one worked model.

A note on scope: one prep provider states that Math for 8th-grade testers draws on state standards through Grade 7. If that holds, arc length, sector area, and the coordinate distance formula are lower-yield than angles, area, volume, and circles. Keep them on the list, but teach them last within geometry.

### 3.1 Geometry — HIGHEST PRIORITY

**Evidence:** Kweller Angles & Polygons 2/6 (#58, #82, #94, #105), Circles 0/1 (#62); ibidPREP Volume 0/2, Geometric Principles 0/1, Area 2/3.

**Rules to own**

- Interior angle sum of an n-gon: (n − 2) × 180. Exterior angles always total 360. Each exterior angle of a regular n-gon: 360 / n.
- Straight line = 180; around a point = 360; vertical angles are equal.
- Parallel lines cut by a transversal: corresponding and alternate interior angles are equal; same-side interior angles sum to 180.
- Triangle: angles sum to 180; an exterior angle equals the sum of the two remote interior angles; the longest side is opposite the largest angle; any side is less than the sum of the other two.
- Circle: C = 2πr = πd; A = πr².
- Rectangular prism: V = lwh; SA = 2(lw + lh + wh). Cube: V = s³; SA = 6s².
- Cylinder: V = πr²h; SA = 2πr² + 2πrh.
- Triangle area = ½bh (height perpendicular to base). Trapezoid = ½(b₁ + b₂)h. Parallelogram = bh.
- Lower priority: arc length = (θ/360) × 2πr; sector area = (θ/360) × πr²; slope = rise/run; midpoint = average of coordinates.

**Traps**

- Using diameter where the formula needs radius (and the reverse).
- Using a slanted side as the height.
- Answering with the interior angle when the exterior was asked, or with one angle when the sum was asked.
- Forgetting that scaling lengths by k scales area by k² and volume by k³.
- Leaving π out, or multiplying it out, when the answer choices keep π.
- Mixed units (inches and feet in the same figure).

**Worked model.** Each exterior angle of a regular polygon measures 40°. How many sides does it have, and what is each interior angle?
Sides = 360 / 40 = 9. Interior = 180 − 40 = 140°. Check: (9 − 2) × 180 = 1260, and 1260 / 9 = 140. ✔

**Worked model.** A cylinder has radius 3 and height 5. Volume = π × 3² × 5 = 45π.
Diameter trap: if the problem had said "diameter 6," the answer is still 45π; using 6 as the radius gives 180π, which will be an answer choice.

**The diagram habit.** Before any calculation: redraw the figure on paper, mark every given, mark every equal angle or side, and write the name of the hidden formula next to it.

### 3.2 Percent, Rates, and Proportions

**Evidence:** Kweller Percent 3/6 (#60, #67, #104), Proportional Relationships 5/7 (#63, #96); ibidPREP Rates 1/2 and 1/2, Fractions 1/2.

**Rules to own**

- Percent change = (new − old) / old × 100. The denominator is always the starting value.
- Multiplier method: increase by p% means multiply by (1 + p/100); decrease means multiply by (1 − p/100).
- Successive changes multiply; they never add.
- Reverse percent: original = final / multiplier.
- Rate × time = distance (or work). Average speed = total distance / total time.
- Unit conversion: multiply by fractions equal to 1 and cancel units on paper.
- Combined ratios: make the shared term match.

**Traps**

- Adding successive percents (up 25% then down 20% is not up 5%).
- Taking 20% of the sale price to "undo" a 20% discount.
- Averaging two speeds instead of using total distance over total time.
- Answering with the percent when the amount was asked, or the part when the whole was asked.
- Confusing "percent of" with "percent greater than."

**Worked models**

- Reverse percent: after a 20% discount the price is $48. Original = 48 / 0.80 = $60. Check: 20% of 60 is 12, and 60 − 12 = 48. ✔
- Successive change: up 25%, then down 20%. 1.25 × 0.80 = 1.00, so no net change.
- Units: 30 mi/hr = 30 × 5280 ft / 3600 sec = 44 ft/sec.
- Average speed: 60 mph out and 40 mph back over the same distance. Use 120 miles each way: 2 hr + 3 hr = 5 hr for 240 miles, so 48 mph (not 50).
- Three-part ratio: a:b = 2:3 and b:c = 4:5. Make b = 12, giving a:b:c = 8:12:15.
- Work: one pipe fills a tank in 6 hours, another in 3. Combined rate = 1/6 + 1/3 = 1/2 tank per hour, so 2 hours.

### 3.3 Equation and Expression Accuracy

**Evidence:** Kweller Basic Equations & Inequalities 4/7 (#102, #108, #112), Basic Operations 8/11 (#71, #85, #98), Advanced Algebra 4/4; ibidPREP Equations 2/3, Expressions 3/5, Words to Math 2/3, Sequences 1/2.

**Diagnosis:** precision gap, not a conceptual gap (Advanced Algebra was 4/4). On a no-return test, precision errors cost exactly as much as conceptual ones, and they cannot be caught later.

**Habits**

- One operation per line. No two-step mental moves.
- When distributing a negative, write the sign change explicitly: −(3x − 4) becomes −3x + 4.
- Multiplying or dividing an inequality by a negative flips the sign.
- Translation table: "5 less than x" is x − 5; "5 is less than x" is 5 < x; "is" means equals; "of" means multiply; "per" means divide.
- Arithmetic sequence: aₙ = a₁ + (n − 1)d. Geometric sequence: aₙ = a₁ × rⁿ⁻¹.
- **Mandatory:** substitute the answer back before clicking Next.
- Answer the question asked. If the question wants 2x + 1, solving for x is only step one.

**Worked models**

- −3x + 5 > 20 → −3x > 15 → x < −5. Check with x = −6: 18 + 5 = 23 > 20. ✔
- Sequence 7, 11, 15, … : 20th term = 7 + 19 × 4 = 83.

### 3.4 Fill-In Precision and Number Sense

**Evidence:** Kweller #58 (171 vs. 1.71); ibidPREP misses on Median, Square Roots, Integers, Probability.

**Rules and habits**

- Perfect squares through 20² from memory; cubes through 6³.
- Simplify roots by pulling out the largest perfect-square factor: √72 = 6√2.
- Median: sort first. With an even count, average the two middle values (3, 7, 8, 12 → 7.5).
- Mean = total / count, so total = mean × count. Most "missing score" problems use this.
- Sign rules and order of operations: −3² = −9, but (−3)² = 9.
- Probability: "and" multiplies, "or" adds (subtract the overlap). Without replacement, the denominator shrinks.
- Worked model: a bag has 3 red and 2 blue marbles. Two reds in a row without replacement = 3/5 × 2/4 = 3/10.

### 3.5 Error taxonomy (use in the error log)

| Code | Meaning | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| C | Concept: did not know the rule | Re-teach, add to formula sheet, 10 new problems |
| S | Setup: knew the rule, built the wrong equation | Translation drills; draw and label |
| X | Execution: arithmetic, sign, or copying error | One-line-per-step; substitution check |
| Q | Question: answered something other than what was asked | "Last line first" habit |
| E | Entry: wrong click, wrong typed value, wrong number of selections | Typed-answer routine |
| T | Time: ran past the hard cap or rushed | Time-cap drills |

On a CAT, codes X, Q, and E are the cheapest points to recover. Expect them to be the majority of early misses.

## 4. One-Page Formula Sheet (Student Rewrites This by Hand)

The student writes this from memory every Sunday in Phase 1 until it is perfect three weeks in a row, then monthly.

- Angles: line 180, point 360, triangle 180, n-gon (n − 2) × 180, exterior total 360
- Circle: C = 2πr, A = πr²
- Areas: rectangle lw, triangle ½bh, parallelogram bh, trapezoid ½(b₁ + b₂)h
- Solids: prism V = lwh, SA = 2(lw + lh + wh); cube s³ and 6s²; cylinder πr²h and 2πr² + 2πrh
- Scaling: lengths × k → area × k², volume × k³
- Percent change = (new − old) / old; original = final / multiplier
- d = rt; average speed = total distance / total time
- Sequences: a₁ + (n − 1)d; a₁ × rⁿ⁻¹
- Mean = total / count; median = middle after sorting
- Conversions: 1 mi = 5280 ft, 1 hr = 3600 sec, 1 ft = 12 in, 1 yd = 3 ft
- Squares 1–20; roots of common squares

## 6. Phase 2 — Mixed Practice Under CAT Rules (Feb 1 – May 2, 2027)

**Time:** ~3.5 hrs/week Math, 45 min/week ELA.

### 6.1 Core session design

- Mixed sets of 20–25 problems. The student writes the topic name next to each problem before solving.
- Every session includes at least 5 typed fill-ins and 2 multi-select items.
- "Solve before looking at the choices" on every multiple-choice problem.
- All sets are no-return. On paper, this means covering finished work and using pen.

### 6.2 Difficulty ladder (simulates adaptive pressure)

Sort practice problems into three tiers using the source's difficulty labels or the tutor's judgment.

| Tier | Description | Share of each set in Feb | Share by April |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | One-step, direct rule use | 30% | 10% |
| 2 | Two to three steps, standard word problems | 50% | 50% |
| 3 | Multi-concept, heavy wording, or unusual setup | 20% | 40% |

**Streak sets (once a week).** Ten Tier 2 questions in a row; the goal is 10/10. Any miss restarts the count on a fresh set at the next session. This builds the consistency that lets the engine move a student up and keep him there.

**Hard-run sets (once a week from March).** Eight Tier 3 questions back to back with the time caps enforced. The goal is composure and good decisions at the hard cap, not a perfect score.

### 6.3 Alternating weeks

- **Week A:** one full timed 50-question Math section (95 minutes, no return).
- **Week B:** targeted review of the two weakest topics from Week A, plus streak and hard-run sets.

### 6.4 Monthly emphasis

| Month | Emphasis |
|---|---|
| February | Topic identification; Tier 2 automaticity; geometry and percent spiral |
| March | Multi-step word problems; Tier 3 exposure; time-cap discipline |
| April | Full sections every other week; entry errors driven to zero |

### 6.5 Checkpoints

- First week of March: update cutoff references and targets after the March 3 offer release.
- Late April: **official practice test (first one), full length, digital.** Target: Math 40/50 (80%), ELA 46/50.
- X, Q, and E errors combined: no more than 3 per 50-question section by the end of April.

## 7. Phase 3 — Full Tests and Timing (May 3 – Aug 29, 2027)

**Time:** ~4 hrs/week during school, 5–6 hrs/week in July and August.

### 7.1 Rationing the official practice tests

The official portal offers four full online practice tests. One prep provider's analysis reports that these are fixed forms (the same questions each time, not adaptive) and that they reuse questions from the 2018–19 and 2022–23 handbook forms. Two consequences:

- They are the best rehearsal for the **interface and navigation**, not for adaptivity.
- Do not use printed handbook forms from those two years as practice before taking the matching official online test, or the score will be inflated.

| Official test | When | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| First | Late April | Phase 2 exit check |
| Second | Late June | Section-order decision |
| Third | Late August | Phase 3 exit check |
| Fourth | Mid-October | Final dress rehearsal |

If NYC Public Schools releases new practice tests with the fall 2027 guide, add them in September and October.

### 7.2 Test cycle (every two weeks)

| Day | Task |
|---|---|
| Sat (week 1) | Full 100-question test, 180 minutes, one sitting, on a computer, at the same time of day as a real administration |
| Sun | Score it; log every miss with a code; note time used per section |
| Mon–Thu | Re-do every miss cold, then a second problem of the same type |
| Sat (week 2) | Two targeted sets: weakest topic (25 problems) and a hard-run set |
| Sun | Formula sheet from memory; streak set |

Other full tests come from third-party digital platforms, ideally at least a few that actually adapt question difficulty, so that the feeling of questions getting harder is familiar before test day.

### 7.3 Converting older 57-question material

- Use percent correct, not raw score.
- For timing, give 1.9 minutes per Math question and 1.6 per ELA question.
- Enforce no-return by hand.

### 7.4 Summer push (July – August)

- Geometry and percent to 90%+ accuracy on mixed Tier 2/Tier 3 sets.
- Two "stamina" sessions: Math section immediately after a full ELA section, since fatigue errors show up in whichever section comes second.
- Lock the section order and the time budget by the end of June; rehearse only that plan afterward.

### 7.5 Targets by late August

- Math 44/50 (88%) on a fixed-form digital test; ELA 46–47/50.
- Tier 2 accuracy 95%+; Tier 3 accuracy 70%+.
- Zero questions answered by rushed guessing in the final ten minutes.

- **Pacing rule (replaces the original):** every question gets a real attempt inside the time caps, every answer is checked before Next, and the section is paced so that the last ten questions get the same care as the first ten.
- Re-read the official fall 2027 guide as soon as it is published and adjust for any format changes.
- Register within the official window. For reference, fall 2026 registration ran October 6–30 and testing ran November 14–21; fall 2027 dates were not published at the time of writing.

| Days out | Task |
|---|---|
| 10–8 | Last full test (fourth official test if not yet used); full review |
| 7–5 | Error-log review only: re-do the 30 most recent misses cold |
| 4–2 | Formula sheet daily; one 15-question streak set per day; one ELA passage set per day |

## 9. ELA Maintenance (Adapted for Passage Blocks)

**Evidence:** Kweller misses only #19 (Summary) and #49 (Detail); ibidPREP Fiction 6/9 (Theme, Plot Development, Purpose), Poetry 7/9 (Analysis, Purpose), Grammar/Editing 10/11 (Concluding Sentence).

**What the CAT changes for ELA**

- A passage and its questions arrive as a block. Answers can be changed inside the block, but the block locks when submitted.
- One provider's review of the official practice tests found seven passage sets per test (six reading, one revising/editing), five to nine questions per set, and three to four stand-alone revising/editing questions. Set sizes vary, so do not expect a fixed number of questions per passage.
- Stand-alone revising/editing items follow Math rules: answer, then no return.

**Weekly assignment (45 min)**

- One fiction or poetry passage (e.g., the Anna Karenina excerpt). Write a one-sentence theme statement **before** answering questions.
- One revising/editing passage (e.g., Game Testing). Find and fix every error before reading the questions.
- Two or three stand-alone editing items under no-return rules.
- Review any miss: misread the question, misread the passage, or attractive wrong answer?

**Block-submit checklist (use on every passage set from Phase 2)**

1. Every question answered.
2. Bookmarked questions revisited.
3. For "best supports" and "summary" questions, re-read the chosen option against the passage once more.
4. Submit, and let it go.

**Targeted work on the known soft spots**

- Fiction: for each passage, note who wants what, what stands in the way, and what changes by the end. Theme = the general lesson of that change.
- Poetry: paraphrase each stanza in one plain sentence; mark the line where the tone turns.
- Summary questions: the right answer covers the whole passage and its main conclusion; wrong answers are usually true details.
- Concluding-sentence questions: the right answer restates the main claim without adding a new idea.

### 10.3 Process metrics (reviewed monthly)

- X + Q + E errors per 50 questions (goal: 3 or fewer by April, 1 or fewer by September)
- Questions that hit the hard cap per section (goal: 4 or fewer)
- Longest streak of consecutive correct Tier 2 answers
- Minutes left when each section ends (goal: 0–5; a large surplus means rushing)

### 10.4 Decision rules

- **Ahead of target by 3+ points at a checkpoint:** shift 30 minutes per week from review to Tier 3 problems.
- **Behind by 1–3 points:** keep the calendar; add one extra streak set per week.
- **Behind by 4+ points:** pause new material for two weeks and re-run the two weakest topics from Phase 1.
- **ELA drops below 90% on two tests in a row:** raise ELA to 75 minutes per week until it recovers.
