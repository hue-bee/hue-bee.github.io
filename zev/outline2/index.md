## Details for the Priority Areas

**Algebra.** Zev should be able to solve a multi-step equation, simplify expressions with negatives and parentheses, and rearrange formulas such as x in terms of y. Check each answer by substituting it back in. Mastery means 5/5 on a fresh set of five algebra questions with every step written out.

**Geometry and measurement.** For every question he should draw the figure, label the known lengths, and split composite shapes into rectangles and triangles. Common errors to drill are mixing up area and perimeter, forgetting to subtract a cut-out region, and unit mismatches.

**Percents and word problems.** Teach him to turn "percent of" into multiplication and to identify the base for percent change. Question 6, a percents question, cost him almost six minutes. Set a rule that after about 2.5 minutes he marks the question, moves on and comes back later.

**ELA inference and supporting ideas.** Require him to underline the line number that supports each answer before he commits to a choice. He should reject answer choices that are too extreme, off-topic or unsupported. For supporting-idea questions he should ask what claim the detail supports. Use the 20s and 25s inference answers as examples of the quick guessing to avoid. He should spend at least about 45 seconds on every ELA question.

## Pacing and Tracking

- Keep a one-page error log with the question, the category and why he missed it (careless, content or timing). Review it at the start of every session.
- Time targets: ELA about 1m30s per question, Math about 1m50s, with a checkpoint at the halfway mark of each section.
- Success benchmark for the November mock: ELA 40+/50 and Math 38+/50, which is 78+ raw. That would be a gain of about 9 points over his current 69.
- Avoid new test material in the final week. Review his own errors instead.
