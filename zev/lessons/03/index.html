Zone 3 · percent change38:30–40:30

Change ÷ original. Never ÷ new.

Which value owns 100% now?

Percent change = (change ÷ original) × 100%

Draw two journeys

$80 → $100: original base is $80.
$100 → $80: original base is $100.
Keep the change magnitude separate from its direction.

Zone 3 · Level 3 · successive percents43:00–45:00

Two changes need two factors

A quantity rises by 20%, then falls by 20%. Is the ending value the same as the starting value?

Does the second percent see a new whole?

What amount does the second 20% act on: the starting quantity or the increased quantity?

Ending = start × first factor × second factor

Do not cancel the percent signs

Equal-looking rates do not undo each other when their bases differ.

Zone 3 · reverse percents45:00–47:00

Reverse the machine, not the percent

A jacket costs $60 after 25% off. What was its original price?

What does $60 represent?

Is $60 the 25% removed, the 75% kept, or the 100% original? Which equation matches your interpretation?

New = original × factor
Original = new ÷ factor

Think of an undo button

Forward change multiplies by the percent kept. Reversing that operation divides by the same factor.

Do not add 25% of $60

That uses the sale price as the base. The discount was based on the original price.

Guided path 3 of 3 · Level 447:00–50:00

Follow the dollars in order

A jacket is discounted 15%, then taxed 8% on the discounted price. The total is $110.16. Find the original price P.

1

Track both bases

After the discount: 0.85P. The tax acts on this reduced amount, not on P.

2

Chain the factors

3

Undo, then verify

Set up P = 110.16 ÷ (0.85 × 1.08). Finish the arithmetic; run both changes forward to check.
