Core Rule

An Inference Is a Bridge, Not a Leap

Think about it
If an answer feels reasonable but no sentence points toward it, is it an inference—or a possibility you invented?
Inference = Text clue + logical connection
Both pieces are required.
SHSAT Trap: “Could Be True”
The test asks what the passage supports, not what could happen in real life. Possibility is wider than proof.
Three-Pass Method

Lens → Lock → Trial

Pass 1 · Lens
Translate the question
What must be proved: a character belief, a cause, an attitude, or a claim-detail relationship?
Pass 2 · Lock
Mark the smallest proof zone
Name the line, sentence, or image before looking for a polished answer choice.
Pass 3 · Trial
Put each choice on trial
Ask: “Which exact words make this choice necessary—or at least best supported?”
A clever shortcut
Treat the passage like a witness. Your answer choice is the claim; the text must testify for it.
Choice Filter

Four Distractors That Sound Better Than They Prove

Extreme
Adds “always,” “never,” “completely,” or a sweeping judgment the text never earns.
Off-topic
Accurate in general, but answers a different question from the one asked.
Unsupported
Plausible from life experience; missing a textual anchor.
Half-right
Begins with a true detail, then attaches a cause, motive, or conclusion not proved.
Which error is hardest to notice?
A half-right choice can quote the passage accurately. Where does its reasoning stop matching the text?
Calibration · Easy to Medium

Name the Proof Before the Choice

1Level 1
Mira placed the repaired radio on the windowsill, turned the dial twice, and smiled when music filled the room.
Which detail most directly shows that the repair succeeded?
2Level 2
When the meeting ended, Jonah stayed behind to straighten every chair, though no one had asked him to do so.
What can reasonably be inferred about Jonah?
AHe dislikes meetings of every kind.
BHe takes responsibility without needing recognition.
CHe was ordered to clean the room earlier.
DHe hopes to become the group’s leader.
Verbal proof frame
“The text says ________. That detail suggests ________ because ________.” Do not name a letter until all three blanks can be filled.
Supporting Ideas

A Detail Has a Job: Which Claim Does It Hold Up?

Do not ask only: “What does the detail say?”
Ask: “Why did the author spend space on this detail? Which nearby claim would weaken if the detail disappeared?”
Claim = roof
Supporting ideas are the beams carrying its weight.
Removal test
Delete the detail mentally. Which claim loses evidence? That claim is probably the detail’s purpose.

Prediction before reading

Nonfiction · Passage

The Map That Listened

1For years, Riverton’s transit planners measured bus service by counting how many riders boarded at each stop.
2The totals showed where buses were crowded, but not why some residents avoided the system altogether.
3A nearly empty stop could signal low demand—or a sidewalk too narrow for a wheelchair, a route that ended before a night shift, or a crossing people considered unsafe.
4The numbers recorded movement; they did not record obstacles.
5Planner Lena Ortiz proposed a second map built from short rider interviews.
6Volunteers asked one question: “What makes this trip difficult?”
7They attached each response to the location it described, creating clusters of comments around dangerous corners, missing shelters, and gaps in late-night service.
8At first, some officials dismissed the comments as too subjective to guide expensive changes.
9Yet the new map repeatedly revealed barriers that boarding totals had hidden.
10It also complicated simple solutions: adding a bus did little if riders still could not reach the stop safely.
11Ortiz did not argue that stories should replace statistics.
12Instead, she called the two maps “a pair of eyes”: one showed the pattern, and the other brought the pattern into focus.
Read twice: first for the argument’s shape; second for the line that proves each answer.
Nonfiction · Questions

Direct Detail → Reasonable Inference

3Level 1
Which line most directly states the limitation of the original map?
Precision check
Choose the line that states the limitation—not merely an example of it.
4Level 2
What can be inferred about the officials’ initial standard for useful evidence?
AThey preferred evidence that seemed measurable and objective.
BThey believed riders were deliberately hiding information.
CThey wanted interviews to replace all numerical records.
DThey had already planned every needed improvement.
Before committing to Question 4
Which word in line 8 reveals the officials’ reason for doubt? Which choices exaggerate beyond that reason?
Nonfiction · Guided Solution 1

Supporting-Idea Question: Use the Removal Test

5Level 3
Lines 3 and 7 mainly support which claim in the passage?
A. Empty stops should be removed from every route.
B. Rider comments can expose barriers that boarding counts miss.
C. Volunteers understand transit better than planners do.
D. Late-night routes are the city’s only serious concern.
Step 1
Compress both details
Line 3 lists hidden reasons for low use. Line 7 maps reported barriers. Write their shared idea in 7 words or fewer.
Step 2
Run the removal test
If lines 3 and 7 vanished, which answer choice would lose its strongest support?
Step 3
Check the size of the claim
Cross out any option that says “every,” declares one group superior, or reduces several barriers to one.
Stop before naming the letter; give the proof aloud first.
Nonfiction · Guided Solution 2 Select Two

Hard Inference: Prove Both Halves

6Level 4
Select the TWO statements best supported by the passage.
□Ortiz views stories and statistics as complementary.
□A service change can fail when access problems remain.
□Personal reports are always more reliable than counts.
□Officials refused to use the interview map.
□The project proved that low demand never exists.
Step 1 · Split
Treat this as five true/false trials
Do not compare choices yet. Demand an independent line-lock for each statement.
Step 2 · Pair
Write one line beside each survivor
A claim about the two evidence types needs one proof zone; a claim about access needs another.
Step 3 · Audit
Circle absolute language
Words such as “always,” “refused,” and “never” require equally absolute proof. Is that proof present?
Digital trap: selecting a pair because the choices sound related
Each selected statement must stand on its own evidence. A strong first selection cannot rescue an unsupported second one.
Poetry Shift

In Poetry, Evidence Often Lives in a Pattern

Think about it
One image can be ambiguous. What changes when several images point in the same emotional direction?
Poetry inference ladder
  1. Name the literal image.
  2. Notice the repeated contrast or movement.
  3. Infer the speaker’s attitude—carefully.
SHSAT Trap: One Dramatic Word
Do not let one word outweigh the poem’s full pattern. Tone comes from accumulation and shifts.
Poetry · Original Practice Text

Before the Store Opens

1My father lifts the metal gate
2while morning is still deciding
3whether to enter the street.
4The lock resists, then turns;
5dust wakes inside the window.
6He rights a leaning row of cans,
7faces every label outward,
8as if the shelves were an audience.
9I sweep yesterday toward the door—
10receipts, a button, one blue thread—
11but he saves the thread in his pocket.
12“Might mend something,” he says.
13Then sunlight reaches the counter,
14and every jar gives it back.
15The street has made up its mind.
16We turn the sign to OPEN.
First read: trace movement
What changes from resistance and dimness to order and light? Which small action reveals the father most clearly?
Poetry · Questions

Image → Pattern → Attitude

7Level 1
Which action most directly shows the father arranging the store carefully?
8Level 2
Why does the speaker say morning is “still deciding” whether to enter?
ATo suggest the street is dim and not yet active.
BTo show that the weather forecast is uncertain.
CTo prove the speaker wants the store to remain closed.
DTo explain why the gate cannot be lifted.
Poetry · Guided Solution 3

Hard Poetry Inference: Follow the Pattern, Not One Word

9Level 4
What do lines 9–16 most strongly suggest about the speaker’s developing view of the father’s habits?
A. The habits seem pointless until customers arrive.
B. The habits transform ordinary preparation into an act of care.
C. The habits prevent the speaker from finishing any work.
D. The habits reveal that the father fears throwing away every object.
Step 1
Track the turn
The speaker sweeps scraps out; the father saves one thread. What value might he see where the speaker first sees waste?
Step 2
Add the ending image
Light reaches the counter, jars reflect it, and the sign turns. Does the poem end in frustration, fear, or changed appreciation?
Step 3
Reject the narrow trap
A choice focused only on keeping objects must also explain the light, order, and opening. If it cannot, it is incomplete.
Give the line pair and logical link before choosing.

Exit Sprint · 5 Minutes

One Choice. One Line. One Because.

10Level 3
After weeks of refusing help, Imani finally set the heavy box down beside Luis. She did not ask him to lift it. Instead, she stepped back just far enough to leave one handle open.
What is most reasonably inferred about Imani at this moment?
A. She has completely overcome her reluctance to accept help.
B. She is cautiously making room for help without requesting it directly.
C. She expects Luis to carry the box alone from now on.
D. She wants Luis to notice that the box is empty.
Final verbal check
Which choice captures both parts of the behavior: her earlier resistance and her new, indirect opening?
Session Wrap-Up

What We Locked In Today

Inference Bridge
A conclusion needs a specific clue and a logical connection. Plausibility alone is not proof.
Supporting-Idea Job
A detail earns its place by strengthening a claim. Use the removal test to identify that claim.
Choice Filter
Remove extreme, off-topic, unsupported, and half-right answers before choosing the best-supported one.
Digital Habit
Within each passage set, verify every response before submission; do not change without textual cause.
