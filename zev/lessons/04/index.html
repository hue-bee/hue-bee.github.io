0:10–0:12 · Sort every actual miss

Name the First Broken Link

C · Careless

Execution broke

You can explain the method independently, but copied, computed, read, or entered something incorrectly.

Probe: Can you locate the first mismatch without receiving a hint?

M · Content

The model was missing

You cannot justify the relationship, rule, or inference even after the clock is removed.

Probe: What fact or structure would you need to know?

T · Timing

The decision process broke

You can solve it independently, but the timed process became a stall or an unsupported rush.

Probe: Which action would have changed the time cost?

One primary cause; an optional secondary tag

0:12–0:13 · Choose the right repair

Same Skill, Different Demands

Level 1

Recognize the structure

Identify the quantity, relationship, or explicit evidence before calculation or interpretation.

Level 2

Connect the steps

Translate a condition, maintain units, or link a claim to a specific detail.

Level 3

Choose an efficient route

Use a boundary, invariant, comparison, or alternative representation to avoid extra work.

Level 4

Defend and transfer

Handle competing interpretations, layered constraints, or a new version of the same structure.

These are instructional levels matching the template’s four-level system—not official calibrated CAT difficulty labels.

0:16–0:18 · Inequality repair

Use the Route With Fewer Sign Risks

Form B · 70Level 3

Determine all values of x that satisfy:

2x + 11 > 3x + 9

Questions that expose the method

  • Can you keep the variable coefficient positive?
  • Which operations preserve order? Which reverse it?
  • How could one value on each side of your boundary test your result?
Clever explanation: order is a relationship

Moving terms is shorthand for performing the same operation on both sides. A sign flip belongs to multiplication or division by a negative—not to every subtraction.

0:21–0:24 · Geometry and dimensional reasoning

A Hole Is a Three-Dimensional Object

Form B · 58Level 4

An 18-millimeter cube becomes a bead when a square tunnel is cut straight through it. The tunnel’s square opening has side length 6 millimeters. Determine the remaining volume.

Which length does the tunnel travel through? How do you know that subtracting only an opening’s area cannot work?

Clever explanation: follow one cross-section through the solid

A constant cross-section swept through a length creates volume. Track the units at every stage, then check that the result is positive and smaller than the original cube.

Read for Learning and Responsibility

4It took many trials before I learned how to knot my sinew thread on the point of my finger, as I saw her do. Then the next difficulty was in keeping my thread stiffly twisted, so that I could easily string my beads upon it. My mother required of me original designs for my lessons in beading. At first I frequently ensnared many a sunny hour into working a long design. Soon I learned from self-inflicted punishment to refrain from drawing complex patterns, for I had to finish whatever I began.

5After some experience I usually drew easy and simple crosses and squares. These were some of the set forms. My original designs were not always symmetrical nor sufficiently characteristic, two faults with which my mother had little patience. The quietness of her oversight made me feel strongly responsible and dependent upon my own judgment. She treated me as a dignified little individual as long as I was on my good behavior; and how humiliated I was when some boldness of mine drew forth a rebuke from her!

Mark selectively

  • One obstacle in the work
  • One change in the child’s choices
  • One detail about the mother’s guidance

Sinew: a strong natural fiber used as thread. Oversight: supervision.
