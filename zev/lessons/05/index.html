The Unfinished Sign

1Mara had promised a new sign for the neighborhood repair table. She had imagined blue letters marching evenly across a white board. Now, with the library opening in twenty minutes, the word REPAIR leaned uphill. The last R crowded the edge. She laid a brush across the letters as if it could hold them in place.

2Her grandfather arrived carrying a cardboard box of clocks. He set it down without examining the sign. “Where should these go?” he asked. Mara pointed toward the far end of the table. “Maybe the sign can go there too,” she said, “behind the box.”

3He lifted the brush. Beneath it, a wet streak joined the two words she had painted: REPAIR TOGETHER. “The table has one crooked leg,” he said. “Will we hide that as well?” Mara glanced at the folded cardboard already tucked beneath the leg. He was not smiling, but he waited.

4A boy appeared with a toy truck whose wheel hung sideways. “Is this the place?” he asked. Mara started to explain that the sign was unfinished. Instead, she pulled out a chair. When he set the truck down, a loose screw rolled toward her grandfather, who trapped it beneath one finger.

5By opening time, two neighbors were leaning over a lamp, the boy was sorting screws, and Mara had found a thinner brush. She could not straighten the entire word without painting the board again. She added a small line beneath TOGETHER and carried the sign to the front of the table.

6“Higher?” her grandfather asked, holding one end. Mara tilted her head. From the doorway, the blue letters still climbed. Behind them, the boy held his truck while a neighbor fitted the wheel. “No,” she said. “There.” She left her brush beside the open box of tools.

Pause & explain
Who wants what? What gets in the way? At which action—not just thought—does Mara begin to respond differently?
