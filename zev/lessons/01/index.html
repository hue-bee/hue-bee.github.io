Expressions · Anatomy

An Expression Is a Sentence Without an Equals Sign

−3x + 5 + 2x − 7
Terms are chunks separated by addition or subtraction. A sign belongs to the term immediately after it. Treat “−3x” as one chunk, not “3x with a loose minus nearby.”
SHSAT trap: combining neighbors
Terms combine by mathematical identity, not by physical closeness. An x-term can “see” another x-term across constants.
Expressions · Distribution

The Outside Factor Is a Delivery Truck

Think first
If −2 stands outside (x − 4), how many deliveries must it make? What happens when a negative factor meets a negative term?
a(b + c) = ab + ac
Every term inside gets exactly one copy of the outside factor.
Danger step
Students often distribute to the first term and abandon the second. Draw two arrows before multiplying anything.
Worked Solution 1 · Level 4

Distribute a Negative Without Losing a Sign

Simplify: 3 − 2(x − 4)
Step 1 · Attach the sign
3 + (−2)(x − 4)
Rewrite subtraction as adding a negative. Now the factor’s sign cannot drift away.
Step 2 · Make both deliveries
3 − 2x + 8
The negative factor multiplies both terms; negative times negative becomes positive.
Step 3 · Combine constants
11 − 2x
Only 3 and 8 are like terms. Keep the x-term separate.
Fast verification
Choose an easy value such as x = 0. The original and simplified expressions must produce the same output.

Equations · Balance

An Equation Is a Promise of Equality

The legal-move test
If you add 6 only to the left side, is the promise still true? What must happen on the right side to preserve equality?
left side = right side
Do the same operation to both sides, and the equation remains balanced.
Worked Solution 2 · Level 4

Variables on Both Sides: Choose the Cleaner Direction

Solve: 4(2x − 3) − 5 = 3(x + 7)
Step 1 · Clear parentheses
8x − 17 = 3x + 21
Distribute first, then combine constants on the left. Do not move terms while parentheses remain.
Step 2 · Collect variables
5x − 17 = 21
Subtract 3x from both sides. This direction leaves a positive coefficient.
Step 3 · Isolate and verify
x = 38/5
Add 17, divide by 5, then substitute 38/5 into both original sides.
Why a fraction is not a warning sign
SHSAT answers need not be integers. A clean fraction can be correct; verify it instead of “fixing” it into a friendlier number.

Formula Rearrangement

Make the Target Variable the VIP

Think in layers
In P = 2L + 2W, what is attached to W? Which attachment is outermost: adding 2L, or multiplying W by 2?
Undo outside layers first. The equation acts like nested wrapping around the target. Remove the outer operation from both sides, then the next one.
Do not “move” terms mysteriously
A term never crosses the equals sign and magically changes sign. You perform the same inverse operation on both sides.
Worked Solution 3 · Level 4

Rearrange Without Guessing Which Term “Moves”

Solve P = 2L + 2W for W
Step 1 · Name the target
Target: W
W is multiplied by 2 and then added to 2L. Keep your eyes on its operation chain.
Step 2 · Remove the outer layer
P − 2L = 2W
Subtract 2L from both sides. This is a legal inverse operation, not a sign-changing shortcut.
Step 3 · Remove the multiplier
W = (P − 2L)/2
Divide every term on both sides by 2. The target now stands alone.
Structure check
Substitute the new expression for W into the original formula. The right side should simplify back to P.
