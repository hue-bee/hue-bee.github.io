# SHSAT Outline — Fall 2026 Administration (Admission for Sept. 2027)

## Test at a Glance

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| Sections | ELA and Math |
| Questions | 50 per section (100 total) |
| Time | 180 minutes total, self-managed (no scheduled break) |
| Delivery | Digital on DOE-provided computers; **computer-adaptive at the item level** (new as of fall 2026) |
| Order | Student chooses whether to start with ELA or Math |
| Calculator | Not permitted |
| Scoring | Each scored item = 1 raw point regardless of difficulty; raw → scaled per section → composite; no wrong-answer penalty |
| Navigation | Math: forward-only. ELA: review permitted only inside a passage set until it is submitted; stand-alone ELA items are forward-only. |

Grade 8 test forms draw on NYC curriculum through Grade 7; Grade 9 forms draw on material through Grade 8.

## Part 1 — English Language Arts (50 questions)

Aligned to the NYS Next Generation ELA Standards. Three question families.

### A. Reading Comprehension (the bulk of the section)
Literary and informational passages, each with a question set.

Informational text types:
- expository/explanatory writing
- argument and opinion pieces, speeches, personal essays
- essays about art or literature
- biography, memoir, journalism
- historical, scientific, technical, or economic accounts for a general audience

Literary text types:
- poetry
- adventure, historical fiction, mystery, science fiction, realistic fiction
- myths, creative nonfiction

Skills tested:
- central idea, summary, and paraphrase
- inference and text-based evidence selection ("which sentence best supports…")
- word/phrase meaning in context and tone or connotation
- author's purpose, point of view, and rhetorical choices
- text structure and the effect of organization
- interpretation of accompanying graphics
- Glossed words appear with a dotted underline and a pop-up definition

### B. Revising/Editing Part A — Passage-Based
A numbered-sentence passage on history, current events, people/places/technology, or biological, physical, and social science. Errors to identify and fix:
- language misuse and imprecise word choice
- missing or unnecessary supporting detail
- missing or inappropriate transitions
- weak or absent introduction or conclusion
- illogical organization (add, delete, move, or replace a sentence)

### C. Revising/Editing Part B — Stand-Alone Items
One sentence, a list of sentences, or a short numbered paragraph. Tasks:
- select the best correction for an error
- identify which sentence contains the error
- combine sentences or revise a portion to clarify relationships between ideas

High-frequency grammar targets (NYS Grade 7 Language standards and earlier):
- verb tense consistency and shifts
- misplaced or dangling modifiers
- sentence structure: fragments, run-ons, faulty parallelism
- subject-verb and pronoun-antecedent agreement, pronoun clarity
- punctuation: commas, semicolons, colons, apostrophes, dashes
- conciseness, precision, and redundancy

### On-Screen ELA Tools
Highlighter, answer eliminator, notepad, digital pencil, line-reader mask, bookmark (within a passage set only), pop-up definitions for glossed words.

## Part 2 — Mathematics (50 questions)

Word problems and computational questions requiring familiar skills applied to complex, multi-step problems. Officially listed domains:

### 1. Ratios and Proportional Relationships
- ratio, rate, unit rate, and unit conversion
- proportions, scale drawings, similar figures
- percent: increase/decrease, discount, tax, tip, simple interest
- constant of proportionality; direct proportionality in tables, graphs, equations
- work, rate, and mixture problems

### 2. The Number System and Expressions
- integer and rational-number operations, absolute value
- fractions, decimals, and order of operations
- exponents, roots, scientific notation
- primes, factors, multiples, GCF and LCM, divisibility
- translating verbal descriptions into algebraic expressions
- simplifying and equivalent expressions; distributive property and factoring
- sequences, patterns, and consecutive-integer problems

### 3. Equations and Inequalities
- multi-step linear equations and inequalities
- systems of two linear equations (substitution and elimination)
- literal equations and formula rearrangement
- slope, intercepts, and graphing lines; linear functions
- number-line representation of inequality solutions

### 4. Statistics and Probability
- mean, median, mode, range; weighted averages and missing-value averages
- data displays: tables, bar and line graphs, scatterplots, box plots, histograms
- simple and compound probability, complements, independent events
- counting principle, basic combinations and permutations
- sampling and inference about populations

### 5. Geometry
- angle relationships: complementary, supplementary, vertical, parallel lines cut by a transversal
- triangle properties, angle sums, Pythagorean theorem
- perimeter, circumference, and area of polygons and circles
- volume and surface area of prisms, cylinders, cones, spheres
- coordinate geometry: distance, midpoint, plotting figures
- transformations, congruence, and similarity

No formula sheet is provided. Perimeter, area, volume, and surface-area formulas plus math vocabulary (e.g., parallel, perpendicular) must be memorized. Grid-in answers are entered with an Equation Editor palette, so practice entering parentheses, radicals, and the multiplication dot.

### On-Screen Math Tools
Highlighter, answer eliminator, notepad, digital pencil, line-reader mask, Equation Editor.


## Question Formats Across Both Sections

- Multiple choice (4 options)
- Fill-in-the-blank / grid-in (numeric entry)
- Technology-enhanced items (TEIs):
  - drag-and-drop ordering or matching
  - multiple-select (choose two or more; no partial credit)
  - inline drop-down menus inside a sentence or equation
  - number-line, graph, and point-plotting interactions

## Strategy Implications of the Adaptive Format

- The first item or passage set is of average difficulty; each response re-estimates the score and sets the difficulty of what comes next, so early accuracy shapes the rest of the section.
- Every scored item is worth one raw point, so harder items are not "worth more" — accuracy, not item selection, drives the score.
- The two-pass "skip the hard ones and return" strategy no longer works in Math. Build a commit-and-move habit: at roughly 90 seconds, eliminate, choose the strongest remaining option, advance.
- Never leave an answer blank; there is no penalty for guessing, and an answer is required to advance.
- Inside an ELA passage set, bookmark tough items, finish the set, then revisit before submitting — after submission the set locks.
- Budget time by passage set and by section in advance, since the clock is entirely self-managed across 180 minutes.
- Rehearse on the official NYC SHSAT Portal: the Student Readiness Tool tutorial plus the full-length online practice tests mirror the real interface and tools.


## Accommodations Notes

- Universal features for all students: zoom, highlighting, note-taking.
- English Language Learners and eligible former ELLs: 360 minutes (double time) with two 15-minute breaks after the first 180 minutes, translated directions in 12 languages, translated ELA footnotes, and paper bilingual glossaries.
- IEP/504 accommodations are delivered through the platform; paper exams are limited to students whose approved accommodations require them.
- Students who test in fall 2026 can sign up for a spring 2027 Test View to review the exam after scores are released.
