## What Changed: The Test Is Now Computer-Adaptive (CAT)
This is the most important contextual fact for the study plan. Beginning with the fall 2026 administration, the SHSAT has moved to a Computer-Adaptive Test (CAT) format: 100 total questions (50 ELA + 50 Math), 180 minutes, with no separate per-section time limit. NYC Public Schools has officially confirmed the 2026 school-day test date as **Wednesday, November 18, 2026**, giving Lucas roughly 10-11 weeks from the initial September 8 session.

Three format changes directly affect how Lucas should train:

- **No skip-and-return on standalone questions.** Once he submits an answer to a standalone grammar or math item, he cannot revisit it — eliminating his old "skip the hard ones and come back" strategy and making first-attempt accuracy and pacing discipline the primary skills to drill.
- **Passage-set flexibility remains.** Within a single reading-comprehension or revising/editing passage, he can move between questions tied to that passage until he leaves the set — so passage-level time management (not individual-question time management) is the real skill to build for ELA.
- **Difficulty responds to performance in real time**, meaning two students who get the same raw number correct can land on different scaled scores depending on how difficult the questions they reached were; sustained accuracy streaks matter more than previously, since early mistakes route him toward easier (lower-scoring) items.
- **Technology-enhanced items (TEIs)** — drag-and-drop reordering, multiple-select (no partial credit), inline dropdowns, and number-line/graph click interactions — now appear alongside standard multiple choice and require dedicated practice since they are unfamiliar interaction formats.

## Guiding Principles for Session Design
Given the parent's explicit notes on processing speed, focus, and forgetting, every session should follow four non-negotiable design rules:

- Sessions should open with a 5-minute "warm-up recall" of the prior session's core skill before introducing anything new, directly counteracting the forgetting pattern.
- New content should be chunked into 10-15 minute blocks with a change of activity or format (e.g., worked example, then timed mini-drill, then a game-style check) to sustain focus.
- Every math or grammar rule should be tied to a concrete, repeatable "trigger phrase" or mnemonic Lucas can recall instantly under adaptive-test pressure, since he cannot revisit standalone items once submitted.
- Incorporate light personal interest hooks (Minecraft resource ratios for proportion problems, piano-timing analogies for pacing drills, Animal Farm-based passages for reading practice) to increase engagement without diluting rigor.
## Phase 1 (Weeks 1-3, Sept 8 – Sept 28): Diagnostic Consolidation and Foundational Repair
The first phase re-establishes fluency in the specific sub-skills where the diagnostic data shows the weakest raw accuracy, while introducing CAT-specific pacing habits early so they become automatic well before test day.

**Math priorities (based on 6/25 to 12/25 minitest gap):**
- Order of operations (PEMDAS with nested grouping symbols and negative numbers) — directly tested in Handbook Q59, Q74, Q77.
- Fractions, decimals, and percent conversions, including percent-of-a-whole-number word problems (Handbook Q64, Q65, Q78).
- Ratio and proportion setups, including unit rate and cross-multiplication (Handbook Q76).
- Basic algebraic equations with one variable, including distributing and combining like terms (Handbook Q60, Q68, Q71).

**ELA priorities (based on stagnant 15/25 minitest ELA score):**
- Revising/Editing rules: comma usage in compound and complex sentences, subject-verb agreement, sentence-combining for clarity (Handbook Q1-Q9).
- Reading comprehension: main idea and author's purpose identification in informational texts, paragraph-function questions (Handbook "Cracking the Code" set).
- Timed single-passage drills (10-12 minutes per passage) to build the passage-set pacing skill that is now more valuable under CAT rules than skip-and-return tactics.

**CAT-specific habit building:** Introduce "no-skip" standalone-question discipline immediately — Lucas practices committing to an answer within a set time window (60-75 seconds for grammar/math) since the adaptive format removes the option to return. Also introduce technology-enhanced item formats (drag-and-drop sequencing, multiple-select with no partial credit) so the interaction style is familiar, not just the content.
## Phase 2 (Weeks 4-6, Sept 29 – Oct 19): Skill Expansion and Speed Building
With foundational gaps addressed, Phase 2 expands into the remaining tested sub-domains while steadily reducing time-per-question toward CAT pacing benchmarks (roughly 1.8 minutes per question on average across 100 questions in 180 minutes, though ELA passage sets and math word problems will need more, and grammar/algebra basics less).

**Math:** Geometry (angle relationships, triangle properties, perimeter/area), statistics (mean, median with removed data points as in Handbook Q62), integer and odd/even number properties (Handbook Q69-Q73), and multi-step word problems requiring two or more operations in sequence (Handbook Q72, Q79).

**ELA:** Literary passage comprehension (character motivation, tone, inference — since Lucas's favorite book, *Animal Farm*, is a strong bridge into inference-based questions), argument-structure questions in Revising/Editing Part B (identifying best supporting/concluding sentences, as in Handbook Q5-Q9), and vocabulary-in-context strategies.

**Timed simulation begins:** Starting week 5, run one 45-50 minute mixed-section mini-simulation per week (alternating ELA and Math) to build stamina and immediate-decision confidence under no-skip conditions.
## Phase 3 (Weeks 7-9, Oct 20 – Nov 9): Full Simulation and Error-Pattern Correction
This phase shifts from skill-building to performance optimization. Administer one full 180-minute CAT-style practice test (using the Tutorverse SHSAT book and Handbook materials as source questions, timed and administered without breaks longer than the real test allows) around week 7, followed by a detailed error-log review session dedicated entirely to categorizing mistakes as content gaps, careless errors, or time-pressure errors — each requiring a different fix.

- Content gaps get a targeted 15-minute re-teach using the original Handbook example as the anchor.
- Careless errors get a "read-twice, mark key words" habit drill.
- Time-pressure errors get additional pacing drills isolating just that question type.

Run a second full-length simulation around week 9, comparing raw accuracy and pacing directly against the week-7 result to confirm upward trend before tapering.
## Phase 4 (Weeks 10-11, Nov 10 – Nov 17): Taper, Confidence, and Logistics
The final stretch prioritizes retention and confidence over new content, consistent with Lucas's forgetting pattern — cramming new material this close to test day is counterproductive.

- Light daily review (15-20 minutes) rotating through previously mastered rules and formulas, not new topics.
- One final half-length timed section (25 questions) around November 12-13 to confirm pacing instincts are intact.
- A relaxed final session on November 16 or 17 focused on test-day logistics: confirming the November 18, 2026 test date and location, reviewing what to bring, and a short confidence-building review of his strongest skills to end on a high note.
- Reward planning: use the boba tea outing Lucas enjoys as a post-test celebration to reduce pre-test anxiety framing.
