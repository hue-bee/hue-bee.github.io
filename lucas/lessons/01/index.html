Zone 1 · Order of OperationsHighest Leverage

Grouping First, Then the Sign

Think about it
Look at −4 + 18 ÷ (−3) × 2. Most students who "know PEMDAS" still get this wrong. What's different about this problem compared to a problem with no negative numbers — and where exactly does a sign error sneak in?
Trigger Phrase — write this on Card #1
"Grouping first, then the sign."
The clever way in
Circle every grouping symbol first — parentheses, then exponents — before touching a single sign. Signs only get decided once the grouping is fully collapsed. This silently corrupts percent, ratio, and multi-step word problems if skipped.
Zone 1 · Practice · I do / We do / You do

Order of Operations — Easy to Hard

1Level 1 · I do
12 − 3 × (8 − 6)². What's inside the parentheses first? Then what happens to that result before anything is subtracted?
2Level 2 · We do
−4 + 18 ÷ (−3) × 2. There's no parentheses to simplify first this time — so what's the very first sign decision you have to make?
3Level 3 · You do
[2(7 − 9) + 10] ÷ (−2). There are two layers of grouping here — which one has to collapse first, and does the sign inside change once you multiply by 2?
4Level 4 · Stretch
Minecraft check: 4 iron ingots make 1 bucket. You mine 30 ingots. How many full buckets, and how many ingots are left over? Is this a grouping problem or a unit-rate problem — and how do you know?
SHSAT Trap: "Knowing PEMDAS" Isn't Enough
Nearly every miss on this skill is a sign error inside a correctly-ordered step, not an ordering error. Slow down at the exact moment a negative number meets a multiplication or division sign.
Zone 2 · The Comma Splice RuleOne Rule, Over-Learned

Two Complete Sentences Need Real Punctuation

Think about it
"The animals rebelled against Mr. Jones, they renamed the farm." Cover up the comma. Is the second half — "they renamed the farm" — a complete sentence all on its own? If both halves stand alone, what does a plain comma fail to do?
Trigger Phrase — write this on Card #2
"Two complete sentences → comma + FANBOYS, or a period, or a semicolon."
Zone 2 · Practice · Animal Farm Stems, 45 Sec Each

Comma Splices — Easy to Hard

5Level 1
"Napoleon gave the orders, the other pigs obeyed." Cover the comma — are both halves complete sentences on their own? Splice or fine?
6Level 2
"Boxer worked harder than any other animal, his strength never seemed to fail." Run the two-step test out loud, then write the fixed sentence.
7Level 3
"Squealer was clever with words, the other animals often believed him." Is a semicolon a better fix here than "and," or does it depend on the relationship between the two ideas?
8Level 4 · Stretch
"The windmill collapsed in the storm, however the animals rebuilt it by spring." Two possible errors are hiding here — a comma splice AND a missing comma before "however." Find both.
SHSAT Trap: A Long Sentence Isn't Automatically Wrong
Length has nothing to do with it. Run the cover-up test every time: cover the joining word, check if both halves stand alone. One rule, applied consistently, beats guessing by ear.
Minute 45–53 · Timed Passage Set

Skim First, Then Hit the Questions

Think about it
You get 6 minutes for one passage and 5 questions. If you spend 90 seconds skimming for "what is this mostly about," how much time is left per question — and why might that still be faster than reading zero times before diving in?
Your one advantage here
Inside a passage set, you can still move between its questions — that's the one place on the real test where "skip and come back" still works. Use it.
Minute 45–53 · Triage

Every Miss Has a Category, Not Just a Wrong Answer

Think about it
If you miss a question, which is more useful to know: that you got it wrong, or WHY you got it wrong? What's different about how you'd fix a "didn't know it" miss versus a "ran out of time" miss?
Didn't Know It
A genuine content gap. This goes on the list for a future re-teach block — not something to drill today.
Knew It, Rushed
Careless, not clueless. The fix is a habit: read the question twice before touching an answer choice.
Ran Out of Time
A pacing issue, not a knowledge issue. This gets isolated, timed drilling on that specific question type.
Why this reframe matters
Sorting mistakes into categories with different fixes turns "I'm bad at this" into "I have three fixable habits." That's the single most reassuring thing that can happen in session one.
Reading the Room

If Something Isn't Landing, We Pivot Quietly

Fatigue by min 30
Cut the grammar block to three items; protect the timed passage — pacing data matters more than a fourth rep.
Crushing the math
Skip problem 3, move straight to a percent-of-a-whole word problem, log that Phase 1 math can compress.
Diagnostic goes rough
Stop scoring publicly, pivot to one worked example with heavy scaffolding. Session one never ends on a scary number.
Shuts down / goes quiet
Name it lightly, move on, then genuinely return to that item later so the promise holds.
