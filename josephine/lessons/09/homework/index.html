Part D — Fiction Passage Set

Questions 19–24 · ~22 min

Read for change: What does Kai want at first? What disrupts that plan? What does Kai finally value?

The Wobble Pattern

1 Kai had promised the science fair judges a drawing robot that could produce a perfect circle every time. On the evening before the fair, the machine rolled across the poster paper, lowered its blue marker, and drew something closer to a seashell: round at first, then tightening into a crooked spiral.

2 “Again,” Kai muttered. The robot traced a second spiral, almost identical to the first. Along the workbench, earlier attempts lay face down like mistakes too embarrassing to watch.

3 Kai’s cousin Lila, who had come to help label the display, held one page toward the lamp. “These lines look printed,” she said. “Even the wobble repeats.”

4 “A repeated error is still an error.” Kai reached for the screwdriver. The left wheel turned smoothly, but the right wheel resisted once during every rotation. A slightly bent axle was lifting one edge of the robot at a regular interval.

5 The replacement axle was somewhere in the school supply cabinet, locked until morning. Kai pictured the judges reading the project title—Precision Through Programming—and then looking down at the stack of blue shells. He peeled the title card from the display board.

6 Lila set three drawings side by side. The spirals were not random. Each wide curve narrowed at the same point, and each small hook leaned in the same direction. “Your code is correcting for a machine it doesn’t actually have,” she said. “What if you tell it about the machine it does have?”

7 Kai opened the program. Instead of commanding the wheels to move at equal speeds, he measured the right wheel’s hesitation and changed the timing. The robot drew an oval, then a lopsided loop, then a circle that closed with only a small bump. Kai could have stopped there.

8 But the face-down pages no longer looked useless. Kai entered the old timing again and changed the marker color after every wheel rotation. Blue curved into green, green into gold. The bent axle pulled each band slightly inward until the page filled with a bright whirl.

9 The next morning, Kai placed the best circle on one side of the display and the colored whirl on the other. Between them, he posted a new title: Learning the Machine You Built.

10 When the first judge asked which drawing showed the robot working correctly, Kai looked from the nearly closed circle to the bands of color. “Both,” he said, and turned the bent axle slowly so the judge could see where each pattern began.
