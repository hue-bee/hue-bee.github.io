0:10–0:25 · Math Core

Probability & Counting

The hardest part is rarely arithmetic. It is deciding whether choices occupy slots, whether order changes the outcome, and whether earlier events change later probabilities.

Model first → compute second → sanity-check last
Counting Decision Map

Ask These Questions in Order

1
Slots?
If an outcome is built one choice at a time, draw a blank for each choice.
2
Order?
If AB and BA are different, arrangement matters. If not, divide out duplicates or choose.
3
Reuse?
Without replacement, later choice counts and probabilities shrink.
4
“At least one”?
Try the complement: 1 minus the probability of none.
Fast diagnostic
Would swapping two selected objects create a new outcome? Your answer decides whether you are arranging or selecting.
Worked Example 1 of 3

A Code Is a Row of Slots

A badge code uses two different letters from A–D, followed by three digits. The first digit cannot be 0; digits may repeat. How many codes are possible?
1
Draw five slots
_ · _ · _ · _ · _
Two letter slots, then three digit slots.
2
Write each slot’s choices
4 · 3 · 9 · 10 · 10
No repeated letter; no leading 0.
3
Multiply independent stages
4 · 3 · 9 · 10 · 10 = 10,800
The product counts every legal code once.
Why 4 · 4 is wrong for the letters
After the first letter is used, only three different letters remain. “Different” silently changes the second slot.

Arrangements vs. Selections

The Swap Test

Order matters

President–vice president is not the same after a swap. Use ordered slots.

n · (n − 1) · …
Order does not matter

A two-person committee is unchanged after a swap. Count each group once.

C(n,r) = n! / [r!(n−r)!]

Worked Example 2 of 3

“At Least One” Often Means Count None

A bag contains 4 red and 3 blue marbles. Two are drawn without replacement. What is the probability of drawing at least one blue marble?
1
Flip the event
“At least one blue” fails only when both marbles are red.
2
Use changing denominators
P(RR) = 4/7 · 3/6 = 2/7
No replacement makes the second draw dependent.
3
Subtract from 1
1 − 2/7 = 5/7
The complement captures one blue or two blues.
Probability should pass a size check
Blue marbles are nearly half the bag, and there are two chances to draw one. An answer much smaller than one-half should feel suspicious.

Translate the Diagram Before Calculating

Name what is given
Radius or diameter? Slant height or vertical height? Total perimeter or one side?
Write the formula
Keep the formula symbolic until every labeled measure has a clear role.
Check dimensions
Length uses units, area uses square units, and volume uses cubic units.

Match Each Measure to Its Formula

Use half the diameter.
V = πr²h
Base area × height.
V = Bh
Any prism.
P = 2l + 2w
Perimeter can create an equation.
Worked Example 3 of 3

Use Volume to Find a Missing Dimension

1
Write before substituting
V = πr²h
2
Replace only known quantities
160π = π(4²)h = 16πh
3
Isolate and label
h = 10 units because 160π ÷ 16π = 10.

FICTION BLOCK · “The Unfinished Map”Read and annotate paragraph jobs · Do not advance to questions yet

1On the final Saturday before the neighborhood history exhibit, Lena found her grandfather’s map where he had left it: spread beneath a jar of dull pencils, its eastern edge unfinished. Streets flowed in careful black lines toward the river and then stopped, as if the city had simply run out of certainty.

5“I can finish that section from memory,” Lena said. She had walked those blocks every school day for six years.

7Grandpa Tomas kept sorting photographs into envelopes. “A map from memory shows what the walker expects,” he said. “The exhibit needs what is there.”

10Lena glanced at the clock. The exhibit committee expected the map by noon. Going outside to verify three short streets felt fussy, almost theatrical. Still, she folded the blank edge into a clipboard and left.

14At Orchard Street, the bakery’s striped awning was gone. In its place stood a narrow grocery with crates of green plantains stacked by the door. Lena erased the tiny loaf she had already sketched in pencil. Two blocks later, she discovered that the alley beside the laundromat had been sealed by a brick wall painted with a blue heron.

Margin job prompts: setup · disagreement · pressure · first discovery. What expectation is already being challenged?
FICTION BLOCK · Passage ContinuedTrack the turning point and the meaning of the final image

19She had passed that wall countless times, but only now noticed how the painted bird’s feet seemed to grip the bricks. An older man watering window boxes saw her staring. He explained that neighbors had painted the mural after the alley closed, “so a dead end would still point somewhere.” Lena wrote his phrase in the map’s margin.

24By the river, the last missing street curved sooner than Lena remembered. Correcting it forced her to shift three buildings and redraw the footpath behind them. Her neat lines became a nest of eraser marks. For a moment, she imagined Grandpa Tomas seeing the mess and silently reaching for a fresh sheet.

29When she returned, he studied the page. Lena began explaining the time, the closed alley, the inconvenient curve. But he touched the penciled note beside the heron.

33“You mapped that, too?” he asked.

35Lena looked at the smudges, the shifted buildings, and the sentence that did not resemble a street at all. “It was there,” she said.

38Grandpa Tomas nodded and placed the map—not a fresh sheet—into the exhibit folder. Through the thin paper, Lena could see the erased loaf faintly beneath the grocery, like a small ghost refusing to leave the neighborhood.

One-sentence purpose: The story shows that ________________________________________________.
