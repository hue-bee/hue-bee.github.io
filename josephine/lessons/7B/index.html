Core Tool

The Three-Step Purpose Funnel

1
Whole passage
What is the author mainly trying to reveal, explain, argue, or explore?
2
Target detail
What changes if this sentence, image, example, or statistic disappears?
3
Connection
Which choice correctly links the detail’s local job to the passage’s larger purpose?
Precision

Topic Is a Noun; Purpose Is a Verb

Too broad
“Urban gardens”

That names the subject, but it does not reveal what the author is doing with the subject.

Purpose statement
“To explain how neighborhood gardens strengthen food access and community ties.”

The verb explain gives every later detail a job to perform.

Ask aloud
If the passage vanished, what understanding would the reader lose? Start with “The author wants the reader to understand that…” Then compress the answer into one sentence.
Signature trap: true but not functional
A choice may accurately describe a character, setting, or fact yet still fail to explain why the author placed this particular detail here.
Detail Jobs

Choose the Verb Before the Choice

Supportsadds proof for a claim
Illustratesturns an idea into an example
Contrastssharpens a difference
Complicatesadds tension or limitation
Transitionsmoves the focus or time
Sentence frame: “The detail [job verb] the author’s purpose by [specific connection].”
Guided Example · Level 1

From Concrete Detail to Main Purpose

Original passage: A vacant lot on Ruiz Avenue once collected windblown trash. Residents converted it into a garden with raised beds and a rain barrel. During its first summer, volunteers donated 640 pounds of tomatoes, beans, and herbs to a nearby pantry. Weekend workdays also brought longtime residents and new families together.
Why does the author include the highlighted sentence?
Question ladder
1. What is the passage mainly explaining?
2. Is 640 pounds a setting detail, a problem, or evidence?
3. Which choice connects that evidence to the community benefit?
Purpose: benefits→Detail: donation→Job: illustrates
Select a choice, then defend it without using its letter.
Practice · Levels 1–2

Name the Job Before Explaining It

1Level 1
A profile explains how a library’s evening hours help working families. The author notes that “attendance doubled after the library began closing at 9 p.m.” What job does the attendance detail perform?
2Level 2
A passage first describes a river as a transportation route, then states that a new dam blocked migrating fish. Does the dam detail mainly support, contrast, or complicate the passage’s view of the river? Explain.
3Level 2
A biography describes a young inventor’s repeated failures before her first patent. Why might the author list three rejected designs instead of simply saying she “struggled”?
4Level 2
A paragraph argues that quiet spaces improve concentration. It ends with a description of a silent room’s pale walls. Is the color detail functional, or merely related to the setting? Apply the removal test.
Distractor Lab

A Choice Can Be True—and Still Wrong

Mini-passage: When Nia reaches the empty rehearsal room, she places her violin case beside the chair but does not open it. From the hallway comes the clean, confident scale of another violinist. Nia tightens the loose hair on her bow, opens the case, and begins with the piece she fears most.
Whole-passage purpose
To reveal how Nia moves from hesitation toward determined action.
True but not the job
“Another musician is practicing nearby.” True—but it only paraphrases the detail.
Functional connection
The heard scale contrasts with Nia’s hesitation and triggers her decision to act.
Paraphrase tells what the detail says. Purpose explains what the detail makes happen in the passage.
Elimination Tool

Build an Evidence Bridge

A
Underline the choice’s verb. Does it claim support, contrast, cause, or transition?
B
Point to the text. Can the detail actually perform that verb?
C
Complete the bridge. Does that job advance the whole-passage purpose?
Fiction Transfer · Level 3

Problem: The Detail Changes the Decision

Original passage: Mara had spent the bus ride rehearsing how she would tell her aunt that she was leaving the family bakery. The university brochure in her bag felt like a ticket already stamped. At the shop, however, she found her aunt teaching a new employee to braid loaves. The braid collapsed, and her aunt laughed—not with embarrassment, but with the patient delight she had once used when teaching Mara. Mara set the brochure beneath the register and tied on an apron, deciding she would speak after the morning rush.
How does the highlighted detail contribute to the passage’s purpose?
Pause before choosing
What was Mara ready to do before the detail? What does she do immediately after it? Does the detail erase her goal, or add emotional tension to the timing of her choice?
Tutor Walkthrough · Level 3

Walkthrough: Track the Before-and-After

1

State the purpose

The passage explores Mara’s conflict between a new future and her attachment to the bakery relationship.

2

Locate the pivot

Before the detail, Mara rehearses leaving. After it, she delays the conversation and puts on an apron.

3

Name the job

The memory-like moment complicates her decision by reviving affection and belonging.

4

Reject overreach

Nothing proves university is unrealistic or that the aunt wants Mara there forever.

Defensible decision
The detail reminds Mara of their bond and therefore complicates—not cancels—her readiness to leave.
Poetry Transfer · Level 4

Problem: An Image Must Serve the Whole Poem

I climbed until the town
became a handful of coins,
until the road that ruled my mornings
thinned to a penciled line.
Above me, one hawk tilted—
not escaping the wind,
but borrowing its force.
I turned downhill carrying
no map, only a different height.
How does the image of the hawk contribute to the poem’s meaning?
Zoom out
What changes between the “road that ruled” and the “different height”? The hawk is not just scenery; ask what its action models for the speaker.
Tutor Walkthrough · Level 4

Walkthrough: Literal Image → Abstract Meaning

1

Translate literally

The hawk does not flee the wind; it uses the wind’s force to move.

2

Track the speaker

The speaker begins under the rule of a fixed road and ends with a changed perspective.

3

Connect the image

The hawk symbolizes using resistance rather than merely escaping it.

4

Control the scope

The poem supports a new way of seeing difficulty; it does not claim total freedom from limits.

Defensible decision
The hawk illustrates how a changed perspective can turn resistance into forward motion.
ELA with Numerical Evidence

A Statistic Is a Rhetorical Detail

A city report argues that shade should be treated as public infrastructure. It describes residents waiting at uncovered bus stops during summer heat. On one July afternoon, researchers recorded pavement beside an unshaded stop at 126°F, while pavement beneath nearby mature trees measured 98°F. The report recommends prioritizing tree planting along heavily used transit routes.
No computation needed
Ask what the numbers make concrete. Do they define a term, prove a contrast, establish a sequence, or introduce a counterclaim?
Statistic → sharpens the heat contrast → supports shade as infrastructure.
Nonfiction Transfer · Level 4

Problem: Distinguish Evidence from Mood

Original passage: Early audiences often treated long-distance electrical messages as stage tricks. During one public demonstration, an operator in Baltimore sent a sentence chosen by spectators to a station forty miles away. Less than a minute later, the distant station returned the same sentence, including an unusual misspelling the spectators had inserted. Reports of the event focused less on the machine’s appearance than on the accuracy of the returned wording.
Why does the author include the detail about the unusual misspelling?
Sentence over atmosphere
A choice may fit the passage’s general excitement yet miss the sentence’s exact work. Why is the deliberately inserted error stronger evidence than an ordinary returned sentence?
Tutor Walkthrough · Level 4

Walkthrough: Specific Proof Beats General Mood

1

Identify the doubt

Audience members suspect a trick, so the passage needs evidence that could not easily be staged.

2

Ask why this detail

An unusual spectator-chosen misspelling acts like a fingerprint; reproducing it ties the return directly to the sent message.

3

Name the job

The detail supports the machine’s demonstrated accuracy with uniquely identifying evidence.

4

Reject the mood trap

Excitement may describe the event broadly, but it does not explain why the author mentions the misspelling.

Defensible decision
The misspelling verifies that the returned wording matched the spectators’ exact message rather than a prepared guess.
Fresh Practice · Levels 3–4

Four Details; Four Different Jobs

5Level 3
A memoir describes the author’s first day in a new country. After several confusing conversations, a cashier silently draws a map on a receipt. How might this action complicate the memoir’s view of communication?
6Level 3
An article arguing for wetland restoration opens with the annual cost of flood damage, then tells the story of one flooded classroom. Why use the classroom after the statistic?
7Level 4
A poem repeatedly calls a locked gate “polite.” In the final stanza, the gate “bows on its hinges” but remains closed. Explain how the repeated word shapes the poem’s criticism.
8Level 4
A historical account explains why a reform failed. Midway through, the author notes that its opponents agreed with its goal but rejected its method. Does this detail support, contrast, or complicate the central explanation?
Digital Simulation · Locked Block

Read Once, Answer, Review, Submit

Original passage: For decades, curators stored faded fabric samples in drawers to protect them from light. A new imaging method now detects traces of dyes too faint for the eye to see. In one textile, the scan revealed a border of green leaves around what had seemed to be a plain brown field. The discovery did more than restore a design: it showed that the maker had combined pigments from two distant trade routes. The cloth became evidence of exchange, not merely decoration.

Session Wrap-Up

The Three Checks to Carry Forward

1
Purpose
Can I state what the whole passage is doing in one sentence with a precise verb?
2
Job
Can I name what the target detail does: supports, illustrates, contrasts, complicates, or transitions?
3
Proof

Commit rule
If a choice is merely true, too broad, too extreme, or unsupported by the target detail, eliminate it. Keep the choice that completes the evidence bridge.
