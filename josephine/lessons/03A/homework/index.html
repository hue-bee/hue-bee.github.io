Part B — Retrieve Before You Review

Unscored ·

Keep notes closed. Build your own reference from memory; then check with your lesson notes and circle only the gaps. These boxes do not count toward the /24 self-score.

1. Areas & perpendicular height

Write the triangle, parallelogram, and trapezoid area formulas. Define every length. Explain why a slanted side is not automatically the height.

2. Circles & containers

Retrieve circumference and circle area; prism and cylinder volume. Label radius versus diameter, and match length, square, or cubic units to each quantity.

3. Angle facts & median

Retrieve a straight-angle total, triangle-angle sum, and polygon interior-angle sum. Which angle relationships require parallel lines? How do you locate an even-sized list’s median?

4. Percent & means

Write the percent-change rule with its reference whole; multipliers for a rise, a fall, and successive changes; mean, total from a mean, and a combined-group mean.

5. Probability & counting

Retrieve probability for equally likely outcomes, the complement relationship, and a two-draw path without replacement. Explain the swap test for distinct jobs versus an unlabeled pair.

6. Commas & clause boundaries

State the rules for an introductory element, a series, a removable interruption, and two independent clauses joined by a coordinating conjunction. Contrast identifying and extra-information clauses.

7. Grammar controllers

Explain how you choose who/whom within a clause, find a verb’s real subject, repair an unclear pronoun, and keep time references consistent. Write a fresh mini-example for one rule.

8. Logic & paragraph jobs

Supply one transition each for addition, contrast, cause/result, example, and sequence. Complete: “The route was icy. ___, the courier took a different street.” Distinguish support, conclusion, and relevance questions.

Part D — Fiction Passage Set

Four Seconds of Ticking

Original fiction written for this homework

[¶1] Niko had promised the neighborhood archive a six-minute recording about Mr. Vega’s shoe-repair shop. On the afternoon before the shop closed for good, Niko arrived with a recorder and eight questions printed in large type. He had already planned to shorten long answers and remove pauses. A polished interview, he thought, should never make a listener wait.

[¶2] “What was the first repair you did here?” he asked. Mr. Vega turned toward a blue tin on the shelf. He rested two fingers on its lid but did not open it. The clock above the counter clicked through several seconds. Niko shifted the recorder closer, although its small green light showed that it was working.

[¶3] “Do you remember the year?” Niko added. It was the next question on his list, and a year seemed easier to supply than a story. Mr. Vega smiled without looking away from the tin. “The year isn’t the part I’m trying to find.”

[¶4] From the tin he lifted a pair of child-sized boots. One heel was worn almost smooth; a neat patch covered a split near the toe. Niko looked down at his list. None of his questions concerned old boots. His thumb moved toward the recorder’s stop button, then stayed there without pressing it.

[¶5] “His mother needed these ready before the early bus,” Mr. Vega said. “The boy sat right there, naming every color of thread I owned. He called the brown one ‘toast.’” He laughed once. “I had forgotten that until just now.” Niko glanced at the patched toe again. It no longer looked like the least interesting thing on the counter.

[¶6] Mr. Vega grew quiet a second time. Niko kept his hands in his lap. Outside, a truck rattled past; inside, the clock continued its small, steady work. At last Mr. Vega said, “I can tell you the number of shoes I repaired most weeks. Remembering the people takes a little longer.” Niko let the next printed question rest.

[¶7] That evening, Niko opened the recording on his computer. A stretch of near-silence appeared between two narrow shapes of sound. He selected it and moved the cursor toward Delete. Then he listened through his headphones. The clock, the passing truck, and Mr. Vega’s breath returned him to the counter. He moved the cursor away.

[¶8] The finished interview was still six minutes long. Niko had removed two of his own questions and a long explanation of how the recorder worked. Between the question about the first repair and the story about the boots, he left four seconds of ticking. Before sending the file, he listened once more, with his printed list face down beside him.

Track the change · optional, unscored

In a few words, note Niko’s starting goal, the turning point, and a later action that reveals a changed priority. Do not commit the set yet.
