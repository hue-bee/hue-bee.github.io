Zone 2 · Paragraph-Job AnnotationCore Skill

Every Paragraph Has a Job to Do

Think about it
If a paragraph didn't exist, would the passage be missing information, or would it be missing a REASON? What's the difference between a paragraph that gives facts and one that persuades?
The rule: label before you answer
Read paragraph → write 3–6 word job → move on
Why this fixes the purpose gap
Questions about purpose and structure are really asking "what job did this paragraph do?" If you've already named the job in the margin, you're not re-reading under time pressure — you're just checking your own notes.
Zone 2 · Practice

Naming the Job — Easy to Hard

1Level 1
A paragraph opens with "Many scientists once believed the Earth was flat." In 3–6 words, what job is this paragraph most likely doing?
2Level 2
A paragraph says "However, recent studies suggest the opposite is true." Name its job — and explain how the word "however" gave it away.
3Level 4
A nonfiction passage's final paragraph restates the author's claim and adds "so what should be done about it." Which job label fits best?
SHSAT Trap: Confusing Topic with Job
"What is this paragraph about" is not the same as "what is this paragraph doing." A paragraph can be about bees but its JOB is to contradict the previous paragraph's claim about them.
Zone 3 · Careless vs. ConceptualDiagnostic

Two Very Different Kinds of Wrong

Think about it
If you got a problem wrong, could you have solved a nearly identical one correctly five minutes later? If yes, was the rule ever really the problem?
Conceptual: didn't know the rule  |  Careless: knew it, executed wrong
The test question to ask every miss
"If I explained the rule to myself right now, could I re-solve this from scratch?" Yes means careless (unit conversion, decimal slip, sign error). No means conceptual — that's what gets re-taught, not just re-drilled.
Zone 3 · Practice

Classify the Miss — Easy to Hard

4Level 1
A student wrote 3.5 ft = 350 in when converting feet to inches (should be 42 in). Careless or conceptual? Name the exact cause.
5Level 2
Solve 4.2 - 1.75. If a student lined up the decimals wrong and got 4.15, is that careless or conceptual? Then find the correct answer.
6Level 4
A student converts 2.5 hours to minutes and writes 150 minutes, then adds it to 45 minutes and gets 105 minutes total. Which statement is TRUE?
SHSAT Trap: One Problem Can Have Two Error Types
Multi-step problems can be conceptually correct in one step and carelessly wrong in another. The error log should tag EACH step separately, not just the final answer.

Zone 5 · The Check-Your-Work Rule

Never Verify the Same Way Twice

Think about it
If you re-do a calculation the exact same way you did it the first time and you made a mistake, will redoing it identically catch that mistake? What has to change about your SECOND pass?
Three second-method checks
Estimate  |  Reverse operation  |  Plug back in
Why a second METHOD, not a repeat
A calculation error hides inside its own method — redoing identical steps just repeats the same blind spot. A different method (estimating, working backward, substituting the answer) exposes errors the first method couldn't see.
Zone 5 · Practice

Solve, Then Verify — Easy to Hard

7Level 1
A rope is 8.4 meters long. Convert to centimeters, then verify your answer by estimating: is 840 cm roughly 8 meters?
8Level 2
Solve 17 - 5.65. Then verify using the reverse operation: does your answer plus 5.65 return 17?
9Level 4
A recipe needs 2.5 pints of liquid, but the measuring cup only shows ounces (1 pint = 16 oz). A student pours 32 oz and calls it "close enough." Which check method proves this is wrong?
SHSAT Trap: "Close Enough" Isn't a Check
Eyeballing an answer feels like checking but skips the actual verification step. The mandatory rule is a SECOND calculation, done a different way — not a gut feeling about whether the number looks right.
