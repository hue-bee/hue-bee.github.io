Digital-Test Habit

A Correct Method Is Not Enough: Commit Carefully

Before advancing, what evidence can you produce?
ELA: point to the exact words that match the claim or paragraph job. Math: point to the units, sign, estimate, and operation order.
The four-second gate
Read the result as a sentence: “My answer has the right meaning, unit, sign, and size.” If one part sounds wrong, repair it before advancing.

Official CAT rule: Math and stand-alone ELA items cannot be revisited after advancing; passage-set answers remain editable only until the set is submitted.

Zone 1 · Best Support

Evidence Must Hit the Exact Claim

What would make this claim undeniably true?
Claim: “The garden reduced cafeteria waste.” Which evidence matters: how attractive the garden looks, or a before-and-after measure of food discarded?
The Target Test
same subject + same action + measurable proof
Trap: topic twins
A choice can mention gardens, food, and students yet fail to prove that waste decreased. Shared nouns are not shared logic.
Modeled Challenge 3-step solution

From Broad Topic to Precise Proof

ModelLevel 3
Claim: Installing reflective roofs can lower a city's summer energy demand. Which fact best supports the claim?
A. White roofs are visible from tall buildings.
B. One district used 14% less electricity for cooling after reflective roofs were installed.
C. Roof coatings are sold in several colors.
D. City summers have become warmer over time.
Step 1
Underline the engine
Subject: reflective roofs. Action: lower. Target: summer energy demand.
Step 2
Demand a measurement
Useful proof compares energy use after the roofs appear, not appearance or availability.
Step 3
Run the because test
The claim is credible because cooling electricity fell after installation. That directly completes the logic.
Clever shortcut: turn the claim into a blank
“Reflective roofs lower energy demand because ______.” Only evidence describing reduced energy use can fill the blank without changing the claim.

Zone 2 · Conclusions

A Conclusion Should Close, Not Expand

What has the paragraph actually earned?
A conclusion may synthesize the central claim and its significance. It may not promise certainty, introduce a new cause, or launch a different topic.
The landing test
Imagine the passage as a plane. A conclusion should touch down on the same runway: same topic, same claim, no sudden turn, no exaggerated speed.
central claim + earned significance − new idea − extreme certainty
Modeled Challenge 3-step solution

Build the Ending from the Passage's Limits

ModelLevel 3
A passage explains that street trees can cool sidewalks, absorb some storm water, and improve neighborhood comfort. Which conclusion is best?
A. Therefore, planting trees will completely eliminate urban heat and flooding.
B. For these reasons, well-planned tree planting can be one useful part of making neighborhoods more comfortable and resilient.
C. Cities should next replace all parking spaces with gardens.
D. Tree species were first classified centuries ago.
Step 1
Compress the passage
Central claim: street trees provide several practical neighborhood benefits.
Step 2
Set certainty level
The passage supports “can help” or “one useful part,” not “completely eliminate.”
Step 3
Reject new doors
Parking policy and classification history were never developed, so they cannot close the passage.

Zone 2 · Relevance & Deletion

Every Sentence Needs a Job Badge

Finish this sentence: “The author included it to…”
If you cannot name a job that advances the paragraph—support, explain, illustrate, contrast, transition—the sentence probably does not belong.
9Level 2
Paragraph job: explain why a school garden improved science learning. Sentence: “The garden fence was painted green.” Keep, revise, or delete? State the missing connection.
10Level 4
When can historical background remain in a paragraph about a current policy? Write a rule that distinguishes useful context from decorative history.
Zone 3 · Relative Clauses

Meaning First: Essential or Extra?

If the clause disappears, can the reader still identify the noun?
Yes: extra information, often which, surrounded by commas. No: identifying information, often that, with no commas.
extra → , which ... ,    identifying → that ...
Who or whom?
Replace with he: use who. Replace with him: use whom. Ignore the words between the pronoun and its verb.
Zone 3 · Practice

Relative Clauses: Meaning Controls Punctuation

11Level 1
The museum, which opened in 1912, is being renovated. Remove the clause. Is the noun still identified? Explain why the commas fit.
12Level 2

13Level 3
Choose who or whom: “The engineer ___ the committee selected will lead the project.” Replace the blank with he or him inside the clause.
14Level 4
Write two versions of one sentence in which adding commas around a relative clause changes the group being described. Explain the meaning shift.
Math Precision · 25 Minutes

Make Every Unit and Sign Visible

Units are algebra
Multiply by conversion fractions equal to 1; cancel labels before numbers.
Signs are data
Carry negatives on every line instead of trusting memory.
Estimates are alarms
An approximate result exposes decimal shifts, reversed rates, and impossible sizes.
Zone 4 · Unit Conversions

Build a Chain Where Units Cancel

Which unit must sit on the opposite side of the fraction?
Start with the given quantity. Place the unwanted unit so it cancels; place the wanted unit so it survives. If the labels do not cancel, the fraction is upside down.
5.5 ft × 12 in/1 ft × 2.54 cm/1 in
Clever check: read only the labels
ft cancels with ft; in cancels with in; centimeters remain. The chain has the correct direction before any arithmetic begins.
Modeled Challenge 3-step solution

Feet to Centimeters Without Guessing Direction

ModelLevel 3
A ribbon is 5.5 feet long. Convert its length to centimeters using 1 foot = 12 inches and 1 inch = 2.54 centimeters.
Step 1
Write the chain
5.5 ft × 12 in/ft × 2.54 cm/in. Feet and inches cancel.
Step 2
Estimate first
About 6 feet is 72 inches; at roughly 2.5 cm each, expect about 180 cm.
Step 3
Compute & label
5.5 × 12 × 2.54 = 167.64. The surviving unit is centimeters, and the result fits the estimate.
Why 27.94 is suspicious
Multiplying 5.5 by 2.54 skips the feet-to-inches bridge. A two-link conversion needs two conversion factors.

Zone 5 · Mixed Numbers & Signs

The Sign Belongs to the Entire Number

What does −2 1/3 mean?
It means the opposite of the whole mixed number: −(2 + 1/3), not −2 + 1/3. Convert first so the sign cannot drift.
−2 1/3 = −7/3
Sign drift
Writing the negative only beside the whole-number part changes the value. Parentheses or an improper fraction keep the sign attached.
Modeled Challenge 3-step solution

Mixed Numbers: Convert, Align, Check

ModelLevel 3
Evaluate −2 1/3 + 5/6.
Step 1
Convert visibly
−2 1/3 = −7/3. The negative applies to all seven thirds.
Step 2
Use one denominator
−7/3 = −14/6, so −14/6 + 5/6 = −9/6.
Step 3
Simplify & estimate
−9/6 = −3/2 = −1 1/2. Starting near −2.3 and adding about 0.8 should land near −1.5.
Zone 5 · Operations

Order of Operations Is a Layer Map

What is the outermost operation?
Before calculating, box grouped expressions and mark exponents. Then travel left to right within each equal-priority layer.
19Level 2
Evaluate 6 − 2(1.5 − 0.7). Estimate the parentheses first; then decide whether the final result should be above or below 6.
20Level 4
Evaluate −3² + 4[2 − (−1.5)] ÷ 0.7. First distinguish −3² from (−3)².
Modeled Challenge 3-step solution

Rates: Convert the Top and Bottom

ModelLevel 4
Convert 72 kilometers per hour to meters per minute.
Step 1
Write the rate as a fraction
72 km / 1 h. Distance is upstairs; time is downstairs.
Step 2
Cancel both units
72 km/h × 1000 m/km × 1 h/60 min.
Step 3
Compute & sanity-check
72,000 ÷ 60 = 1,200 m/min. A little over 1 km each minute fits 72 km each hour.
Why time conversions feel backward
Hours begin in the denominator, so the conversion factor must place hours in the numerator. The labels—not intuition—choose the orientation.

Session Close · Error Log

Name the Failure Point, Not Just the Topic

ELA tag
Claim too broad? Evidence only topic-related? Conclusion too extreme? Sentence had no paragraph job? Clause meaning misread?
Math tag
Conversion reversed? Unit skipped? Negative drifted? Mixed number not converted? Decimal or operation layer mishandled?
One-line repair
Write the exact behavior for next time: “Cancel units before computing” or “State the claim target before reading choices.”
Final commitment check
Before advancing: Does the answer match the requested meaning? Does the unit survive correctly? Is the sign sensible? Is the size close to the estimate?
