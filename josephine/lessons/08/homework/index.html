Part A — Stand-Alone ELA Decisions

8 Questions · ~14 min

Identify the exact target before comparing choices. A choice that shares the topic but misses the claim is not support.

Four-second gateTargetText proofCertaintyCommit

1. A student claims that adding a morning bus route improved access to an after-school robotics club. Which fact most directly supports the claim? L1

Match subject + action + measurable result.

2. Claim: “A new checkout system shortened the time customers waited at a neighborhood market.” Which evidence is strongest? L2

Demand a before-and-after measure.

3. A paragraph explains that a school’s refill stations reduced purchases of single-use water bottles during one semester. Which conclusion best stays within the evidence? L2

Close the claim; do not enlarge it.

4. A paragraph’s purpose is to explain how rehearsal recordings helped a student orchestra improve timing. Which sentence should be deleted as irrelevant? L3

Give every sentence a job badge.

5. Which sentence correctly punctuates a nonessential relative clause? L2

Remove the clause: is the noun still identified?

6. The photographer ___ the magazine hired documented the restoration. Which word correctly completes the sentence? L3

The magazine hired him—not he.

7. One class tested a peer-feedback checklist for three weeks and revised more errors than it had during the previous three weeks. Which conclusion is most defensible? L4

Match certainty to sample and duration.

8. Claim: “Installing motion sensors caused the recreation center to use less electricity for lighting.” Which evidence best separates that effect from seasonal changes? L4

Look for a meaningful comparison.

Part B — Revising/Editing Passage Set

4 Questions · ~12 min

Responses remain editable inside this set. Recheck all four against the passage, then submit the set once.

Borrow, Build, Return

[1] The East Harbor Library had long lent books, films, and recordings. [2] In March, it opened a “Library of Tools” that allowed cardholders to borrow drills, levels, sewing machines, and gardening equipment. [3] The pilot program aimed to help residents complete occasional repairs without purchasing tools they might use only once.

[4] Before the opening, library staff surveyed 260 households about the equipment they needed and the obstacles they faced. [5] Sixty-eight percent of respondents named cost as the main reason they had delayed a small repair. [6] The library building, which was completed in 1987, has pale brick walls. [7] Staff used the survey results to choose the first 75 tools and to create short safety demonstrations.

[8] During the program’s first twelve weeks, members borrowed 612 items. [9] In follow-up forms, 154 of 181 respondents reported completing a task they otherwise would have postponed. [10] These early results do not show how the program will perform over many years. [11] Still, they suggest that a carefully selected lending collection can remove one practical barrier to household repairs.

9. Which sentence provides the most direct numerical support for the idea that the program helped residents complete delayed tasks? L2

10. Which sentence should be deleted because it does not support the passage’s explanation of the tool-lending program? L2

11. Which revision of sentence 6 correctly treats the information about the building’s completion date as extra information? L3

12. Which choice best describes why sentences 10 and 11 form an effective conclusion? L4

Answer all four; then audit the set.
