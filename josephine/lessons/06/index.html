Percents · Core Model

Percent Is an Exchange Rate per 100

Build meaning before the equation
If 30 percent of a group has a trait, what would that mean in an equivalent group of exactly 100? Which quantity is the whole, and which is only a piece?
part = percent × whole
Convert the percent to a decimal first.
Three-question discipline
What is the whole? What part is described? Is the percent the rate linking them?

Percents · Two Directions

Forward Multiplies; Reverse Divides

ForwardWhole Known
What is r percent of W?
part = r × W
The word of usually links rate to whole by multiplication.
ReverseWhole Hidden
P is r percent of what?
whole = P ÷ r
Same relationship; a different unknown is isolated.

Percent Change · Base Control

The Denominator Is the Old Value

Why does the base matter?
A rise of 12 means something different when the original is 40 than when it is 400. Which starting amount did the change have to work against?
percent change = (new − old) ÷ old
Denominator custody
Circle the old value. The numerator measures the move; the denominator compares the move to the starting point.

Successive Changes

Percent Changes Compose as Multipliers

State-transition form
new = old × (1 ± r)
Why addition fails
The second rate acts on a changed base. Multipliers preserve the sequence.
Predict before multiplying
After a 20 percent rise and 20 percent fall, is the second 20 percent taken from the same amount as the first?

Vocab in Context · Method

Meaning Is an Intersection of Constraints

1Cover

Hide the word and choices. Prevent anchoring.

2Predict

Write a plain replacement from sentence logic.

3Substitute

Test grammar and meaning inside the sentence.

4Pressure-test

Reject mood matches that miss the local job.

Why cover the choices?
A polished distractor invites the brain to defend it. Prediction creates an independent standard.
Global-mood trap
A choice can match broad excitement, sadness, or topic and still fail the tested sentence’s narrower action or attitude.

When Mood Fits but the Sentence Does Not

Observed pattern

Interrogate the attraction

The specificity ladder
Topic is broadest; mood is narrower; sentence function is narrower still; word meaning is tightest. The answer must survive the tightest level.

Context Practice · Public-Domain Poem

Let Contrast Define “Heeds”

Snowy Mountains
Higher and still more high,
Palaces made for cloud,
Above the dingy city-roofs
Blue-white like angels with broad wings,
Pillars of the sky at rest—
The mountains from the great plateau
Uprise.

But the world heeds them not:
They have been here now for too long a time.
Use the hinge word
The first stanza magnifies the mountains. What reversal does “But” announce? If the world does not do something to this grandeur, what plain verb belongs?
16Context Build
Without a dictionary, replace heeds. Explain how “too long a time” sharpens the meaning.
Syntax narrows options
The direct object “them” and negation “not” require an action toward the mountains. A word about appearance cannot do the same grammatical job.
