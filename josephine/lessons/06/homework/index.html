Passage Set · A City Garden After Dark

1 For years, volunteers at Riverside Garden assumed that daytime observations told the whole story. 2 Then a biology class installed motion-sensitive cameras and discovered a substantial nighttime population: moths pollinated evening flowers, bats skimmed insects above the pond, and raccoons inspected compost bins.

3 The cameras did not merely add names to a species list. 4 They revealed a schedule. 5 Bees dominated sunny hours, whereas moths became active after dusk; birds visited the pond early, while bats arrived much later. 6 Because these groups used the same small space at different times, their activities were less likely to conflict.

7 The findings also prompted a practical change. 8 Volunteers had planned to install bright security lights throughout the garden. 9 Instead, they adopted a more measured plan: low amber lights along two paths, dark zones near flowers, and timers that reduced illumination after closing. 10 The revised design protected visitors without erasing the nighttime activity the cameras had made visible.
