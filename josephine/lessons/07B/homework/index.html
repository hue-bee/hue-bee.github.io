A Wider Beam

1 Ravi had built his solar lantern for the engineering fair, not for the basement stairs behind the auditorium. 2 He had spent two weeks cutting a star-shaped filter so the lantern would scatter points of light across the ceiling. 3 At his display table, though, the stars looked weak beneath the gym’s bright fixtures, while the projects beside his flashed and spun.

4 During cleanup, the custodian asked to borrow the lantern. 5 She held it low as she descended the unlit basement stairs, and its broad spill of light revealed a loose strip of metal at the edge of one step. 6 “That could have caught someone’s shoe,” she said, pressing the strip flat with her heel.

7 Ravi looked back toward the gym, where the judges were gathering around a motorized model. 8 Then he turned the lantern over in his hands. 9 The star filter narrowed the beam. 10 He unscrewed it, slid the decorative piece into his pocket, and began sketching a clip that could fasten the lantern to a railing.

7. Which statement best expresses the passage’s main purpose? L2

8. How does the highlighted detail in sentence 5 contribute to the passage? L3

9. Why does the author contrast the faint ceiling stars with the visible loose metal strip? L3

10. What is the main effect of Ravi placing the star filter in his pocket rather than throwing it away? L4

60-second review: Purpose stated? Job verb named? Exact words located? Overstatement removed?

Blueprint

My grandfather’s ruler lies
across the workbench,
its brass edge strict as noon.

I use it to draw a garden gate,
each bar obedient.
Then rain enters through the cracked pane,
loosening graphite into gray rivers.

A bean from lunch rolls
into the wet paper’s fold.
By morning, one pale root
has ignored every measured line.

I lift the ruler—
not to erase the plan,
but to leave a place for opening.

Why a Fallen Log May Stay

1 Visitors sometimes report fallen logs in city parks because they assume the wood has been forgotten. 2 Ecologists, however, may leave selected logs in place as part of a deliberate habitat plan. 3 As wood decays, it holds moisture, shelters insects, and returns nutrients to the soil.

4 In one summer survey, researchers compared soil beside retained logs with exposed soil only two meters away. 5 After five rainless days, the soil beside the logs contained 31 percent more moisture, and observers found three times as many salamanders there. 6 Those results helped park managers identify places where leaving wood would provide the greatest ecological benefit.

7 The policy does not mean that every fallen tree remains untouched. 8 Crews remove wood that blocks paths or threatens structures, and small signs explain why other logs remain. 9 By pairing ecological goals with safety checks and public explanation, parks can make apparent disorder serve a carefully managed purpose.

15. Which statement best expresses the passage’s main purpose? L2

16. Why does the author include the measurements in sentence 5? L3

17. How does sentence 7 contribute to the author’s explanation? L4

18. Why does the author mention the small signs in sentence 8? L4

60-second review: What do the numbers prove? Which sentence limits the claim? Which detail answers a reader’s likely concern?

Listening Before Radar

1 Along several coastlines, enormous concrete curves still face the sea. 2 Built before radar, these “sound mirrors” gathered the faint noise of approaching aircraft and directed it toward a listener standing at a focal point. 3 The structures could not identify an aircraft precisely, but under favorable conditions they gave crews additional minutes to prepare.

4 During a 1930 trial, operators detected an aircraft before observers could see it, yet a passing ship’s engine briefly sent their estimate in the wrong direction. 5 The same test displayed both the promise and the limitation of listening for danger across long distances.

6 Radar soon made the mirrors obsolete. 7 Their survival, however, preserves more than an abandoned technology: the silent curves mark a stage when engineers had learned to extend human hearing but had not yet learned to replace it with radio detection.

19. Which statement best expresses the passage’s main purpose? L3

20. How does the highlighted trial detail contribute to the passage? L4

21. What is the main effect of the phrase “extend human hearing but had not yet learned to replace it”? L4
