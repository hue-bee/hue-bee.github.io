Editing Rule 1 · Boundaries

Punctuation Is Traffic Control

Ask the boundary question
Can the words on the left stand alone? Can the words on the right stand alone? Your answer—not the sentence’s length—determines the punctuation.
GREEN
One complete thought: no internal stop unless a phrase needs separation.
YELLOW
Complete + incomplete: use one comma where the dependent/introductory part ends.
RED
Complete + complete: period, semicolon, or comma + coordinating conjunction.

Editing Rule 2 · Combining

Build a Sentence with One Main Engine

Which idea deserves the steering wheel?
If two facts are not equally important, why coordinate them with and? Which fact should become the main clause, and which can ride as a phrase or dependent clause?
The “No Lost Cargo” test
1. Underline every distinct fact in the original. 2. Choose the fact that advances the paragraph. 3. Attach the other facts without changing time, cause, or emphasis. 4. Recount the facts after revising.
SHSAT Trap · Shorter Is Not Automatically Better
A concise choice can still delete a cause, reverse a timeline, or create a dangling modifier. Meaning outranks elegance.

Editing Rule 3 · Transition Logic

A Transition Names the Turn

Cover the choices
Before reading a transition list, finish this sentence: “Idea B ___ Idea A.” Does B support, qualify, contradict, result from, or exemplify A?
RELATIONSHIP SENTENCE
B [relationship verb] A → choose the matching signal
The Double-Turn Trap
A sentence may concede one point and then reverse direction. If one transition cannot express both moves, the sentence needs restructuring—not a fancier synonym.

The Cooling Library

[1]On summer afternoons, the stone reading room of the East Harbor Library often became warmer indoors than the shaded courtyard outside. [2]The building’s tall windows admitted abundant light, they also trapped heat long after sunset. [3]Replacing the historic windows would have reduced the heat, but preservation rules limited alterations to the façade.

[4]Architects proposed a less visible solution: a narrow ventilation shaft running behind the bookcases. [5]As warm air rose through the shaft, cooler air entered at floor level. [6]Consequently, the system used no compressor; it depended on the movement of air caused by temperature differences.

[7]During the first month, a cluster of sensors were recording temperatures every two minutes. [8]The data showed that the room cooled most rapidly after librarians opened the lower vents. [9]Designed to remain hidden from visitors, the preservation committee approved the shaft for installation throughout the building.

Math Concept · Translation

Boundary Words Are Fence Instructions

Who may stand on the fence?
If a museum admits at most 240 people, may exactly 240 enter? Your answer decides whether the symbol includes equality—before any algebra begins.
AT LEAST
boundary included; values extend upward: greater than or equal to
MORE THAN
boundary excluded; values extend upward: strictly greater than
AT MOST
boundary included; values extend downward: less than or equal to
FEWER THAN
boundary excluded; values extend downward: strictly less than

Math Concept · Graphing

Graph in Two Moves: Endpoint, Then Ray

Test the boundary itself
For x < 3, does 3 satisfy the statement? If not, what must the endpoint look like? Then test 2 and 4: which side contains true values?
THE E.R.A. ROUTINE
E = Endpoint · R = Ray · A = Audit with one test value
Clever check
Do not memorize “less points left.” Substitute one number on each side. The graph becomes evidence, not a drawing convention.

The Sign-Flip Rule

Negative Multiplication Mirrors the Number Line

Why must the sign reverse?
Multiplying by a negative reflects every value across zero. If 2 sits left of 5, where do −2 and −5 sit after reflection? Which number is now greater?
ONLY ONE TRIGGER
Reverse the inequality only when multiplying or dividing both sides by a negative.
Do Not Flip for Subtraction
Subtracting a positive from both sides shifts both values equally; it does not reverse their order. Circle the actual multiplication/division step that triggers the mirror.
