The Library of Smells

1 At a conservation lab in France, researchers preserve more than pages and bindings: they preserve the odors of old books. 2 Paper made from different fibers releases distinctive compounds as it ages, and leather covers contribute scents of their own.

3 To capture an odor, technicians place a book in a sealed chamber and draw the surrounding air through a small absorbent tube. 4 The collected compounds can later be studied without repeatedly handling the fragile volume.

5 One researcher compared the project to recording a voice before it disappears. 6 The comparison points to the lab’s larger goal: preserving a sensory part of history that ordinary photographs and catalog entries cannot contain.
