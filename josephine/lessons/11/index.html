Passage 7 · Q3Level 2

Formal style

Which revision of sentence 11 best maintains the formal style?

A. However, in 2018, a dust storm covered the planet, bringing the rover’s progress to a standstill.
B. However, in 2018, a dust storm shrouded the planet, impermanently halting the rover’s progress.
C. However, in 2018, a dust storm enveloped the whole of the planet, arresting the rover’s progress for a time.
D. However, in 2018, a dust storm covered up the whole planet, which quickly brought a stop to the progress of the rover.
Local context, sentences 8–12

Opportunity continued to provide valuable data about craters and other surface features. Throughout the mission, the rover traveled 28.06 miles. When it neared a marathon distance, scientists named its location Marathon Valley. However, in 2018, a dust storm spread all over the planet, putting the rover’s progress on hold. Prior to this planet-wide storm, smaller storms had deposited dust on the rover’s solar panels.
Ask in order: Which choice is precise? Which is concise? Which avoids conversational phrasing? Which introduces no strange or inaccurate connotation?
Formal-style engine

Do not choose the “fanciest” sentence

1. Semantic fit

Does every verb mean exactly what happened? Substitute a plain synonym and inspect the claim.

2. Register fit

Formal writing prefers neutral precision, not inflated diction and not conversational phrasal verbs.

3. Economy fit

After meaning and register survive, prefer fewer moving parts and a clear causal chain.
Best revision = precise meaning ∩ formal register ∩ efficient syntax
Defense protocol: For each option, name one exact property—not “sounds better.” Use labels such as redundant, colloquial, inflated, vague, temporally inaccurate, or clean.
Trap: A longer Latinate word can make a sentence less formal if its connotation is wrong. Formality is disciplined exactness, not ornament.

Passage 7 · Q5Level 4

Sentence combining

(12) Prior to this planet-wide dust storm, minor dust storms had periodically deposited dust onto the rover’s solar panels.
(13) These prevented it from charging its batteries.

Which combination best preserves the meaning and grammar?

A. Preventing the rover from charging its batteries, minor dust storms had periodically deposited a layer of dust onto the solar panels...
B. Periodically depositing a layer of dust onto the solar panels..., minor dust storms had prevented it from charging...
C. Minor dust storms...prevented the rover from charging its batteries because they had periodically deposited a layer of dust...
D. Prior to this planet-wide dust storm, minor dust storms had periodically deposited a layer of dust onto the solar panels..., preventing it from charging its batteries.

Causal skeleton

Ask: Who deposits? What physically prevents charging? What does each pronoun point to? Which opening modifier logically attaches to the subject that follows?
Combining engine

Preserve the causal ownership

Agent

The grammatical subject should be capable of performing the opening action.

Pronoun tether

Replace every “it,” “they,” or “these” with its noun. Reject ambiguity or impossible reference.

Cause chain

Keep event order intact: source → mechanism → effect. Concision cannot reverse the science.
Modifier test: “Who is doing the -ing?” The noun immediately after the comma must own that action.
High-level move: Reduce every option to a three-node causal skeleton before comparing wording. Structure is harder for distractors to disguise than style.
Final defense: State the subject, each verb, and each pronoun antecedent aloud. Stop before selecting until all three remain logically compatible.

Math Q3Level 2

Equivalent proportions · multi-select

A diner used 18 dozen buns for 150 customers. At the same rate, which proportions can determine the dozen buns needed for 250 customers? Select three.

18/150 = x/250
18/250 = x/150
150/18 = 250/x
x/18 = 250/150
x/150 = 18/250

Unit-signature test

Ask: Is each ratio internally consistent? Are the two ratios oriented the same way, or are both reciprocals? Does each cross-product pair the same physical quantities?
Proportion engine

Treat units like fingerprints

Fast equivalence checks

  1. Write the canonical rate with labels.
  2. Allow three legal transformations: reciprocal both sides, swap sides, scale corresponding quantities.
  3. Reject any equation that moves one number without its paired unit role.
  4. Use cross-products only after the unit structure passes.

Cross-product invariant

outside product = inside product
The products are not just numbers. Each must carry the same compound unit: dozen × people. Mismatched day pairings expose a structural distractor.
Multi-select guardrail: Evaluate every choice independently. Finding one valid equation does not tell you how the remaining equations behave.
Student defense: For each proposed proportion, say its numerator unit and denominator unit on both sides. Leave the boxes unmarked until all five have a unit verdict.

Inclusive-language map

Boundary words

At least includes the boundary and everything above.
At most includes the boundary and everything below.
More than / fewer than exclude the boundary.

Table ontology

The left column names categories. The right column counts people. Probability is built from counts of people—not by adding the category labels.
P(event) = (sum of qualifying frequencies) / (sum of all frequencies)
Reasonableness check: Before reducing, should the probability be less than, equal to, or greater than one quarter? Justify from the table visually.
Math Q32Level 4

Least possible overlap

Of 800 students, 720 said “yes” to Question A and 640 said “yes” to Question B. What is the least possible number who could have said “yes” to both?

A. 80
B. 160
C. 560
D. 640
First question: If the two “yes” groups tried to avoid each other completely, how many distinct seats would they demand?

The container forces collision

Minimum-overlap engine

Think seats, not circles

Capacity argument

Pretend A and B are placed into different seats. If their combined demand exceeds the room capacity, every excess placement must reuse a seat. Reused seats are the forced overlap.
minimum overlap = max(0, |A| + |B| − N)

Complement cross-check

Translate “yes to A” into “no to A,” and likewise for B. Small complement groups limit how many students can escape being in both.
both = A − (A but not B)
Extremal question: To minimize “both,” where should every possible B student be placed first? Only after those non-A seats fill do placements become unavoidable overlap.
Trap: “Least possible” is not the smaller group and not an ordinary subtraction. It asks for the arrangement that minimizes intersection while respecting the total population.
