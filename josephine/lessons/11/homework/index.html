A Roof That Measures Rain

[1] At Harborview Middle School, heavy storms once sent water racing from the gym roof into an overloaded drain. [2] Rather than enlarge the entire drainage system, the environmental club proposed a smaller experiment. [3] Students installed six shallow trays planted with hardy sedum, a plant that can survive both heat and brief flooding.

[4] Sensors beneath the trays measured how much rainwater reached the roof after passing through the soil. [5] During the first storm, the planted trays retained nearly half of the water that fell on them. [6] On the other hand, the students repeated the test during storms of different lengths. [7] The later trials helped the group distinguish a promising result from a lucky one.

[8] Impressed by the data, three additional trays were approved by the principal. [9] The club now plans to compare roof temperature above planted and unplanted sections. [10] Its next investigation may show whether the trays reduce both runoff and summer heat.

Review questions 9–12 before leaving this set.
