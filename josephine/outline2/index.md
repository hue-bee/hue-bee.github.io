# Josephine Lupo: SHSAT CAT Study Plan for November 2026

## Executive assessment

Josephine’s current profile is **uneven rather than uniformly weak**. Her March diagnostic was 441 overall (ELA 240, Math 201), while her June diagnostic was 444 overall (ELA 208, Math 236). The total barely changed, but Math gained 35 scaled points while ELA lost 32; preparation therefore needs to **recover ELA quickly without surrendering the genuine Math progress**.[^1][^2]

Brooklyn Tech’s lowest qualifying score for 2026 admissions was 506, while the next higher 2026 cutoffs included Staten Island Tech at 517, Bronx Science at 525, Queens Science at 531, HSMSE at 539, and Stuyvesant at 561. Cutoffs change annually, so the working target should not be 506 exactly; the safer instructional goal is a **practice-performance band equivalent to at least 520**, sustained on two late-cycle assessments.[^3]

The November 2026 SHSAT is not directly comparable to Josephine’s two 114-question paper-form diagnostics. The new exam has 50 ELA and 50 Math questions in 180 minutes and is computer-adaptive; its final performance estimate depends on the pattern of correct and incorrect responses in relation to calibrated item difficulty. Old raw-to-scaled conversions should therefore be treated as historical indicators, not predictions of her 2026 result.[^4]

## CAT implications

The fall 2026 SHSAT begins each subject with an average-difficulty item or ELA passage set and repeatedly updates the student’s estimated performance as answers are submitted. A correct response may lead to a more challenging item, while an incorrect response may lead to an easier one; every student still answers 50 questions in each subject and is tested on the same standards and content areas.[^4]

Navigation changes how Josephine must work. She must answer before advancing; Math and stand-alone ELA questions cannot be revisited, while questions attached to the current ELA passage may be reviewed only before that passage set is submitted. Once a subject is submitted, it is closed.[^4]

The exam includes multiple-choice, fill-in-the-blank, and technology-enhanced items. Josephine may choose which subject to take first, and the official portal provides a Student Readiness Tool plus four online practice tests that mirror the test’s form, content, tools, and navigation.[^4]

These rules produce four preparation requirements:

- **Decision quality before submission:** eliminate the paper-test habit of marking a question for later.
- **Stable early and late accuracy:** do not attempt to “game” the adaptive sequence; apply the same verification routine throughout.
- **Digital reading fluency:** practice highlighting, note-taking, scrolling, and passage-set review until the interface costs almost no attention.
- **Emotional control after hard questions:** a difficult item can indicate that the system is estimating stronger performance; difficulty should trigger methodical work, not panic.

## ELA accomplishments

### Passage purpose

Josephine must stop treating each question as an isolated hunt for a matching phrase. For every passage, she should be able to state the **central claim or controlling idea**, identify the job of each paragraph, and explain how a selected detail advances the whole passage.

The required routine is:

1. Write a 3–7 word gist beside each paragraph.
2. Assign a function verb: *introduces, defines, illustrates, contrasts, complicates, explains a cause, shows an effect, concedes,* or *concludes*.
3. State the passage’s main point in one sentence containing both topic and author’s message.
4. For a purpose question, complete: “The author includes this detail to ___, which supports the larger idea that ___.”
5. Predict the answer before evaluating choices.
6. Reject answers that are true locally but do not explain the detail’s role in the passage.

This routine directly targets the mismatch between Josephine’s stronger literal-detail performance and very weak detail-to-purpose performance.[^2][^1]

### Organization and structure

Josephine needs a functional vocabulary for common passage structures: chronology, cause/effect, problem/solution, comparison/contrast, claim/evidence, question/answer, and general-to-specific development. She must distinguish **what a paragraph says** from **why the author placed it there**.

Instruction should repeatedly contrast tempting but incomplete answers with functional answers. For example, “The paragraph discusses an early experiment” summarizes content; “The paragraph provides the failed attempt that motivates the successful method described next” explains structure.

Mastery requires Josephine to:

- Identify the relationship between adjacent paragraphs.
- Explain the effect of deleting, moving, or adding a paragraph or sentence.
- Recognize pivot words and conceptual shifts, not merely chronological transitions.
- Connect text features, examples, quotations, or graphics to the author’s claim.
- Answer structure questions from a passage map rather than rereading randomly.

### Fiction reasoning

Fiction performance was weak on both diagnostics, so practice must go beyond plot recall. For each fiction passage, Josephine should create a compact **G-O-S-T map**: Goal, Obstacle, Shift, and Theme.[^1][^2]

She must learn to:

- Infer motivation from actions, dialogue, and internal thoughts.
- Separate a character’s belief from the narrator’s or author’s viewpoint.
- Track emotional changes and identify the precise event that causes each shift.
- Distinguish internal from external conflict.
- Support tone and character claims with two pieces of evidence.
- Interpret figurative language by its effect on mood, character, or theme.
- Reject extreme answers and answers that describe a plausible event not supported by the passage.

### Revising and editing

The 90%-to-40% swing suggests that grammar knowledge is not yet automatic under a different form or pacing condition. The goal is not simply to review comma rules; it is to build a reliable decision sequence for stand-alone items that cannot be revisited.[^2][^1]

The sequence of instruction should cover:

- Complete sentences, fragments, run-ons, and comma splices.
- Independent/dependent clauses and punctuation with coordinating and subordinating conjunctions.
- Commas after introductory material, around nonessential information, and in lists.
- Subject–verb agreement, pronoun agreement/reference, and consistent verb tense.
- Modifier placement, parallel structure, comparisons, and idiomatic usage.
- Concision, redundancy, precision, and formal tone.
- Logical transitions and relationships such as contrast, cause, example, and continuation.
- Sentence placement, effective introductions, relevant evidence, and conclusions that follow from the passage.

For each editing answer, Josephine must name the tested rule. “It sounds better” is not an acceptable justification. A rule-based explanation is the mastery criterion.

### Main idea and vocabulary

Main idea and vocabulary-in-context were each 50% in June, so both need a repeatable method rather than additional guessing. A main-idea answer must cover the passage’s full scope, include the author’s key point, and avoid unsupported specificity. Josephine should generate her own one-sentence summary before reading the choices.[^1]

For vocabulary in context, she should temporarily cover the target word, substitute a simple word based on the surrounding sentence and paragraph, determine positive/negative/neutral tone, and then select the option matching both meaning and tone. Dictionary meanings that do not fit the local context must be rejected.

## Math accomplishments

### No-return protocol

Before submitting every Math item, Josephine should execute a short four-step protocol:

1. **Ask:** underline or restate exactly what the problem requests.
2. **Plan:** name the governing idea, formula, or equation.
3. **Solve:** keep units and signs visible at every line.
4. **Verify:** estimate magnitude, substitute when possible, and confirm that the selected answer addresses the original question.

The protocol must be practiced until it takes roughly 10–20 seconds on routine items. It replaces the old strategy of planning to revisit uncertain work later, which the 2026 interface does not permit.[^4]

### Geometry

Geometry needs systematic reteaching rather than scattered problem practice. The sequence should be:

- Angle facts: complementary/supplementary angles, vertical angles, angles on a line, triangle angle sum, and parallel-line angle relationships.
- Triangles and quadrilaterals: classification, missing angles, perimeter, area, and decomposing composite figures.
- Circles: radius/diameter, circumference, area, and recognizing when an answer should retain pi.
- Coordinate geometry: distance along horizontal/vertical segments, midpoint reasoning, slope patterns when grade-appropriate, reflections, and area on the coordinate plane.
- Three-dimensional measurement: volume and surface-area reasoning with correct linear, square, or cubic units.
- Scale drawings and similar relationships: distinguish a scale factor for length from its effect on area.

Josephine should maintain a one-page formula sheet during preparation, but each formula must be paired with a diagram and a sentence explaining when it applies. Formula recall alone does not establish mastery.

### Inequalities

Inequalities were 20% on both diagnostics, indicating a persistent gap. Instruction should begin with meaning before procedures: an inequality describes a range of values, not one answer.[^2][^1]

Josephine must be able to:

- Translate phrases such as *at least, no more than, greater than,* and *fewer than*.
- Solve one- and multi-step inequalities.
- Reverse the inequality sign when multiplying or dividing by a negative number and explain why.
- Graph solutions with correct open/closed endpoints and direction.
- Interpret a graphed solution in words.
- Solve compound inequalities and check candidate values.
- Build inequalities from word problems and interpret whole-number constraints in context.

### Percents and measurement

The June percent and measurement results require rebuilding application fluency. Instruction should connect three representations—fraction, decimal, and percent—before moving to percent-of, percent increase/decrease, discount, tax, tip, markup, reverse-percent, and successive-change problems.[^1]

Measurement work should include unit conversions, dimensional labels, perimeter versus area, area versus volume, elapsed measurement, and reasonableness checks. Josephine must write the conversion factor before calculating and circle the requested unit before submitting.

### Data and statistics

Data/statistics was low on both forms and 0% in June. Josephine must interpret tables, line plots, bar graphs, scatter-style displays when included, and two-way information without confusing labels or scales.[^2][^1]

Required skills include:

- Mean, median, mode, and range.
- Finding a missing value from a given mean.
- Weighted totals and averages in context.
- Comparing distributions using center and spread at the tested grade level.
- Reading graph intervals, units, and truncated axes accurately.
- Translating verbal conditions into a table or equation.

### Retention domains

Algebra, ratios/proportions, factors/multiples, probability, and word problems improved substantially by June. They should not consume the main lesson block, but every Math session and homework set must include 4–6 cumulative questions from these areas. If a strength falls below 80% for two consecutive mixed sets, it returns to direct instruction.[^1][^2]

## Lesson architecture

Every 60-minute lesson should follow a consistent structure so that instruction produces measurable change rather than exposure alone:

| Minutes | Activity | Purpose |
|---:|---|---|
| 0–5 | Retrieval warm-up | Maintain prior skills and inspect homework patterns |
| 5–12 | Error-log review | Correct the reasoning that produced errors, not merely the final answer |
| 12–27 | Explicit instruction | Model one strategy or concept with think-aloud reasoning |
| 27–42 | Guided practice | Student explains each step while support is gradually removed |
| 42–55 | Timed digital/no-return set | Transfer the skill to CAT conditions |
| 55–60 | Exit ticket and assignment | Record mastery, unresolved issue, and next task |

Session A should normally include about 40 minutes of ELA, 10 minutes of cumulative Math, and 10 minutes of review/measurement. Session B should reverse that allocation. This interleaving protects June’s Math gains while giving ELA the larger total share.

## Independent work

Two tutoring hours per week are insufficient by themselves to close a roughly 62-point gap from Josephine’s June score to the prior Brooklyn Tech cutoff. The plan therefore requires **four independent sessions of 30–40 minutes per week**, plus scheduled full-length simulations.[^3][^1]

- **After Session A:** one ELA passage set using paragraph-purpose notes, followed by complete correction.
- **After Session B:** 15–20 Math questions, about half from the current topic and half cumulative.
- **Third workday:** 10 revising/editing questions plus vocabulary-in-context practice.
- **Fourth workday:** a digital no-return sprint alternating ELA stand-alone items and mixed Math.
- **Daily micro-review:** 8–10 minutes of formulas, transition relationships, and five vocabulary words in sentences.

Every missed or uncertain item belongs in an error log with six fields: date/source, skill, chosen reasoning, correct reasoning, error category, and a future prevention rule. An item is not corrected until Josephine can solve it again after at least 48 hours without looking at the explanation.

## Simulations

Use the official Student Readiness Tool during Week 1 and official online practice tests for full simulations because NYCPS states that these resources reproduce the exam’s form, content, and functionality. Full 180-minute tests will not fit into a regular tutoring meeting and should be completed at home under adult supervision or in a separately scheduled proctored block.[^4]

Recommended schedule:

- **End of Week 2:** Official digital Practice Test 1, diagnostic purpose only.
- **End of Week 5:** Official digital Practice Test 2, first target check.
- **End of Week 7:** Official digital Practice Test 3, final major simulation.
- **Practice Test 4:** Reserve for an earlier make-up, a confidence-building untimed review, or a final test only if at least five days remain for full analysis and recovery.

For each simulation, record section order, completion time, performance by skill/domain, changed answers within ELA sets, uncertain submissions, interface mistakes, and fatigue by quarter. Compare ELA-first and Math-first at least once each. The preliminary recommendation is **ELA first**, because Josephine’s ELA performance is less stable and passage reasoning is likely to benefit from maximum alertness; the final choice must be based on simulation evidence, not preference.

## Progress benchmarks

Because the CAT transition makes legacy raw-score conversion unreliable, mastery should be monitored through content accuracy, error recurrence, pacing, and any comparable performance score the official platform provides.[^4]

| Checkpoint | ELA benchmark | Math benchmark | CAT benchmark |
|---|---|---|---|
| End Week 2 | 70% untimed on purpose/structure items | 70% on Geometry I | Completes tutorial without navigation errors |
| End Week 4 | 75% on mixed passage questions; 80% editing | 75% geometry/inequality combined | Fewer than 2 premature submissions per sprint |
| End Week 6 | 78–82% on mixed ELA; no priority skill below 70% | 78–82% mixed Math; strengths remain at least 80% | Completes passage-set review within planned time |
| End Week 7 | Approximately 40/50 ELA on a fixed-form content audit | Approximately 40/50 Math on a fixed-form content audit | No interface errors; preventable errors below 10% |

The 40/50 figures are **instructional accuracy targets**, not a promised SHSAT score. The official CAT calculates performance from the response pattern and calibrated item difficulty, then transforms each subject’s performance score to a scaled score before adding the two subtests.[^4]

## Adjustment rules

The plan should change only from evidence:

- If purpose/structure remains below 65% after Week 3, replace one weekly fiction set with additional paragraph-function work and use shorter passages until the reasoning routine is accurate.
- If editing reaches 90% twice, reduce it to one 10-question maintenance set per week and transfer time to fiction or structure.
- If geometry is below 70% after Week 3, pause composite-figure work and reteach prerequisite angle, area, and unit concepts with diagrams.
- If Math mixed accuracy drops while current-topic accuracy stays high, increase cumulative retrieval and reduce single-topic homework.
- If Josephine knows the content but submits preventable wrong answers, shorten timed sets and require the four-step verification protocol before restoring speed.
- If full-test stamina is the main limiter, add one 75–90 minute combined digital block each weekend rather than increasing isolated worksheets.
- If the Week 5 official practice result remains far below the Brooklyn Tech trajectory, narrow instruction to the four highest-yield deficits and discuss Brooklyn Latin as a parallel outcome while retaining Brooklyn Tech as the target.

The most important outcome is not one peak practice score. Josephine needs **repeatable performance**: strong passage-level reasoning, rule-based editing, complete geometry/inequality foundations, retained algebra/ratio strength, and calm one-way decision-making on the digital CAT.
