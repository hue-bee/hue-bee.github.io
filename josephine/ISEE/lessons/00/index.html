Where the 2026 Digital ISEE Differs from the SHSAT

Question the rule change first
On the Fall 2026 SHSAT, math items locked behind you so the adaptive engine could re-score every click. On the Digital ISEE, the section is fixed-form and every question counts equally. What happens to your score if you spend four minutes wrestling Question 14 while three quick items wait at the end?
THE DIGITAL ISEE NAVIGATION RULE
Pass 1: Bank + Flag → Review Screen → Pass 2: Wrestle → Fill All
Clever pivot: “Trade the one-way tunnel for the two-pass loop.”
You can skip, flag, cross out choices, and jump back from the Review Screen inside each active section. Wrong answers carry zero penalty across four choices (A–D), so zero items stay blank.
0:05–0:10 · Zone 1 · Upper Level Architecture

Five Clocks, Two Pairs, and Stanines 1–9

Why two verbal sections and two math sections?
Schools pair Verbal Reasoning with Reading Comprehension, and Quantitative Reasoning with Mathematics Achievement. What does it signal when a student’s logic-based Reasoning score matches—or outpaces—their curriculum Achievement score?
Digital platform mechanics to expect
You cannot exit a section early; if you finish ahead, use remaining minutes on flagged items. A 10-minute alert pops up in every section. Roughly 21 of the 160 items are unscored trial questions mixed invisibly—so treat every item seriously and never stall.
Winter Season timing advantage
December belongs to the Winter season (Dec–Mar). Digital score reports typically post in 3–5 days, leaving comfortable margin ahead of December 31 school deadlines.

Verbal Reasoning: Predict Before Choices Hypnotize You

Why cover the choices first?
Neither standalone synonyms nor sentence completions appeared on the SHSAT. At 30 seconds per item, what happens when you plug all four answer words into the sentence one by one hoping one “sounds smart”?
TWO ITEM FAMILIES IN SECTION 1
~19 Synonyms (Root + Charge) · ~21 Completions (Clue + Pivot)
Clever explanation: “Root Surgery + The One-Blank Guillotine.”
On tough synonyms, slice prefix + root and assign tone (+ / −). On two-blank completions, predict the more constrained blank first and slice away every pair whose half misses—never evaluate two blanks simultaneously.

Worked Walkthrough: Double-Reversal Completion

Model ALevel 4 · Showcase
Far from being the _____ doctrinaire her detractors depicted, the symphony director proved remarkably _____ when hall acoustics required last-minute seating changes.
(A) rigid ... amenable    (B) flexible ... obstinate
(C) austere ... uncompromising    (D) genial ... receptive
Probe the structure before reading choices
What does the opener “Far from being X, she proved Y” guarantee about the relationship between the two blanks—must they agree in tone or oppose each other?
Step 1Map Both Clues and Polarity
“Doctrinaire” (strictly attached to rules) anchors Blank 1 as unyielding (−). “Far from being” flips the second clause to its opposite: adapting to “last-minute seating changes” requires Blank 2 to mean cooperative / adaptable (+).
Step 2Apply the One-Blank Guillotine
Test Blank 1 against “unyielding (−)” alone: flexible in (B) and genial in (D) carry positive, accommodating tones. Cross out (B) and (D) immediately without spending a second checking their second words.
Step 3Break the Tie on Blank 2
Compare survivors (A) and (C) on Blank 2: uncompromising in (C) repeats the negative charge and contradicts adjusting seating, whereas amenable (“open and responsive to change”) completes the reversal cleanly: (A) rigid ... amenable.

Quantitative Comparisons: Compare, Don’t Compute

Meet the brand-new ISEE question type
About 17 items in Section 2 place two quantities in Column A and Column B with fixed choices: (A) A is greater, (B) B is greater, (C) Equal, (D) Cannot be determined. If both columns contain only fixed numbers and zero unknowns, could (D) ever be valid?
THE BALANCE-BEAM + FROZEN PROTOCOL
Cancel shared terms → Test Fractions · 0 · 1 · Negatives · Extremes
Clever explanation: “Play Devil’s Advocate for Choice D”
Add or subtract identical pieces across both columns just like an inequality. When variables remain, test one easy case to find a winner—then actively hunt for a second legal number (0, 1/2, or −2) that reverses the winner.

Worked Walkthrough: Springing the Column D Sign Trap

Model BLevel 4 · Showcase
Given that x ≠ 0 and x2 > x, compare the two quantities:
Column A
x3
Column B
x
Step 1Find Every Legal Region First
Resist dividing x2 > x by x because its sign is unknown. Positive numbers greater than 1 satisfy x2 > x, and every negative number satisfies it too since x2 > 0 > x.
Step 2Test Positive Region (x > 1)
Plug in x = 2 (legal since 4 > 2). Column A is 23 = 8; Column B is 2. Column A wins here, crossing out Choices (B) and (C).
Step 3Stress-Test Negative Region (x < 0)
Plug in x = −2 (legal since (−2)2 = 4 > −2). Now Column A is (−2)3 = −8 while Column B is −2. Because −8 < −2, Column B wins. Opposite outcomes confirm Choice (D).

Reading Comprehension: Six Passages, Zero Poetry

How does Section 3 differ from SHSAT ELA?
There are no grammar/editing items and no poems—just 6 short informational passages (science, history, humanities, contemporary life) with 6 questions each (~5 min 50 sec per set). Why write a 4-word job for each paragraph instead of trying to memorize every fact?
Paragraph Job → Line Window → Own Prediction → Four-Choice Filter
Clever filter: The three ISEE distractor costumes
1. Megaphone words: always, never, prove, solely when the author hedges.
2. Recycled collage: exact nouns from the passage glued into an untrue claim.
3. Wrong zoom level: one-paragraph detail offered as the whole passage’s purpose.

0:52–0:54 · Step-by-Step Solution 3 of 3 · Math Achievement Level 4

Worked Walkthrough: Round-Trip Speed + Final-Step Trap

Model CLevel 4 · Showcase
A river launch travels 24 miles upstream at 8 miles per hour and returns the same 24 miles downstream at 12 miles per hour. How many miles per hour faster is its downstream speed than its average speed for the entire round trip?
Step 1Find Each Leg’s Clock Separately
Never average 8 and 12 to get 10. Upstream takes 24 ÷ 8 = 3 hours; downstream takes 24 ÷ 12 = 2 hours. Because the boat spends longer going slow, the true average must sit below 10 mph.
Step 2Compute Total Distance ÷ Total Time
Round-trip distance is 24 + 24 = 48 miles; total time is 3 + 2 = 5 hours. Divide cleanly: 48 ÷ 5 = 96 ÷ 10 = 9.6 mph.
Step 3Answer the Exact Comparison Asked
ERB plants 9.6 and 2.0 as traps. Re-read the final phrase—downstream speed minus round-trip average speed: 12.0 − 9.6 = 2.4 mph.

The Typed Essay: Unscored Does Not Mean Unread

Why do schools receive an ungraded copy?
Application essays go through weeks of home editing. What does an admissions committee learn by reading a raw 30-minute typed response right beside your polished application?
Clever blueprint: “4–22–4 with Two Concrete Scenes”
Prompts ask about a person, challenge, community need, or class you would design. Spend 4 minutes outlining a clear thesis plus two specific, named examples on scratch paper; 22 minutes typing 4 crisp paragraphs; 4 minutes upgrading verbs and fixing typos.
