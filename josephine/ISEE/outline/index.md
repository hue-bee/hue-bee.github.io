# ISEE Upper Level: Test Overview (2026–27)

## What is the ISEE?

The Independent School Entrance Exam (ISEE) is an admission test used by independent (private) schools. The Upper Level is for students applying to grades 9 through 12. It is administered by E3n, the organization formed in 2026 when ERB and EMA merged.

The test has five sections, always given in the same order: four scored multiple-choice sections followed by an unscored essay.

## At a glance

- **Who takes it:** Students applying to grades 9–12
- **Multiple-choice questions:** 160, across four scored sections
- **Essay:** 1 prompt, unscored
- **Testing time:** 2 hours 40 minutes (130 minutes multiple choice + 30 minutes essay)
- **Breaks:** Two, 10 minutes each
- **Total time in the seat:** About 3 hours, plus instructions
- **Answer choices:** Four per question
- **Wrong-answer penalty:** None, so every question should be answered
- **Calculator:** Not permitted
- **Formats:** Online or paper, with the same question counts and timing

## Section 1: Verbal Reasoning (40 questions, 20 minutes)

This section measures vocabulary and verbal reasoning. It has two question types, split roughly in half.

- **Synonyms (vocabulary):** A single word is given, and the student picks the answer choice closest in meaning. These test word knowledge directly, including roots, prefixes, and suffixes.
- **Sentence completions:** A sentence has one or two blanks, and the student picks the word or pair of words that best completes it. These test vocabulary in context and the ability to follow the logic of a sentence (contrast, cause and effect, definition).

This is the fastest-paced section, at about 30 seconds per question.

## Section 2: Quantitative Reasoning (37 questions, 35 minutes)

This section measures how a student reasons with numbers, more than how much math has been studied. Many questions can be solved with little or no calculation by estimating, comparing, or spotting structure.

- **Word problems (18 scored):** Short problems of one to three sentences.
- **Quantitative comparisons (14 scored):** Two quantities, Column A and Column B, are given. The student decides whether A is greater, B is greater, the two are equal, or the relationship cannot be determined.

Content areas drawn on:

- Number sense
- Algebraic concepts
- Geometry
- Measurement
- Data analysis and probability

## Section 3: Reading Comprehension (36 questions, 35 minutes)

This section has six passages with six questions each. Passages vary widely in topic and are drawn from areas such as history and social sciences, science and technology, and arts and humanities.

Question types:

- **Main idea:** The central point or purpose of the passage
- **Supporting ideas:** Specific details stated in the passage
- **Inference:** Conclusions that follow from the passage but are not stated outright
- **Vocabulary in context:** The meaning of a word or phrase as used in the passage
- **Organization and logic:** Sequence, pattern, relationship, and structure of the passage
- **Tone, style, and figurative language:** The author's attitude and use of language

No outside knowledge of the subject is needed. The skill is identifying the passage's structure and the author's position.

## Section 4: Mathematics Achievement (47 questions, 40 minutes)

This section is aligned with grade-level standards, so it tests math that students have learned in school. It focuses on computation, procedures, and knowledge of mathematical terminology. All questions are standard multiple choice.

| Skill area | Approx. scored questions |
|---|---|
| Algebraic concepts | 13–17 |
| Data analysis and probability | 8–13 |
| Number sense | 5–11 |
| Geometry | 5–8 |
| Measurement | 5–8 |

Topics that commonly appear include:

- Order of operations, fractions, decimals, factors, and multiples
- Absolute value and number types
- Algebraic expressions, equations, and inequalities
- Linear equations and their graphs
- Function notation and multiplying polynomials
- Distance, rate, and time problems
- Midpoint and distance formulas
- Mean, median, mode, range, and weighted average
- Reading tables and graphs
- Combinations and probability
- Matrices

## Section 5: Essay (1 prompt, 30 minutes)

- The student receives one prompt, with no choice of topic.
- The prompt is a sentence or two asking the student to describe or explain something about themselves, their community, or the wider world. Some prompts invite the student to take a position.
- On paper, the student writes by hand on two lined pages and may not go beyond them. Online, the response is typed.
- There is no required word count. A complete, organized essay of three to five paragraphs is a sensible target.
- The essay is not scored, but a copy is sent to every school that receives the student's score report.

A workable 30-minute plan is about 5 minutes to plan, 20 minutes to write, and 5 minutes to proofread.

## Scoring

- Each correct answer earns one raw point. Nothing is deducted for wrong or blank answers.
- Each of the four scored sections receives its own results. There is no total or composite score.
- **Scaled score:** 760 to 940 per section
- **Percentile rank:** 1 to 99, comparing the student with other applicants to the same grade
- **Stanine:** 1 to 9, which groups percentile ranks into nine bands. This is the number many admission offices look at first.
- Results for online tests usually arrive within about 3 to 5 days.

## Test dates and locations

A student may take the ISEE once in each of three testing seasons per admission year:

- **Fall:** August through November
- **Winter:** December through March
- **Spring/Summer:** April through July

Testing options:

- At home, online with a remote proctor
- At a member school, on paper or online
- At a testing office, on paper or online
- At a Prometric test center, online
