Digital Format · Mental Model

Adaptive Means the Route Changes, Not the Destination

Fixed

What everyone shares

50 ELA questions, 50 Math questions, grade-level standards, and 180 total minutes.

Adaptive

What can change

The difficulty of a later item can respond to the pattern of earlier responses.

Best response
Treat the item in front of you as the only item that matters. Difficulty guessing spends attention without earning evidence.
Adaptive Thinking

Three Myths to Replace

Myth

“This feels easy, so I am doing badly.”

Feeling is not measurement. Topic familiarity, wording, and fatigue all change perceived difficulty.

Replace with: “What does this item ask me to prove?”
Myth

“One miss ruins the route.”

The estimate is updated from the response pattern, not from a single dramatic moment.

Replace with: “Reset before the next item.”
Myth

“Harder must mean slower.”

Some difficult items collapse quickly when represented well; some simple items punish careless reading.

Replace with: “Spend time where progress is possible.”
Discuss: Which replacement thought would save you the most attention during a long test? Defend the choice with a reason.

Difficulty Ladder

Level Changes Mean More Connections

Level 1

One visible move

The needed operation or detail is stated directly.

Ask: What fact is already waiting for me?
Level 2

Two linked moves

A familiar idea must be translated before it is used.

Ask: What should I represent first?
Level 3

Choose the pathway

Several methods look possible; one is cleaner or safer.

Ask: Which structure reduces the work?
Level 4

Coordinate constraints

Multiple conditions, tempting traps, or evidence layers interact.

Ask: What must be true at the same time?
Important distinction
A Level 4 item is not necessarily “advanced math” or “hard vocabulary.” It often uses grade-level ideas but requires tighter organization.
Math Lens · Representation

Translate Before You Calculate

Sentence

“The length is 6 feet more than twice the width.”

L = 2w + 6
Probe: Which part of the sentence creates multiplication? Which part creates addition? Why is 2(w + 6) a different story?
Turn-and-test: Change “6 feet more than twice” to “twice the sum of the width and 6.” What must change in both the equation and diagram?
Math Practice · Easy to Hard

One Habit, Four Levels

1Level 1
Four identical tickets cost $28. What is the cost of one ticket? What unit rate is requested?
2Level 2
A recipe uses 3/4 cup of oats for 6 bars. How many cups are needed for 14 bars? Would a table or unit rate be cleaner?
3Level 3
A rectangle’s length is 6 feet more than twice its width. Its perimeter is 48 feet. Find its dimensions. Where does each side appear in the perimeter?
4Level 4
A tank is 3/5 full. After 18 gallons are added, it is 3/4 full. Find the tank’s capacity. What quantity stays unchanged?
Digital habit
Before opening a calculator or typing, build a representation. A clean model protects you from doing accurate arithmetic on the wrong quantities.
Guided Model · Level 3

Rectangle: Turn Words into Constraints

1
Notice the target and totalUnknown dimensions; perimeter is 48 feet.
2
Model both relationshipsL = 2w + 6 and 2L + 2w = 48.
3
Substitute, then simplify2(2w + 6) + 2w = 48 → 6w + 12 = 48 → w = 6.
4
Return and verifyL = 2(6) + 6 = 18; check 2(18) + 2(6) = 48.
Why this is Level 3
The arithmetic is routine. The challenge is coordinating a verbal relationship with the perimeter formula and knowing where to substitute.
Guided Model · Level 4

Tank Capacity: Make the Difference Visible

1
Name the wholeLet C be the tank’s total capacity.
2
Represent the change(3/4)C − (3/5)C = 18.
3
Find the fractional gap15/20 − 12/20 = 3/20, so (3/20)C = 18.
4
Solve and test the storyC = 120; the two amounts are 72 and 90 gallons, a difference of 18.
Deeper question: Why would adding 3/5 + 3/4 be structurally wrong even if the fraction arithmetic were perfect?

Diagnose the Representation Error

ChallengeLevel 4

A streaming plan charges $12 per month plus $3 per movie. A customer paid $39. Which equation models the number of movies m?

A12m + 3 = 39
B3(m + 12) = 39
C12 + 3m = 39
D12 + 39m = 3
Click once to select; shift-click to eliminate.

Interrogate each term

Which number is fixed? Which amount repeats? What does the variable count? Which expression should carry the variable?

ELA Lens · Evidence

Build a Bridge from Claim to Text

Question

What must be proved?

Restate the task as a claim with a blank.

Evidence

Which words do the proving?

Point to a specific detail, pattern, contrast, or change.

Literal

The text states the idea directly.

Move: locate and match.

Inferential

Two or more details support an unstated conclusion.

Move: combine without inventing.

Structural

Placement and organization reveal purpose.

Move: ask why this part is here.
ELA trap
An option can be true in real life, related to the topic, and still unsupported by this text. Relevance is not evidence.
ELA Text · Read with Purpose

The Rooftop Weather Station

1When the science club installed a weather station on the school roof, the first week of data looked disappointingly ordinary. The temperature rose by day and fell at night; the wind shifted without any obvious pattern. Several students wondered whether the project had been worth the effort.

2Mara noticed, however, that the roof stayed warmer than the nearby park after sunset. Instead of announcing a conclusion, she asked the club to compare clear nights with cloudy ones and to check whether the station’s dark platform affected the readings.

3Over the next month, the students moved a spare sensor among grass, pavement, and a shaded wall. Their measurements did not produce one simple rule. They did reveal that surfaces released stored heat at different rates—and that the placement of a sensor could shape the story the numbers seemed to tell.

First read: What changes between paragraph 1 and paragraph 3?

Margin map

P1: ordinary data → doubt
P2: pattern noticed → question refined
P3: comparison tested → nuanced insight

Compression sentence

Write one sentence using although or but to capture the passage’s main movement.

Read aloud: Each partner proposes a 12-word summary. Keep the version that preserves both the investigation and its caution.

3Level 3

Level-up signal
The question moves from locating one fact to explaining how multiple details work together. Your evidence must become more connected, not merely longer.
Guided Model · Level 3

Inference: Combine Without Inventing

Detail 1

Mara notices a pattern but does not immediately announce a conclusion.

Detail 2

She asks for comparisons and checks whether the platform affects the readings.

Inference

Mara treats an initial pattern as a hypothesis to test, not as proof.

1
Restate the taskDescribe Mara’s approach—not the weather result.
2
Collect two actionsShe delays a conclusion and requests controlled comparisons.
3
Name the shared qualityCareful, skeptical, evidence-testing.
4
Check the boundaryDo not claim she distrusts all data; the text supports caution, not rejection.
Precision check: Which single word in the inference would become too strong if changed to “proves”?

Digital Tools · Strategic Use

A Tool Helps Only When It Has a Job

Highlight

Mark the task, constraint, or evidence—not entire paragraphs.

Use when: wording controls the solution.

Eliminate

Cross out an option only after naming its flaw.

Use when: two choices remain plausible.

Notepad

Store a model, unit, short equation, or paragraph map.

Use when: working memory is crowded.

Bookmark

Mark uncertainty only if the interface permits a return and time remains.

Use when: progress has stopped.
Tool misuse
Highlighting everything, writing full sentences of scratch work, or repeatedly toggling choices creates activity without adding structure.
Tool test
Before clicking, finish this sentence: “I am using this tool to ______.” If the blank is unclear, keep reading or modeling.
