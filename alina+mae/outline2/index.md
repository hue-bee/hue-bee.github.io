# SHSAT (Computer-Adaptive) Study Plan — Alina Afshar & Mae Held

**Target exam:** November 2027 (8th grade) · **Prepared:** October 22, 2026 · **Format:** one joint 90-minute session per week

Both students posted identical headline numbers (19/22 ELA, 11/20 Math) on the Oct 18, 2026 diagnostic, but the reports describe two different students: Alina is a strong reasoner who loses points to precision and pacing; Mae has genuine conceptual gaps in proportional reasoning, percent, probability, and grade-8 content she has not met yet. The plan runs 47 weekly sessions from the week of Oct 26, 2026 through the week of Nov 1, 2027, in four phases with three checkpoints and six at-home full-length simulations.

## 2. The format you're preparing them for

- **ELA:** 50 questions on reading comprehension and revising/editing. Within a passage set students can return to items until they submit the set; stand-alone items and anything in a submitted set are closed.
- **Math:** 50 word problems and computational items, forward-only (no returning after advancing), no formulas or definitions provided, equation editor for typed responses.
- **Overall:** 100 questions in 180 minutes; students choose which section to start with; every item must be answered before advancing; **no calculator**.
- **Item types:** multi-select, drag-and-drop, inline choice, number-line/graph interaction.
- **Adaptivity:** each correct answer may route to a harder item and each miss to an easier one; everyone answers the same 50 per section.
- **Pace reality:** ~1.8 min/item on Math is tighter than the ~2 min/item both girls used on the diagnostic, so pacing is an explicit curriculum thread.

## 3. Individual plans: what each student must accomplish

### Alina — convert reasoning into points

**Five-month objective:** zero misses on tier ≤ 3.2 math items and ≥ 90% on ELA evidence questions.

- **Order of operations with negatives.** State and apply −4² = −16 vs. (−4)² = 16; distribute negatives through parentheses. (She got 5 on −4²+7(5−7) instead of −30.) *Deliverable:* 20/20 on a mixed integer/exponent drill by session 3.
- **Linear equations with variables on both sides.** Both 4x+7=5x and 6x+9=7x went wrong and consumed nearly 9 minutes combined. One fixed routine — move the smaller x-term, isolate, substitute back to check — executed in under 60 seconds. This is her biggest point-and-time leak.
- **Percent as a multiplier.** Her 46.8 means she multiplied by 1.04 instead of 1.14. Read percents as decimals aloud ("14% is 0.14"); increase = original × (1 + r); then extend to reverse percent and successive changes.
- **The "test with numbers" reflex.** On "always even for every integer n" she picked 2n+1 in 37 s. Rule: any always/never/every-integer item gets n = 1, 2, 3 plugged in before committing. Same reflex for systems — compare coefficient ratios (10x+8y=132 is exactly 2× the first equation) before solving.
- **Surface area and volume with a drawn net.** Her 50 for the 6×5×10 open box shows she counted one face, not five. Draw the solid, list every face, then compute.
- **Pacing discipline.** Learn a 2.5-minute hard cap (Q10 took 386 s for a correct 720/7 — on the real test that costs three other items). Conversely, a 45-second *floor* on "which sentence best supports" items (her Q2 miss was at 23 s).
- **ELA evidence protocol.** Restate the claim in her own words, then ask whether the candidate sentence *proves* it rather than *relates* to it. (Q17: chose sentence 7 over 17; Q18: chose a non-numerical sentence over the one with event results.) Drill the two-select format — it is a confirmed CAT item type.

### Mae — build the multiplicative and grade-8 foundation

**Objective:** move her routing ceiling from tier 3.4 to tier 4+ by June through concept-building, not polishing.

- **Multiplicative vs. additive reasoning (most important fix).** 66 for a 5:7 scale-up of 64, and 5:14:7 for the three-part ratio, both come from adding instead of scaling. Bar models and scale-factor language ("5:7 means multiply by 7/5"), then the common-multiple method (30:48:56), then similar-figure perimeters and areas.
- **Percent of a number from scratch.** 54 for 45 up 14% after 230 s indicates no stable method. Build 10%/1% blocks (4.5 + 1.8 = 6.3 → 51.3), then the multiplier form, then reverse percent.
- **Reading all the givens.** Her 39 mph ignored the 20-minute stop. Routine: underline every number, then ask "did I use each one?"
- **Compound probability.** 2/5 was P(red on one draw). Teach the complement rule, 1 − P(both blue) = 1 − (9/15)(8/14) = 23/35, built from tree diagrams with shrinking denominators.
- **Distributive property both ways.** 4(x+12) ≠ 4x+12. Verify equivalence by substituting x = 1 and x = 2 (also prepares multi-select equivalence items).
- **Grade-8 content she hasn't seen.** Function notation (gave −5 for f(5) = 3(5) − 4), cylinder/cone/sphere volume (168π vs. 396π), slope-intercept from two points, exponents and scientific notation, Pythagorean theorem, transformations. Pre-taught May–June 2027 so she isn't meeting them for the first time in school in October 2027.
- **Speed on what she knows.** Solved the system correctly in 393 s; elimination practice should cut that under 3 minutes.
- **ELA extreme-language traps.** Her Q6 and Q14 wrong answers contain absolutes or reversals ("always the least expensive," "citywide averages are more useful"). Rule: circle always/never/only/more-than in answer choices and demand textual proof before selecting. R/E Part A focus on paragraph purpose and transitions.

## 4. Group sequence and session design

The two share enough (percent, both-sides equations, quantitative evidence, pacing) that one concept per session works, with differentiated problem sets at the same concept — Alina gets tier-4 extensions and precision targets; Mae gets scaffolded tier-3 sets and concept checks.

| Phase | Weeks | Sessions | Math thread | ELA thread |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1 Foundations** | Oct 26 – Jan 18 | 1–10 | Integers/order of ops → fractions → equations (both sides) → percent I/II → ratios & rates I/II → expressions; Checkpoint 1 | Evidence protocol, inference and trap taxonomy, R/E Part B conventions, R/E Part A paragraph logic |
| **2 Core content** | Jan 25 – Apr 26 | 11–22 | Inequalities, number properties, geometry I/II (area, SA/volume), statistics, probability, systems, coordinate geometry, integrated word problems, tech-enhanced formats; full 50-item ELA and Math timed sets; Checkpoint 2 | Full timed passage sets with per-set budgets, poetry, argumentative texts, paired passages |
| **3 Grade-8 pre-teach** | May 3 – Jun 21 | 23–30 | Exponents/scientific notation, roots & Pythagorean, functions, linear graphs, transformations/similarity, cylinder/cone/sphere volume, bivariate data; full-length #1 and Checkpoint 3 | Harder informational and literary nonfiction, section-order decision |
| **Summer volume** | Jun 28 – Aug 30 | 31–38 | Mixed timed sets at 1.8 min/item, station rotation on each student's top-5 errors, tech-enhanced bootcamp, forward-only commit drills; full-lengths #2–#3 | Speed-annotation protocol, multi-select/two-part items |
| **4 Simulation & taper** | Sep 6 – Nov 1 | 39–47 | Biweekly full-lengths #4–#6 (last on the NYCPS official practice test), weak-domain sprints, formula write-out from memory, test-day plan | Hardest passage sets, final conventions review |

**Session shape (90 min):**

1. 0–10 min — no-calculator warm-up, 6 mixed items, 90-second locked timer each (simulates forward-only)
2. 10–45 min — Math block: new concept (15), guided practice (10), independent timed set (10)
3. 45–50 min — break
4. 50–80 min — ELA block: skill mini-lesson (8), one timed passage/R&E set (14), debrief with evidence justification (8)
5. 80–90 min — error log (category + fix for every miss) and homework assignment

**Homework:** 4 days × 30–45 min — 10–15 math items daily (no calculator), two passage or R/E sets weekly, error-log entries. Summer rises to 60 items + 2 sets per week.
