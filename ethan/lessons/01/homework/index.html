Part A — Composite 3D Solids: Volume & Surface Area

4 Problems · ~15 min

Split every composite solid into shapes you already know before you calculate anything. Show ALL work.

Formulas V(prism) = l·w·h V(pyramid) = ⅓·B·h V(cylinder) = πr²h V(cone) = ⅓πr²h SA = sum of all faces
Problem 1 L3

A rectangular box (base 12 cm × 10 cm, height 10 cm) has a square pyramid attached on top with the same base and height h. The total volume is 1,560 cm³. Find h.

  • 1 Write the box's volume and the pyramid's volume as two separate formulas first.
  • 2 The box has no unknowns — calculate it fully before touching the pyramid.
  • 3 Subtract the box's volume from 1,560 to isolate the pyramid's volume.
  • 4 Plug the known base area into V = ⅓Bh and solve for h.
h = cm
Problem 2 L2

What is the total surface area of a rectangular prism with dimensions 5 in × 5 in × 9 in?

  • 1 Two faces are 5×5 squares; the other four are 5×9 rectangles.
  • 2 SA = 2(lw) + 2(lh) + 2(wh) — plug in 5, 5, 9 for each term.
  • 3 Add all six face areas together before you pick an answer.
Answer: in²
Problem 3 L3

A cylinder (radius 5 in, height 12 in) has a cone (same radius, height 8 in) sitting on top. Find the total volume, in terms of π.

  • 1 Find V(cylinder) = πr²h and V(cone) = ⅓πr²h separately.
  • 2 Both shapes share the same radius, so B = πr² is identical for both formulas.
  • 3 Add the two volumes together — leave π in your final answer, don't multiply it out.
Answer: in³ (in terms of π)
Problem 4 L4

A composite solid is a cube of side 8 cm with a smaller cube of side 3 cm removed from one corner. Find (a) the remaining volume and (b) whether the total surface area increases, decreases, or stays the same compared to the original cube. Explain in one sentence.

  • 1 Remaining volume = big cube's volume − small cube's volume.
  • 2 For surface area, think about what happens at the notch: some area is removed, but new inner faces are exposed.
(a) V = cm³
(b) SA

Part C — Inequality Shading & Coordinate Geometry

2 Problems · ~10 min

Never trust the line alone — always test a point to confirm the direction of shading.

Problem 8 L3

The graph shows a solid boundary line with the region below-right of it shaded. Which inequality matches the shaded region if the line's equation is y = −x + 6?

  • 1 The line is solid, so the inequality includes "or equal to."
  • 2 Pick a clear point inside the shaded region, like (6, 0), and test it in each candidate inequality.
  • 3 The correct inequality is the one that makes your test point TRUE.
Inequality: y −x + 6
Problem 9 L4

In right triangle ABC (right angle at A), AL is the angle bisector of angle A, and angle B measures 25°. If CL = 15 cm, find the length of LB.

  • 1 AL splits the 90° right angle into two 45° angles — mark both on your sketch.
  • 2 In right triangle ACL, you know one angle (45°) and the side opposite it (CL = 15). Use tangent to find AC.
  • 3 Use the Angle Bisector Theorem: AB/AC = LB/LC to solve for LB.
LB = cm
