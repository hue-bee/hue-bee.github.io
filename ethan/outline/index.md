# SHSAT Grade 9 Outline — Fall 2026 Testing
### For current 9th graders competing for 10th-grade seats (entry September 2027)

---

## 1. What Makes the Grade 9 Test Different

The Grade 9 form is a **superset of the Grade 8 form**: same two sections, same length, same timing, same adaptive delivery — but the content assumes a full additional year of school.

| Feature | Grade 8 Form | Grade 9 Form |
|---|---|---|
| Math content basis | NYS standards through Grade 7 | Material through **Grade 8** |
| Math emphasis | Pre-algebra plus early algebra | Full Algebra I foundations plus intro geometry |
| ELA reading level | Middle-school texts | Denser syntax, more sophisticated argument and poetry |
| Sitting | School day in November, plus weekend sessions | Separate sitting, historically a **December weekend** |
| Seats available | ~4,000+ across eight schools | Extremely few — roughly 3–10 at Stuyvesant and Bronx Science, ~20–30 at Brooklyn Tech |
| Cutoff scores | Lower | Substantially higher and far more volatile year to year |

**Strategic reality:** because seats are scarce, the qualifying score can swing wildly. Recent Grade 9 lowest-qualifying scores include Stuyvesant 562 (2025), 572 (2024), 575 (2023); Bronx Science 525 (2025) but 650 (2024); Staten Island Tech 572 (2025) versus 524 (2024). Plan for a near-perfect performance, not for a historical average.

## 2. Format at a Glance (Fall 2026)

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| Sections | English Language Arts and Mathematics |
| Questions | 50 per section — 100 total |
| Time | 180 minutes, entirely student-managed, no scheduled break |
| Delivery | Digital on school-provided computers |
| Adaptivity | **Computer-adaptive at the item level** — new for fall 2026 |
| Section order | Student chooses which section to begin |
| Calculator | Not permitted |
| Formula sheet | Not provided |
| Scoring | 1 raw point per scored item regardless of difficulty; raw → scaled per section → composite; no guessing penalty |
| Navigation (Math) | Forward-only |
| Navigation (ELA) | Review allowed only inside a passage set until submitted; stand-alone items forward-only |
| Item types | Multiple choice (4 options), grid-in numeric entry, and technology-enhanced items — drag-and-drop, multiple-select, inline drop-downs, number-line and graph interactions |

## 3. Mathematics (50 questions)

Same five official domains as the Grade 8 form, but questions may assume completion of 8th-grade math. The starred items below are where the Grade 9 form typically reaches beyond the Grade 8 form.

### 3.1 Ratios and Proportional Relationships
- Ratio, rate, unit rate, unit conversion
- Proportions, scale drawings, similar figures
- Percent: increase and decrease, discount, tax, tip, simple and compound interest
- Constant of proportionality across tables, graphs, and equations
- Work, rate, distance, and mixture problems
- ★ Comparing proportional relationships represented in different forms

### 3.2 The Number System and Expressions
- Integer and rational-number operations, absolute value, order of operations
- Fractions, decimals, and repeating-decimal conversions
- ★ Laws of exponents, including negative and zero exponents
- ★ Scientific notation: operations and comparisons of magnitude
- ★ Square and cube roots; estimating and approximating irrational numbers
- Primes, factors, multiples, GCF, LCM, divisibility
- Translating verbal descriptions into algebraic expressions
- Simplifying, distributing, and generating equivalent expressions
- ★ Factoring, including common factors, difference of squares, and simple trinomials
- Sequences, patterns, and consecutive-integer problems

### 3.3 Equations, Inequalities, and Functions
- Multi-step linear equations and inequalities, including variables on both sides
- ★ Systems of two linear equations solved algebraically and graphically; recognizing zero, one, or infinite solutions
- Literal equations and formula rearrangement
- Slope, intercepts, slope-intercept and point-slope forms, graphing lines
- ★ Function concept and notation: input-output relationships, evaluating \(f(x)\), interpreting graphs and tables, rate of change
- ★ Distinguishing linear from nonlinear relationships
- ★ Basic quadratic patterns and perfect squares
- Representing inequality solutions on a number line

### 3.4 Statistics and Probability
- Mean, median, mode, range; weighted and missing-value averages
- Data displays: tables, bar and line graphs, box plots, histograms
- ★ Scatterplots, clustering, outliers, association, and line of best fit
- ★ Two-way tables and relative frequency
- Simple and compound probability, complements, independent events
- Counting principle; basic combinations and permutations
- Sampling and inference about a population

### 3.5 Geometry
- Angle relationships: complementary, supplementary, vertical, parallel lines cut by a transversal
- Triangle properties, angle sums, exterior angles
- ★ Pythagorean theorem, its converse, and applications in three dimensions
- ★ Distance between two points in the coordinate plane
- Perimeter, circumference, and area of polygons and circles
- ★ Volume of cylinders, cones, and spheres in addition to prisms; surface area
- ★ Transformations — translations, reflections, rotations, dilations — and congruence and similarity criteria
- Coordinate geometry: plotting figures, slope of parallel and perpendicular lines

### 3.6 Two Non-Negotiables
- **Memorize the formulas.** Nothing is supplied. Area, perimeter, circumference, volume, surface area, distance, slope, exponent rules, and vocabulary such as *parallel* and *perpendicular* must be automatic.
- **Rehearse the Equation Editor.** Grid-in responses are typed through an on-screen palette; practice entering fractions, radicals, parentheses, exponents, and the multiplication dot under time.

## 4. English Language Arts (50 questions)

Aligned to the NYS Next Generation ELA Standards, with passages and revising tasks pitched at a higher reading level than the Grade 8 form. Three question families.

### 4.1 Reading Comprehension
The largest share of the section; each passage carries a question set.

**Informational passage types**
- Expository and explanatory writing
- Argument and opinion pieces, speeches, personal essays
- Essays about art or literature
- Biography, memoir, journalism
- Historical, scientific, technical, or economic accounts for a general audience

**Literary passage types**
- Poetry
- Adventure, historical fiction, mystery, science fiction, realistic fiction
- Myths and creative nonfiction

**Skills assessed**
- Central idea, summary, and paraphrase
- Inference and text-based evidence selection
- Word and phrase meaning in context; tone and connotation
- Author's purpose, point of view, and rhetorical choices
- Text structure and the effect of organization on meaning
- Interpretation of accompanying charts, diagrams, or images
- Glossed vocabulary appears with a dotted underline and a pop-up definition

### 4.2 Revising/Editing Part A — Passage-Based
A passage with numbered sentences, often on history, current events, technology, or the biological, physical, and social sciences.

- Language misuse and imprecise word choice
- Missing supporting detail, or detail that does not belong
- Missing or inappropriate transitions
- Weak or absent introduction or conclusion
- Illogical organization — add, delete, move, or replace a sentence

### 4.3 Revising/Editing Part B — Stand-Alone Items
A single sentence, a short list of sentences, or a brief numbered paragraph.

- Choose the best correction for an error
- Identify which sentence contains the error
- Combine or revise sentences to clarify relationships between ideas

**High-frequency grammar targets**
- Verb tense consistency and inappropriate shifts
- Misplaced and dangling modifiers
- Fragments, run-ons, faulty parallelism
- Subject-verb and pronoun-antecedent agreement; pronoun clarity
- Punctuation: commas, semicolons, colons, apostrophes, dashes
- Conciseness, precision, redundancy

### 4.4 On-Screen Tools
Highlighter, answer eliminator, notepad, digital pencil, line-reader mask, bookmark (within a passage set only), pop-up definitions, and the Equation Editor in Math.

## 5. Strategy for the Adaptive Grade 9 Test

1. The opening item or passage set is average difficulty; each answer re-estimates the score and sets what comes next, so early accuracy determines the difficulty path for the rest of the section.
2. Harder items are not worth more — every scored item is one raw point. Accuracy wins; hunting for hard questions does not.
3. Two-pass timing is dead in Math. Train a commit-and-move rule: at about 90 seconds, eliminate, choose the strongest remaining option, advance.
4. Never leave a blank. There is no guessing penalty, and an answer is required to move forward.
5. Inside an ELA passage set, bookmark hard items, finish the set, then revisit before submitting — submission locks the set.
6. Because Grade 9 cutoffs sit near the top of the scale, the practical target is close to zero avoidable errors. Error-log every miss by cause: content gap, misread, careless arithmetic, or timing.
7. Rehearse in the actual interface through the NYC SHSAT Portal — the Student Readiness Tool tutorial plus the full-length online practice tests.

## 6. Logistics and Accommodations

- **Registration** runs through the student's school counselor or the MySchools dashboard. Test tickets, showing date, time, and location, post roughly two weeks after the registration deadline.
- **Timing of the Grade 9 sitting:** historically a December weekend, after the 8th-grade November administration. Published 2026 dates have varied across sources, so confirm the exact Grade 9 date and deadline with the school counselor or the official NYC Public Schools SHSAT page.
- **Results** are released in March, alongside high school offers.
- **All students** get universal platform features: zoom, highlighting, note-taking.
- **English Language Learners and eligible former ELLs** receive 360 minutes with two 15-minute breaks after the first 180, translated directions in 12 languages, translated ELA footnotes, and paper bilingual glossaries.
- **IEP and 504 accommodations** are delivered through the platform; paper exams are reserved for students whose approved accommodations require them.
- **Test View:** students who test in fall 2026 may sign up to review their exam in spring 2027 after scores are released.

## 7. Suggested Prep Sequence

| Phase | Focus |
|---|---|
| Diagnostic | Full-length official online practice test in the real interface; log error causes, not just the score |
| Grade 8 content sweep | Exponent laws, scientific notation, radicals, functions, systems, transformations, Pythagorean applications, scatterplots — the starred topics above |
| ELA calibration | Two timed passage sets per session at elevated reading level; daily short grammar sets |
| Adaptive conditioning | Forward-only Math drills with a hard 90-second commit rule |
| Final 3 weeks | Weekly full-lengths, Equation Editor speed work, section-order rehearsal, and a target score set above the highest recent Grade 9 cutoff for the schools in play |
