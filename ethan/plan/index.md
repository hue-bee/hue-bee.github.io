## Sequenced 10-Week Plan

The sequence below assumes 2–3 sessions per week (45–60 minutes each) plus one weekly timed practice set. Order matters: each phase is built to be completed before you move to the next, since later phases assume mastery of earlier skills (e.g., inequality graphing before compound inequality word problems).

### Phase 1 (Weeks 1–2): ELA Foundations — Passage Structure and Main Idea

Your weakest ELA skills are Analyzing Organization/Structure (42.9 percent) and Main Idea (50 percent), and these are prerequisite skills for every other ELA question type. Sessions should teach you to annotate paragraph function (intro/transition/support/conclusion) while reading, then summarize each paragraph in five words before answering structure questions. Drill with 3–4 short nonfiction passages per session, reviewing every wrong answer by asking "what is this paragraph's job in the passage?" Build a personal glossary of transition-word cues (however, although, in addition) since several of your missed items (Q8, Q24, Q49, Q53) involved misreading a structural pivot.

### Phase 2 (Weeks 2–3): ELA Poetry Deep Dive

Your poetry passages scored only 25 percent, the single lowest ELA category, so this gets a dedicated phase rather than being folded into general reading practice. Work on speaker identification, tone shifts between stanzas, and literal vs. figurative language before touching multiple-choice questions — you missed poetry items tied to comparing two speakers' roles (Q40, Q41), suggesting you need explicit practice tracking "who is speaking and what do they want" across stanzas. Use 2 poems per session, paired with think-aloud annotation.

### Phase 3 (Weeks 3–4): ELA Detail and Inference Precision

You missed Detail questions at a meaningfully higher rate than Inference (53.85 percent vs 80 percent), which is unusual since Detail questions should be the "easiest" (answer is textually stated). This points to careless line-reference errors rather than a comprehension gap. Train yourself to physically underline the cited line/paragraph before selecting an answer, and to eliminate answer choices that add information not present in the text. Mix in "Connecting Specific Information to Main Idea" questions here since they share the same underline-and-verify strategy.

### Phase 4 (Weeks 4–5): ELA Revising/Editing and Writing Mechanics

Your Writing/Revising sat at 50 percent, your weakest non-reading ELA category. Focus on comma usage, sentence-combining logic, and transition selection using short grammar drills (10–15 questions per session) rather than full passages, since these are rule-based and improve quickly with spaced repetition. Review your missed items like Q6, Q8, and Q9 as a set — all three involve choosing the correct logical connector between sentences.

### Phase 5 (Weeks 5–6): Math — Measurement and Data/Statistics Reset

These are your two weakest math domains (0 percent and 33.3 percent), but they likely reflect unfamiliarity with a specific format (unit conversion, scatterplot/table trend reading) rather than a math ability gap given your 80.7 percent overall math score. Reteach unit conversion chains (feet↔inches↔yards, metric prefixes) and how to read rate-of-change from a table (the item you missed, Q63, involved identifying a linear rate pattern in a table). For Data/Statistics, practice reading line graphs for "rate" questions like the Asia-travel-graph item, since slope-as-rate is the recurring skill.

### Phase 6 (Weeks 6–7): Math — Probability and Inequalities

Your Probability sat at 44.4 percent and Inequalities at 40 percent, with the practice pages confirming continued struggles on "at least" probability problems (Q72, Q74) and coordinate-plane inequality shading (Q106, Q110). Work on the complement rule (1 − P(none/fewer)) explicitly for "at least" problems, and drill translating inequality statements into correct shaded regions/boundary line types (solid vs. dashed) using a step-by-step test-point method.

### Phase 7 (Weeks 7–8): Math — Geometry and 3D Composite Figures

Your Geometry sits at 63.64 percent on the diagnostic, and the practice set shows continued difficulty with composed 3D shapes (cuboid + pyramid volume, Q62). Sequence this after Measurement (Phase 5) since composite volume problems require solid unit-conversion fluency first. Practice decomposing composite solids into known formulas (rectangular prism + pyramid, cylinder + cone) and always writing the two separate volume formulas before combining.

### Phase 8 (Week 8): Math — Algebra Cleanup

Algebra is your strongest domain (100 percent on the April diagnostic), but the later practice pages show new errors on exponent rules (Q78) and polynomial factoring (Q65, Q95, Q114). A single focused session on negative/fractional exponent rules and difference-of-squares factoring should close this gap quickly given your existing algebra fluency.

### Phase 9 (Weeks 9–10): Full-Length Timed Practice Tests

Take two full-length timed SHSAT practice exams under real test conditions, with a detailed error-log review after each (categorize every miss by domain/skill, not just right/wrong). This phase measures whether Phases 1–8 have transferred to your test-day performance and identifies any remaining leaks before the actual exam.

### Phase 10 (Ongoing): Weekly Mixed Review

From Week 3 onward, run one cumulative mixed-review set per week (10 ELA + 10 Math spanning all domains covered so far) to prevent skill decay in areas you have already "fixed," since the SHSAT rewards consistency across the full test rather than mastery of isolated topics.
