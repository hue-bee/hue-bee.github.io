Diagnostic Autopsy

Interrogate the Miss—Not Yourself

Tutor asks
“You solved 13 of 22 hard Math questions, but only 3 of 7 easy ones. On an easy miss, what did you know—and what did you fail to do?”
Use facts, not labels
Not: “I’m careless.”
Instead: “I solved for adult tickets but the question asked for student tickets.”
Concept miss
“I did not know that subtracting a negative becomes addition.” Remedy: reteach the rule, then clone it.
Execution miss
“I knew the rule, skipped the sign line, and entered −10.” Remedy: change the procedure.
Clever reframe
A miss is not a verdict. It is a bug report: reproduce it, name the cause, patch the process, then test the patch.
Diagnostic Autopsy

Sort the Four Easy Misses Live

AConcept?
Could you explain the governing rule before seeing the solution?
BExecution?
Did your written work show the correct rule but lose a sign, unit, step, or target?
The counterfactual test
If the same problem returned tomorrow with different numbers, would knowing the answer explanation be enough—or would your procedure need to change?
Diagnosis sentence: “I ___ because I ___; next time I will ___ before advancing.”
CAT Operating Rules

The Test Re-Estimates After Every Response

What “adaptive” means
Everyone starts around average difficulty. Correct responses raise the score estimate and may lead to harder items; incorrect responses lower it and may lead to easier items. The estimate uses the response history so far.
So what matters?
If an item feels hard, should you panic—or recognize that difficulty may reflect strong earlier performance?
NYCPS CAT model: 50 questions per subject; same grade-level standards and content areas; difficulty adapts during the test.

The Four-Point Commit Ritual

Our 3:00 coaching rule
At 2:30 with no path: estimate or eliminate. By 3:00–3:15: make the best supported answer and move. This is a pacing protocol, not an NYCPS interface rule.
Why it works

System 2 · Scrap Paper

G-R-R-S-C: Five Lines, One Path

G
Goal
Box the requested quantity.
R
Relevant facts
Copy facts with units.
R
Representation
Equation, table, bar, diagram?
S
Solve
One line per step.
C
Check
Target, unit, sign, field.
Clever shortcut
Do not begin by choosing an operation. Begin by naming the goal; then choose the picture that makes the relationships obvious.
Common failure
Starting arithmetic before deciding what the numbers mean. Fast calculation on the wrong target is still wrong.
Teach it back

Guided Practice · Student Narrates

Model 1: See the Equal Parts

Practice ALevel 2
Maya played 18 matches. She had 3 draws and won twice as many matches as she lost. How many matches did she lose?
Tutor asks only
What is the goal? Which matches should be removed first? After removing draws, how many equal “loss-sized” parts remain?
Clever solution
Remove 3 draws: 15 decided matches. “Wins twice losses” makes 3 equal parts. Each part is 15 ÷ 3 = 5, so losses = 5.
Guided Practice · Student Narrates

Model 2: Start with an Easier World

Practice BLevel 3
A parking lot has 22 vehicles, only cars and motorcycles. Altogether they have 68 wheels. How many motorcycles are there?
Tutor asks only
What if all 22 were motorcycles? How many wheels would that be? Each time one becomes a car, how many wheels are added?
Clever solution
Use motorcycles as the 2-wheel baseline. The 24 extra wheels create 12 cars. Therefore motorcycles = 22 − 12 = 10.
Guided Practice · You Drive

One Problem, Two Possible Pictures

Practice CLevel 4
At a theater, adult and student tickets total 146. There are 18 more adult tickets than student tickets. How many student tickets were sold?
Guided questions
If you remove the 18-ticket “extra,” what remains? Who owns half of that remainder? Why is 82 a tempting answer—and why is it wrong?
(146 − 18) ÷ 2 = 64 students

Fast Review · Math

The Cleverest Path Is the Safest Short Path

1 · CLevel 1
−3 − (−7) = −3 + 7 = 4
Clever image: subtracting a debt of 7 improves your position by 7.
Trap: treating both minus signs as one negative operation.
2 · 42Level 2
A = 1/2 · 12 · 7 = 6 · 7 = 42
Clever move: halve the even factor first; avoid 84 ÷ 2.
Field check: enter 42, not “42 cm².”
3 · ALevel 4
(146 − 18) ÷ 2 = 64
Clever move: remove the adult “extra,” then split the equal remainder.
82 is adults; the question asks students.
Fast Review · ELA

Prefer the Choice That Makes the Link Explicit

4 · BLevel 2
Because reveals cause → response in one clean sentence. A is choppy; C is grammatically tangled.
5 · BLevel 3
Overflow means the first containers lacked capacity. Routing extra water elsewhere increased total storage.
6 · CLevel 4
C directly names both the mechanism—hoses carried extra water—and the result—more containers held it.
Evidence frame
“Because the text says the hoses carried extra water to a second row of containers, the best inference is that the hoses increased storage.”
Clever ELA rule
For evidence questions, do not choose the most important sentence. Choose the sentence that proves the exact claim.

✓ Before You Advance
1 · Target
What exactly was asked?
Say the requested quantity in words. Make sure your answer matches it.
2 · Unit
What measure belongs?
Length, area, volume, dollars, minutes, people—or no unit in the entry field?
3 · Sign + sense
Could this answer be true?
Check positive/negative and compare the magnitude with a quick estimate.
4 · Field
Is the response complete?
Correct option, numeric entry, or required number of selections—then commit.
