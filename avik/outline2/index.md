## Executive assessment

The July paper-format assessment also showed a fairly balanced profile—37/57 in ELA (65%) and 35/57 in Math (61%)—but its estimated 460–490 range should not be treated as a direct forecast for the new computer-adaptive exam because the test length, delivery, navigation, and scoring model have changed.

The highest-return objective is to raise Math from 32/50 into a consistently stronger range by repairing **number sense and operations, multistep word-problem translation, measurement/geometry, ratios/proportions, and no-calculator execution**. ELA has improved, but **inference, supporting evidence, craft and structure, and passage-level organization** still need disciplined maintenance. The goal is not merely to increase percent correct; it is to make Avik accurate enough on routine and medium questions to reach and remain in a more difficult adaptive pathway.

The November 18, 2026 school-day SHSAT is 64 days after September 15. Registration opens October 6 and closes October 30, so registration should be confirmed during the plan rather than left until the final weeks.[^1][^2]

## CAT implications
The Fall 2026 exam contains 50 ELA and 50 Math questions in 180 minutes, and students may choose which section to take first. Both sections can include multiple-choice, fill-in-the-blank, and technology-enhanced items, so practice must include response entry—not just traditional four-choice worksheets.[^3]

Navigation changes the optimal strategy. Avik must answer before advancing; Math and stand-alone ELA items cannot be revisited, while questions inside an ELA passage set may be reviewed only until that set is submitted. Consequently, the old paper-test advice to “skip and return later” is not usable for Math, and a careless click becomes final.[^4][^3]

NYCPS provides highlighter, answer eliminator, notepad, pencil, line-reader, bookmark within passage sets, glossed-word support, and an equation editor for relevant Math responses. Practice should deliberately assign each tool a purpose; excessive digital annotation can waste time just as easily as it can aid comprehension.[^4]

Calculators are prohibited, but DOE-provided scrap paper is available. Every Math lesson should therefore include written no-calculator computation, and every digital simulation should require Avik to transfer the final result carefully from paper into the response field.[^5]
## Math improvement plan
### Number sense and operations
This is the first content block because it supports ratios, geometry, algebra, statistics, and multistep word problems. Avik must be able to execute accurately without a calculator and recognize equivalent forms quickly.

Required accomplishments:

- Perform integer, decimal, fraction, and mixed-number operations accurately, including negative signs and order of operations.
- Convert fluently among fractions, decimals, and percents; estimate first so an unreasonable result is visible.
- Use factors, multiples, prime factorization, GCF, LCM, exponents, square roots, and divisibility rules.
- Compare and order rational numbers and interpret absolute value and distance on a number line.
- Keep units attached to quantities and distinguish additive changes from multiplicative changes.
- Complete routine computation at **90% or better on two consecutive timed sets** before the skill is treated as secure.

Instructional routine:

1. Begin each study day with an 8–10 minute no-calculator fluency set.
2. Require a written estimate before exact computation on decimals, fractions, and percents.
3. Use an error code after each miss: concept gap, setup/translation, arithmetic, sign, unit, reading, or response-entry error.
4. Re-solve every missed problem without seeing the explanation, then complete two “same skeleton, new numbers” variants.
### Word-problem translation
The 2/7 result makes word problems the single clearest subcategory for score growth. The aim is not a collection of tricks; it is a repeatable translation process that works when the context is unfamiliar.

Required accomplishments:

- Identify the requested quantity and its unit before calculating.
- Define variables with meaning, not merely as isolated letters.
- Translate common relationships: total, difference, rate, unit rate, percent change, ratio, part-whole, consecutive integers, and combined quantities.
- Distinguish what is given from what must be derived.
- Represent a problem using an equation, proportion, table, labeled diagram, or bar model.
- Solve in stages and check the answer against the original situation.
- Reach **80% or better on mixed multistep word-problem sets**, then 85% under CAT-style one-way navigation.

Use the “G-R-R-S-C” protocol on scrap paper:

- **G — Goal:** circle or write what the question asks.
- **R — Relevant facts:** list only needed quantities with units.
- **R — Representation:** equation, ratio table, diagram, or model.
- **S — Solve:** show one line per logical step.
- **C — Check:** answer the actual question, verify units, sign, and reasonableness.
### Ratios and proportions
Avik's 4/6 result is workable but not yet reliable enough because ratio reasoning appears inside geometry, rates, percent, scale drawings, mixtures, and word problems.

Required accomplishments:

- Simplify ratios and distinguish part-to-part from part-to-whole comparisons.
- Find unit rates and use dimensional reasoning.
- Solve proportions with tables, equations, or scaling rather than indiscriminate cross multiplication.
- Handle percent of, percent increase/decrease, tax, discount, markup, and repeated percentage change.
- Solve scale drawing, similar-figure, recipe, speed, and combined-rate situations.
- Recognize when a relationship is not proportional.

Mastery gate: at least 17/20 across two mixed sets, with no repeated unit or setup errors.
### Geometry and measurement
Geometry/measurement was 5/10, and measurement alone was 2/5. This block needs concept repair plus diagram discipline.

Required accomplishments:

- Memorize and use perimeter, circumference, area, surface-area, and volume formulas; the test does not promise to supply definitions or formulas.[^4]
- Convert within measurement systems and square or cube conversion factors when converting area or volume.
- Solve composite-area and missing-dimension problems.
- Use angle relationships: vertical, complementary, supplementary, triangle sum, exterior angles, and parallel-line relationships.
- Work with scale, coordinate geometry, distance, transformations, and similarity as represented in official samples.
- Draw or relabel every figure on scrap paper, mark equal lengths/angles, and record units.
- Distinguish perimeter from area and surface area from volume before selecting a formula.

Mastery gate: 85% on a 20-question mixed geometry/measurement set, including at least four multistep and four technology-enhanced/fill-in responses.
### Algebra maintenance
Algebra is currently a relative strength at 9/11, so it should not consume the early plan. It should be used as the language for solving weak-area word problems.

Required accomplishments:

- Simplify expressions using distribution and combining like terms.
- Solve one- and multistep equations and inequalities, including variables on both sides.
- Evaluate expressions by substitution and preserve order of operations.
- Translate verbal relationships into equations.
- Check solutions in the original equation and interpret them in context.

One short mixed Algebra set per week is sufficient unless performance falls below 80%.
### Timing and final-answer control
Avik spent more than five minutes on three identified questions, including two Math items. On the new CAT, he cannot leave a hard Math item and return, but he also cannot allow one item to consume seven minutes repeatedly.

Use this decision rule:

- By 30–40 seconds, identify the topic and representation.
- By 90 seconds, the setup should exist on paper.
- At about 2:30, if no productive path exists, eliminate choices, estimate, or test plausible values.
- By 3:00–3:15, make the best supported response and advance.
- Reserve longer than three minutes only when the setup is correct and one or two concrete steps remain.

Because the last diagnostic ended with 14:37 unused, the first adjustment is not “work faster.” It is **spend 10–20 extra seconds verifying each final Math response**: requested quantity, unit, sign, answer field, and reasonableness.
## ELA improvement plan
### Inference
Inference produced four misses on the latest form and was only 50% in July. Avik must learn to choose the answer that is most strongly compelled by the text, not merely an answer that seems plausible.

Required accomplishments:

- Separate explicit evidence from a reasonable inference.
- Identify the exact sentence or paired clues supporting the inference.
- Complete the frame: “Because the text says ___ and ___, the best conclusion is ___.”
- Reject choices that are too broad, too strong, based on outside knowledge, only partly supported, or true but irrelevant.
- Handle character motivation, author implication, tone, cause/effect, and prediction questions.
- Reach 85% on mixed inference sets with an evidence citation for every answer.
### Supporting ideas and evidence
The 7/10 result suggests that Avik sometimes recognizes the passage broadly but selects evidence that is less precise than another option.

Required accomplishments:

- State the claim or idea that the evidence must support before evaluating choices.
- Rank evidence by directness, specificity, and scope.
- Distinguish a relevant fact from the **best** supporting fact.
- Match pronouns and references correctly across nearby sentences.
- Eliminate choices that support a related idea rather than the exact one asked.

Practice should pair each evidence question with a one-sentence justification: “This choice proves ___ because ___.”
### Main idea and structure
The latest 3/4 result is better than July's 58%, but the sample is small. Main-idea work should be embedded in every passage rather than isolated as a once-weekly topic.

Required accomplishments:

- Write a 5–8 word paragraph label after each paragraph.
- State the passage's topic, controlling idea, and purpose separately.
- Distinguish main idea from a supporting detail, lesson, theme, or overly broad topic.
- Track structural moves such as problem/solution, claim/evidence, chronology, cause/effect, comparison, and question/investigation/conclusion.
- For fiction, track goal, conflict, change, and theme; for poetry, track speaker, shift, imagery, and central insight.
### Craft, grammar, and organization
Craft and Structure is a relative strength, while punctuation, transitions, and supporting-idea organization each showed one miss in a two-question sample. These require focused maintenance rather than a large standalone unit.

Required accomplishments:

- Infer word meaning and connotation from local context.
- Explain why an author includes a sentence, example, comparison, or structural shift.
- Identify tone from patterns of diction rather than one dramatic word.
- Review comma boundaries, semicolons/colons, apostrophes, verb tense, agreement, modifiers, parallel structure, and pronoun clarity.
- Name the logical relationship before choosing a transition: addition, contrast, cause/effect, example, continuation, or conclusion.
- In passage revision, test whether a sentence advances the paragraph's purpose and whether it belongs in that location.
### Digital passage-set method
For each ELA passage set:

1. Read the title and genre; predict what to track.
2. Read fully and create brief paragraph labels; highlight only thesis/claim, major shifts, and decisive evidence.
3. Answer from a prediction before comparing choices.
4. Eliminate choices using specific labels: unsupported, distorted, too broad, too narrow, wrong paragraph, or outside knowledge.
5. Bookmark only questions that have a genuine unresolved choice.
6. Before submitting the set, revisit bookmarks and confirm that every answer has direct textual support.
7. Submit only after this internal review because the set cannot be reopened.[^4]

The latest ELA pace was fast, and three questions were answered in 9–13 seconds. Some rapid answers may reflect confidence, but the pattern warrants a minimum check: reread the question stem, identify the relevant text location, and compare the final two choices before submission.

## Weekly workload
A sustainable workload is approximately **6–7 hours per week**, including tutoring, until the final taper.

| Component | Frequency | Time | Purpose |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Tutor lesson | 1 per week | 60 minutes | Teach, diagnose reasoning, and set the next mastery gate |
| No-calculator Math fluency | 5 per week | 10–15 minutes | Eliminate routine and easy-item losses |
| Focused Math set | 4 per week | 25–35 minutes | Repair the week's primary content |
| ELA passage set | 3 per week | 20–30 minutes | Maintain reading and improve evidence discipline |
| Digital mixed block | 1 per week initially; 2 later | 45–60 minutes | Build one-way navigation and interface fluency |
| Error-log review | 3 per week | 10 minutes | Convert mistakes into reusable rules |
| Full simulation | Weeks 7 and 9, optional Week 5 checkpoint | 180 minutes plus review | Measure endurance, section order, pacing, and transfer |

No full practice test is complete until its review is complete. A three-hour test should be followed over the next one or two days by enough review to re-solve every miss, identify the cause, and prove correction on a parallel item.
## Lesson architecture
Each 60-minute tutoring session should follow a stable structure:

| Minutes | Activity |
|---:|---|
| 0–5 | Retrieval warm-up: formulas, fraction/percent facts, or grammar rule |
| 5–15 | Review the error log and one repeat pattern from homework |
| 15–30 | Explicit instruction and modeling of the week's priority skill |
| 30–45 | Guided practice with verbal explanation of setup and evidence |
| 45–55 | Timed CAT-style mini-set with one-way navigation |
| 55–60 | Exit ticket, mastery result, and precise homework assignment |

The tutor should not spend the hour merely watching Avik complete many questions. The highest-value work is exposing why he chooses a representation, where his setup diverges, and whether an error is conceptual, linguistic, computational, or procedural.
## Error-log system
Use one row per missed or uncertain item:

| Field | Required entry |
|---|---|
| Source/item | Test and question number |
| Domain | ELA inference, Math ratio, geometry measurement, etc. |
| Difficulty | Easy, medium, hard if available |
| Error type | Concept, translation, arithmetic, evidence, trap answer, time, unit, or entry |
| What happened | One factual sentence, not “careless mistake” |
| Correct rule | Generalizable lesson in the student's own words |
| Corrected work | Full re-solution or textual evidence |
| Clone result | Results on two parallel problems |
| Recheck date | 3–5 days later |

A topic is not mastered when Avik understands the explanation. It is mastered when he can solve a new version accurately, under time pressure, without prompting.
## Practice-test protocol
NYCPS offers a Student Readiness Tool and four browser-based online practice tests designed to mirror the exam's content and functionality. At least two of Avik's remaining full simulations should use the official interface, and shorter work should imitate its no-return rules even when another platform permits backtracking.[^3]

For every full simulation:

- Use one device, a mouse/trackpad setup similar to school, scrap paper, no calculator, no phone, and exactly 180 minutes.
- Predetermine section order and record the time at the section switch.
- Do not pause for instruction, snacks, or answer discussion.
- Respect passage-set submission and one-way Math navigation.
- Record item-level correctness, topic, difficulty when available, time outliers, and response-entry errors.
- Review within 24 hours while the reasoning is still memorable.
## Section order
Begin full simulations with **Math first** for the next two tests because Math is the larger growth area and benefits from peak concentration. Keep that order only if Math accuracy improves without creating a late ELA decline. If ELA drops noticeably from fatigue, test ELA-first once and choose the order that produces the stronger combined result; NYCPS allows either section first and flexible time division.[^5]

A reasonable initial budget is 100 minutes for Math and 80 minutes for ELA. Avik's latest actual split was approximately 105/60 with nearly 15 minutes unused, so the plan should gradually redirect about 10 minutes toward more deliberate ELA evidence checks while keeping a small safety buffer.
## Score targets
Because CAT scoring uses response pattern and item difficulty, raw-correct targets are practice benchmarks rather than promised official scaled scores. Use the following progression:[^3]

| Checkpoint | Math target | ELA target | Process target |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| End of Week 2 | 75% on focused number sets | 80% on targeted passages | Every miss classified |
| End of Week 4 | 80% on mixed Math | 80% on inference/evidence | No more than one avoidable error per mini-set |
| End of Week 6 | 36–38/50 | 39–40/50 | No unverified final entry; stable one-way pacing |
| Final two simulations | 38–42/50 | 40–43/50 | No cluster of easy misses; consistent timing and tool use |

The most important leading indicators are fewer easy Math errors, fewer setup/unit mistakes, and stronger evidence justification in ELA. Raw-score gains without those process improvements are less likely to hold under an adaptive exam.
## Test-week checklist
- Confirm SHSAT registration before October 30.[^1]
- Confirm the exact test date, location, reporting time, and any approved accommodations.
- Maintain the normal sleep and wake schedule for at least five days before the test.
- Stop full-length testing after the final weekend simulation.
- Review formulas, error-log rules, and interface habits—not entire books.
- Use the DOE-provided computer and scrap paper; do not bring or rely on a calculator or smartwatch.[^5]
- On Math, solve on paper, verify the requested quantity and response field, then advance.
- On ELA passage sets, revisit bookmarks before submitting because the set cannot be reopened.[^4]
## Parent and tutor monitoring
A short weekly update should report only four items: content mastered, recurring error pattern, latest timed result, and next week's focus. Avoid overreacting to a single score; use two consecutive sets or tests to identify a genuine trend.

The plan should be revised immediately if any of the following occurs:

- Easy Math remains below 80% after two weeks.
- Word problems remain below 75% after explicit translation instruction.
- Geometry stays below 70% after formula and diagram remediation.
- ELA inference/evidence remains below 75% across three passage sets.
- More than two questions per section are lost to response-entry or navigation mistakes.
- Full-test stamina, anxiety, or accommodations change the observed performance profile.

The final instructional emphasis is clear: **make Math fundamentals automatic, make word-problem and geometry setups visible on paper, and make every ELA answer traceable to evidence**. Those changes directly address the diagnostic profile while fitting the non-return, item-adaptive structure of the November 2026 SHSAT.
