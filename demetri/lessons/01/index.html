2026 Digital Rules

Every “Next” Button Is a Commit Gate

SHSAT Math
After advancing from a question, you cannot return. The adaptive system selects later difficulty from earlier responses.
SHSAT ELA
Within a passage set, revisit its questions; once the set is submitted, that set is closed.
TACHS
Check unanswered items inside the current subtest; after submitting the subtest, you cannot reopen it.
Official navigation rules: NYC Public Schools SHSAT guidance; TACHS 2026 Student Handbook, p. 29.
Universal Strategy

Use the Four-Gate Commit Routine

1
Read the ask
Restate the question in five words. What exactly must the answer represent?
2
Choose a route
Name the method before calculating: equation, diagram, elimination, or evidence hunt.
3
Verify locally
Check sign, unit, estimate, grammar, or proof line—the risk specific to this item.
4
Commit cleanly
Select only after the answer and the question are speaking about the same thing.
Say your thinking aloud
Which gate do you usually skip? How would I know from watching your work—rushing into arithmetic, changing answers without evidence, or reaching the right value but answering the wrong quantity?
Clever memory cue: R-C-V-C
Read → Choose → Verify → Commit. Think of it as an airport gate: once the door closes, the suitcase you forgot stays behind.
Math Lens

Find the Question Under the Question

Before touching a number
Is this problem asking for a part, a whole, a rate, a change, or a constraint? Which words reveal that role?
N–N–M–C
Name the target → mark the Numbers → choose the Move → run a Check
Why this works
Most difficult problems hide ordinary math behind an unfamiliar story. Naming the target strips off the costume before the arithmetic begins.
Math Diagnostic A

Core Number Sense + Algebra Easy to Hard

1Level 1
A jacket marked $240 is discounted by 35%. What is the sale price? Which quantity is the whole, and what should your answer be smaller than?
2Level 2
The ratio of blue beads to gold beads is 3:5. There are 64 beads total. How many are blue? Does 3 mean three beads or three equal shares?
3Level 3
A studio charges an $18 registration fee plus $4.50 per class. Maya can spend at most $54. What is the greatest whole number of classes she can take? Where does “at most” enter the math?
4Level 4
The product of two consecutive positive integers is 72. Their sum is the width of a rectangle whose length is 3 greater than its width. Find the rectangle’s perimeter. Which result is only an intermediate value?
Tutor listens for
Do you translate before calculating, keep units attached, estimate before trusting, and stop only when you have answered the exact question?
Math Visual Model

A Proportion Is Two Rails on One Train

Think about it
If 4 notebooks cost $10, what change carries 4 to 10? Could the same relationship carry 14 dollars back to a number of notebooks? Which rail tells you the unit?
Clever explanation
The multiplier is the train engine. It must pull both rails through matching stops; if one rail changes by a different factor, the cars uncouple.
same pair · same direction · same multiplier
Math Diagnostic B

Rates, Percents + Geometry Easy to Hard

5Level 1
A cyclist travels 27 miles in 1.5 hours at a constant rate. What is the unit rate in miles per hour? Which unit must disappear?
6Level 2
A quantity increases from 80 to 92. What is the percent increase? Should the change be compared with 80 or 92—and why?
7Level 3
On a scale drawing, 1 cm represents 2.5 m. A room measures 6.4 cm by 3.2 cm on the drawing. Find its actual area. Do lengths and areas scale in the same way?
8Level 4
A square and an equilateral triangle have equal perimeters. The square’s side is 3 cm shorter than the triangle’s side. Find the side length of each figure. What must be equal even though the side counts differ?
Accuracy trap: scaling area
A scale factor changes every length. Area is built from two lengths, so convert both dimensions before multiplying rather than attaching square units too early.
TACHS Ability

Track the Rule, Not the Decoration

Feature audit
From the first figure to the second, what changed in direction, position, count, shading, and size? Which features stayed invariant?
Clever explanation
Treat the picture like a witness lineup. Ask each feature separately where it was and what happened. Do not let one dramatic feature distract you from a quieter second rule.
rotate? reflect? add? subtract? alternate?

ELA Reading

Build a Proof Map, Not a Memory

Before answering
What job does each paragraph perform—introduce a puzzle, explain a cause, give evidence, complicate the idea, or conclude? Which line would you return to for proof?
Clever explanation: passage GPS
You do not memorize every building on a trip. You remember the route and landmarks. Paragraph jobs are landmarks; key words are street signs; the question sends you back to the correct block.
question → paragraph job → proof line → answer
Original Reading Passage

When a Roof Becomes Part of the Weather

1On a summer afternoon, two neighboring buildings can feel as if they stand in different climates. One roof, sealed beneath dark asphalt, grows hot enough to shimmer. The other supports low mats of sedum, grasses, and shallow soil. The planted roof is not merely prettier. It changes what happens to sunlight and rain after they strike the building.

2Dark roofing absorbs much of the sun’s energy and later releases that heat into the surrounding air. Plants intercept part of the sunlight, while moisture in leaves and soil carries away energy as water evaporates. As a result, the roof’s surface may remain cooler, and rooms below may require less air-conditioning.

3A planted roof also delays storm water. Instead of rushing immediately into street drains, rain collects in the growing layer and leaves gradually through evaporation or drainage. This delay matters in cities where a sudden downpour can overwhelm pipes. Yet green roofs are not effortless solutions: buildings must support the added weight, and plants need designs suited to wind, drought, and shallow soil.

4For that reason, researchers rarely describe one roof as a cure for urban heat or flooding. Its value appears when many carefully designed roofs work together. A single planted surface is modest; a network of them can function like scattered pieces of a larger climate system.

Thirty-second map
Write a two- or three-word job for each paragraph. Where does the author shift from benefits to limits? Which final image unifies the passage?
Reading Diagnostic

Evidence Questions Easy to Hard

13Level 1
According to paragraph 3, how does a planted roof affect storm water? Point to the exact verb that proves your choice.
14Level 2
As used in paragraph 2, “intercept” most nearly means— Which nearby sentence shows what happens next?
15Level 3
Why does the author include the limitations in paragraph 3? Would the central idea become less trustworthy without them?
16Level 4
Which statement best expresses the passage’s central idea? Defend it using evidence from two different paragraphs. How does paragraph 4 refine—not repeat—the benefits?
Written Expression

Give Every Sentence a Job Interview

Ask the sentence
What job do you perform here—state the focus, provide evidence, explain it, connect ideas, or conclude? If the sentence disappeared, what logical work would be missing?
Clever explanation
A sentence may be grammatical and still unemployed. On revision questions, do not ask only “Is it correct?” Ask “Is it useful in this exact position?”
correct + clear + connected + correctly placed
Written Expression Diagnostic

Revision Choices Easy to Hard

17Level 1
Choose the correct verb: “Neither the coach nor the players ___ willing to leave the court.” Which subject sits closest to the verb?
A was
B were
C is
D be
18Level 2
“The forecast predicted rain. ___, the team moved the concert indoors.” Which transition expresses cause and result?
A However
B Therefore
C Meanwhile
D Similarly
19Level 3
A paragraph explains how school gardens improve science learning. One sentence describes the cafeteria’s new paint color. Keep, revise, move, or delete? What exact job could it perform?
20Level 4
Revise for clarity and economy: “Due to the fact that the bridge was old in age, inspectors made the decision to close it temporarily for a period of two weeks.” Which phrases repeat meaning?
TACHS + SHSAT transfer
Grammar checks correctness; revision checks purpose. The strongest choice usually preserves the writer’s meaning while making the sentence clearer, leaner, and better connected.
Pressure Strategy

Match the Check to the Most Likely Failure

Math check

Estimate the size, attach the unit, and substitute when possible. Ask: Could this answer exist in the story?

Reading check

Name the proof location. Ask: Am I choosing what the passage says, or what I already believe?

Ability check

List all changing features. Ask: Did I notice a second rule hiding behind the first?

Writing check

Read the sentence in context. Ask: Is it merely correct, or is it correct for this job and location?
